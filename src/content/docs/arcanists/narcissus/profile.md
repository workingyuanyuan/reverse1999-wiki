## Cover

An arcanist's work, first exhibited in the 1990s and then for a century after. Completed in spring, on March 21. First exhibited on an island in the Aegean region, now touring the world.

Dimensions: 55 7/64 x 31 1/2 x 12 3/32 in.

## Item

### Daffodil Wraps

A knitted shawl adorned with three-dimensional daffodils, soft against the skin but offering no warmth. Its irregular hem and scattered yellow-and-white blossoms keep its pool-green hue from appearing dull.

She knows this shade of green as well as she knows the daffodils that grow in the valley. She once wandered beside the stream, peering as far as she could in the direction of its flow and imagining the day she might venture beyond Ephaistonos—like a droplet joining the sea.

### Accessory Collection

A collection of accessories in varied styles that come together suprisingly well, including three bracelets and several hair ornaments.

The interviewee purchased these accessories from various souvenir shops across Ephaistonos. Glass-bead bracelets and star-shaped hair clips form her idea of "fashion" beyond the island. Few of her fellow nymphs share her taste, but that never troubles her—she has found friends willing to accept her for who she is. Though gifts from those friends never fail to expand her collection, she still delights in choosing pieces for herself.

### Replica of the "Blessing"

A handmade accessory with a shell shaped like a scale as its pendant, its iridescent sheen created with some kind of industrial coating.

The interviewee never shies away from admitting that the piece is an imitation. In fact, she readily praises its craftsmanship: "It shines just like my 'blessing' did; they got the shape and size exactly right ... Even if they won't tell me who made it, I saw the cut on Hermes's hand, the paint Apollo hid, and the receipt for the silver chain Themis bought! So it stays right here, to commemorate our great adventure together."

## Story

### What Isn't in the Epic

It is always the great beings who receive the most attention. As early as the 8th century BCE, people had compiled detailed genealogies for the gods. The story of Odysseus evolved as it passed from mouth to mouth and was later gathered and recorded by the famed blind poet. Today, anyone can easily turn the pages and revisit the hero's celebrated voyage.

But beyond them are countless spirits and nymphs. They are the proud daughters of Oceanus, taking no part in human wars and becoming entangled in none of the gods' disputes. They simply sing their own songs and glide gracefully through the gaps between stories.

Gaia gave birth to lofty mountains and marshlands, which would one day become the nymphs' homes. They gather myrtle, carnations, mint, and thyme for their wreaths, string dewdrops into garments, and roam the mountains and valleys all day on nimble feet.

The nymphs' arcane skills extend no further than little tricks such as turning into plants. They tend to newborn gods and heroes and accept only a lock of shorn hair in return, yet have no desire for their power.

Their traces occasionally appear between lines of dactylic hexameter, usually because they have suffered some harmless prank by a god—or played one on a passing hero in return ...

Narcissus lived this carefree life for a very long time, until she entered that year's Aquatic Games and achieved the excellent result of last place.

Last place?!

The proud nymph fled the venue screaming and was forced to take a hard look at her perfect life.

### The Champion's Interview

Pandora Wilson: Welcome, welcome, and congratulations to Ephaistonos's newest champion.

Narcissus: Have you come to spread the tale of my magnificent victory? Then we must begin with a bedtime story. No, even earlier than that—it was one distant evening—

Pandora Wilson: It seems you have a great many stories to tell.

Narcissus: Of course! There are so many details I could share. Your readers must want to know how closely we worked together and how, at the final critical moment, I—the great Narcissus—claimed the Eye of Typhon!

Narcissus: I've already prepared a five-minute speech. One moment, let me put on my laurel wreath ... All right, shall we begin?

Pandora Wilson: More than your achievements, we are interested in you. I hear your path into the sporting world was not an easy one. What kind of training helped you overcome those difficulties?

Narcissus: Training ... That's right, I certainly did some training! Mm-hmm! All the spring nymphs were all very supportive!

Pandora Wilson: And what are your plans for the future? Will you specialize in a particular event?

Narcissus: Um, a specialty ... Well ...

Pandora Wilson: Are you confident you can defeat your old rival, Hedone, at the next Aquatic Games?

Narcissus: The next Games ...

Pandora Wilson: You sound as though you're about to cry.

Narcissus: J-Just wait and see! I'll deliver an even more dazzling performance next time!
