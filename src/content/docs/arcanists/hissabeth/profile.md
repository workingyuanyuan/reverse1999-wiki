First displayed in Lusignan, Vienne, Fifth Republic of France, later transferred to the United States and archived at the LSCC.

A practical garment balancing functionality and aesthetics, capable of providing a comfortable temperature for ectothermic snake species.

### Serpentine Scales

Clear-textured, smooth-edged, small handmade decorations crafted in her spare time. She can identify every detail of the serpentine material used, recognizing which experiment each strata of the design came from. Although the stitching is somewhat rough, it remains a precious and unique record of their work.

### "Family Member"

One of her eight siblings. Occasionally quarrels with the interviewee, often holds different opinions, but always faces the world together.

As the world before them transforms anew, their eyes watch on with curiosity.

## Story

### Binary Dust

Look closely—this is the 20th century!

It burst through the door before anyone could react, rushing in at full speed. Humanity barely had time to take to the skies before it was already reaching for the surface of the moon.

Silicon chips began to gleam across new fields, gradually revealing an extraordinary vision. Screens and data streams built a new world shaped by new desires, like an ouroboros shedding its skin.

The LSCC maintained its cutting-edge spirit. Employees turned offices into gaming arcades; bookshelves were crammed with the Global Catalog, Tumbling Stone, and neatly printed cognitive enhancement research papers.

Laboratories in Palo Alto were lit all night, bright enough to dull the stars overhead. What did it matter? Compared to those great advances of the era, everything else was just dust in the wind.

In a small apartment, nine pairs of eyes fixated on the scrolling numbers. The machines, the theories, the roaring processors, and glowing monitors never commented on themselves, nor did they incite any religious awe. When parents spoke about personal computers, they praised them for making communication easier, weaving the world closer together.

Yes, this was the reality she and her siblings chose to pursue.

### Serpentine Primavera

> When she opens her eyes, even the joy of spring may be turned to stone.

Three sisters make their home in the wilderness. Known as the greatest of "sculptors." Their garden is filled with stone figures so lifelike that they could humble even Pheidias himself. Though rumors persist about petrification spells being the true secret to their art.

The youngest of the Gorgon sisters is deeply loved by the older two. During springtime, she patiently untangles her sisters' serpentine locks by the babbling brook. Managing living hair proves challenging when each strand fights back. All in preparation for their "Rite of Spring" dance, where those same snakes help by counting out the beat with their hiss, keeping each movement of their performance perfectly in time.

As Medusa's head went flying through the air, her last thoughts may well have drifted back to one of those spring days.

Hissabeth will never know. But she finds some peace in knowing her own head is still where it belongs.

#### "Local Fashions"

A golden fruit shimmering with an arcane glow. The interviewee readily admits it comes from Laplace's arcane materials warehouse.

"Oui, it's actually a Fruit of Good and Evil. I painted it together with my siblings. If it looks familiar to you—that's right, I modeled it after the images in the archives.

It's my new project: myth interpretation!"

#### "The Immortal Coil"

An ancient crown discovered by the interviewee amid some ruins on the Petrified Islands. Her siblings adorned it with the fresh leaves of spring.

Wearing an unidentified relic—especially one suspected of carrying the traces of arcane skill—might seem reckless. Yet when speaking about it, the interviewee and her siblings express disappointment: the crown feels as if it was calling out, adapting itself to its new owner when she put it on, but aside from its unusually perfect size, there seemed to be no other effects.

#### Primavera Robe

A soft, iridescent gown crafted by the skilled women of Lesbos and Chios.

The history of the Gorgon may be as long as these islands themselves. To explore the subtopic "Origins of Myth," the interviewee and her siblings spent a long time traveling around the region. She admits that convincing the local Daughters of Artemis to lay down their bows was the hardest part of the research.

"But it was worth it—I witnessed a rare ritual. Be sure to read my new paper!"

#### Mugwort Bouquet

A bouquet with a strong fragrance. Through the humid, sweltering height of summer, a bundle of mugwort like this can clear the air. During an Eastern festival, households hang mugwort and calamus above their doors to ward off evil and protect the home.

"My siblings and I are quite fond of this bundle, especially the scent ... Wait, some people believe it can drive off snakes?"

#### Tinkling Jades

Advance with a bow, withdraw with a rise—and only then shall the jade ring clear.

A pendant made up of several jade and gemstone pieces—jade huang, hanging pendants, a dragon-shaped charm, agate tubes, and turquoise beads. Legend holds that shamans once wore jade huang in rites to the gods—perhaps a detail that could serve the interviewee's research into myth.

"It's heavy ..." While talking about this piece, the interviewee was busy chewing on a honey-drizzled rice delicacy called zongzi. "And it jingles at the slightest movement!"

#### Misty Gauze

An exquisite gauze robe, light as mist, thin as cloud. When the interviewee wears it, shifting light plays across the fabric with every movement, as though she were draped in a pool of clear water.

"It's cool and slippery to the touch. I bet someone would be interested in this kind of weaving technique. *It feels just like a snake! Yes, a snake!"*
