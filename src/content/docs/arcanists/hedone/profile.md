## Item

### "Spur of the Moment"

A great bow some two meters long, gilded from end to end, resplendent as the sun. It was once enshrined in the temple of Hekatos, weathering long ages.

"The elders believed that only a god could draw this heavy bow. On a whim, I gave it a try—and loosed an arrow that struck the sacred flame a thousand meters away." The priest thus bestowed the golden bow upon one beloved of the gods.

### Golden Stephanos

A lightweight golden crown, adorned with a pearl chain strung on silver wire. Descended from a millennium-old design, it has been simplified in form. The interviewee states this was done so she could more easily take part in various athletic competitions.

On Ephaistonos, this crown symbolizes her power and majesty, and its wearer bears the responsibility of protecting her people and their legacy. Here, however, it is merely an elegant accent in the interviewee's hair.

### "Beyond Fate"

A set of pure gold arm guards, comprising an armguard, an arm ring, a wrist guard, and a ring. It may be regarded as a gift from a bargain struck, or as a footnote to a hero's fate.

The volcano's power allows it to shift into any form.

"After leaving the isle, its power was restricted: it can no longer change shape as freely as before. Still, it remains happy to take the form of a golden arrow—my weapon of choice."

## Story

### Journey of a Drachma

Even as Alexander's empire collapsed into the wars of the Diadochi, trade still sailed across the seas.

Greek was the language of the Aegean, and the drachma its currency. A silver coin, minted in a city-state of the Kymaion people, was carried by ship to the port of Alexandria. There, it would be used to purchase grain, wine, and olive oil. Its second destination would be Antioch, where the silk trade flourished. Merchants who had turned a handsome profit carried the coin farther east, to the riverside city of Seleucia. People there loved to trade for glassware, spices, and ivory. In the end, it would be buried underground on the eyes of a dead man, or tossed into a clay jar to journey toward the distant East alongside other silver coins.

Hedone turned the coin between her fingers, knowing its fate by heart. She had lived beneath those sails since childhood, well versed in where wealth came from and where it flowed.

The sea meant golden opportunity, but also danger and challenge. The age of heroes and monsters was fading, but she continued her hunt, seeking any challenge that would give her the thrill of wagering life and death. On their sea hunts, they would pass by cities wreathed in smoke and ash, watching ravens circle over the harbors. No one yet realized: war had never truly left history, and a mighty wolf had already turned its gaze their way.

### Volca-Cola

Pandora Wilson: I've interviewed reincarnators before, but unlike them ... your rebirth seems to be a complete transformation into your past self.

Hedone: I have always been myself. Sovereign of Ephaistonos, the hero who slew Typhon—Hedone. It's only that before my awakening, I lacked the memories of being her.

Pandora Wilson: Before your awakening, did you ever consider living a different life?

Hedone: Never. Competition, challenge, and the pursuit of pleasure! I was already a champion long before I knew my true identity.

Hedone: Even if I my memories had not returned, I know that I would have defeated Typhon, brought a new sovereign to Ephaistonos, and thrown myself into the "Downpour."

Pandora Wilson: As a hidden sanctuary for arcanists, very little information about Ephaistonos reaches the outside world.

Pandora Wilson: Since you're now willing to reopen communication, we'd like to write an exclusive report on Ephaistonos.

Hedone: Communication is limited to a small amount of trade. I won't expose Ephaistonos entirely to the dangers of the outside world.

Hedone: What kind of content did you have in mind?

Pandora Wilson: I've heard you preserve many sacred traditions, living a classical, ancient Greek arcanist life—

Hedone: Haha, well we don't ride dolphins to work, as some have claimed, though we certainly could!

Hedone: Ephaistonos has always honored its cultural heritage without ever falling behind the times.

Pandora Wilson: Sounds like you have a better idea?

Hedone: I'd like to slip in an advertisement. Spicy, smooth, and a great bargain—Ephaistonos's own arcane beverage, Volca-Cola.

Hedone: You'll drive up our publicity, and I'll reward you with a sizeable sponsorship fee. Do we have a deal?
