## Cover

A special infected's work. Exhibited in the 1990s for 19 years. Completed in Winter, on February 16. Originally exhibited in Longyearbyen, Svalbard, Kingdom of Norway, the exhibit later went on an exhibition tour in Antarctica.

Dimensions: 67 23/32 x 27 9/16 x 25 19/32 inches.

Medium: Anything Unknown

Fragrance Note: Aquatic, Woody - Bergamot Orange, Seaweed, Grey Amber

Afflatus: Mother Spirit's Glow \[Spirit\], Myth Archaeology

## Item

### Deer Fur Trapper Hat

A deer fur trapper hat, which the interviewee says once belonged to her grandfather.

The original hat was oversized, so she modified it, carefully stitching up the worn spots. The two sharp points sticking out are clear proof of the alteration.

### The Bulging Backpack

A regular camping pack couldn't possibly hold this much—it appears to have been modified multiple times to fit all survival essentials.

There were multiple hidden compartments; the interviewee eagerly opened each to show us her storage tricks. Revealing things like a chocolate wrapper, a half-melted candle, and bundles of senne grass tucked in every nook and cranny.

### "Will of Steel"

Its sharp pick and solid axehead make it an important tool for climbing icy slopes.

"Never broken," the interviewee said, as if she were talking about more than just an ice axe.

## Story

### Southward to No Man's Land!

The early 20th century—the glorious age after the Second Industrial Revolution. Electricity, new technology, a complete system of knowledge—and a fire to "turn the world upside down."

In 1909, an American explorer cut through the sea ice at the North Pole, giving the hesitant a firm slap on the back: Get going, dreamers ... conquer the unknown! Dig through every inch of snow, and carve your name into the bones of the Jurassic!

From Ushuaia, from Sydney, from Wales, from Bergen—teams raced toward the last uncharted continent.

One after another, they braved the wind and snow, planted flags at the South Pole, charted every edge, and declared human sovereignty over the outermost frontiers.

And from the bottom of the earth, they shouted together, "We have solved the last mystery!"

### "Mother Spirit"

Pandora Wilson: Alright, let's begin with this "Mother Spirit." You said her legend is an inseparable part of your life and culture.

Nautika: Thank you for interviewing me, Ms. Wilson! I hope UTTU Magazine can share the stories of my culture so that others can experience the magic of myth.

Pandora Wilson: Of course—now back to the "Mother Spirit," the singular deity of your homeland's mythology. What is She like?

Nautika: She's the preserver of light, the weaver of the endless day, and Her messenger is a giant white moose.

Pandora Wilson: Does that mean she looks like a moose?

Nautika: Hmm ... not exactly. Áddjá—oh, I mean, my grandfather and I—would often wonder about Her when I was little.

Nautika: In his mind, She was a great tree with leaves that shone like sunlight—a maple tree, or something.

Pandora Wilson: No fixed form, a legend open to interpretation ...

Nautika: No, no, I wouldn't say that. The Mother Spirit might not have one agreed-upon image, but Her stories and teachings are fixed. Her guidance is real—every one of us feels it in our own way.

Nautika: Try imagining it yourself, Ms. Wilson. A warm spirit of the all-encompassing mother, a forever guide in your life ... what does She look like to you?

Pandora Wilson: Huh. That's a novel idea ... perhaps she'd float on the wind on spider silk ...

### From Darkness, Light

> Destiny may take much from us, but in doing so leaves us its gifts.

Death draws near.

A gift of myrrh—that red-brown incense, so fragrant, yet bitter and sharp, like a layered aria.

A gift of fragrant boughs, to build the precious nest of death.

A gift of dawn's light, so that the song of rebirth may be sung.

In the bronze censer, the flames burn hotter. It blazes, radiant as a heart being reforged. Nothing in the world could shine brighter. The poets, gods, and sages gathered around it cry out. Even they cannot bear a flame of life so pure.

At last, the fire dies. No one dares draw near. She spreads her snow-white wings from the half-spent ashes and flies toward the city where the sun was born.

That is the source of all fire, the first light in every eye, and hope rekindled in the long darkness. Even if this rebirth is priceless, even if the light destroys what draws too near, the goal is there. The future is there. She decides to press on once more, flying into the unknown without hesitation.

#### Phoenix Crown

A crown inspired by a bird's beating wings, marking the moment a divine bird is reborn.

It is said that the moment this crown is worn again, all birds will fly there and gift the wearer with their most beautiful feather.

#### Burning Feathers

The feathers closest to her chest, bearing the interviewee's deepest longing.

No matter how many times she burns to ash and rises again, so long as her faith holds, this vivid red will never go out.

#### "Refining"

A gold chain ornament, crafted using traditional stamping and embossing techniques, fitted snugly around the interviewee's waist.

Only the purest gold grows more radiant after passing through fire—just as a soul finds its brilliance only after passing through death.
