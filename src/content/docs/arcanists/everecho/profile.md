## Cover

An arcanist's work. Exhibited in the 1990s for an unknown period. Believed to have been completed sometime in spring, mid-April. Exhibited in Nowa Miedź, Republic of Poland. The exhibition was briefly suspended at one point. No long-distance movement has been observed.

Dimensions: 64 11/64×26 49/64×25 13/64 in.

Medium: Keeping Watch

Fragrance Notes: Floral Chypre - Fir, Orris Root, Daisy, Chalk Powder, Bitter Orange Oil

Afflatus: Echoes of Remembrance \[Mineral\] Songs Amid the Ruins

## Item

### "I am Honza!"

A strange puppet carved from basswood—the most common timber of Eastern Europe—together with worn linen and leather. The only uncommon thing about it was this: "Idiot! Don't look at me with those dull eyes of yours!" The words rang out from the interviewee, though her lips never moved.

### A Listener's Veil

A veil of white lace, so light it seems to have escaped gravity. According to the interviewee, throughout history, the broken memories, emotions, and experiences of countless people have taken shape as the tiny pearls and star-like gems that adorn it.

"Nothing is ever truly forgotten."

### Daisy-Dyed Vest

A child's vest, left behind in history's mud. Someone picked it up, washed it in silence, and dyed it daisy gold, bright as the flowers that grow wild over the ruins. Later, as it dried in the sunlight, the interviewee says she heard a murmur carried on the wind: "Don't leave me here, Mum."

## Story

### Portrait of an Intergenerational Phantom

What is tradition? Or perhaps the better question is this: how are people meant to remember it?

For those who have not yet known misfortune, the answer is obvious—it's food, clothing, movement, and speech. It's living within history, where even the smallest daily habits repeat themselves in the body like some ancient, inherited faith. Tradition is memory, and memory is the ever-living present.

Until the twentieth century arrived.

Humanity's understanding of the world swelled alongside its ambitions, and countless peoples were swept into political persecution, war, and even slaughter. Borders were torn apart by force. The march toward national independence was cut short, again and again, until people themselves—the most basic unit of any society—ended up losing their homes, their culture, even their language, whether they meant to or not.

So now, how are people meant to remember?

The uprooted cannot answer that question. They come instead to a mysterious building, seeking either lasting shelter or a few moments of peace.

In the undisturbed stillness, the shattered, the silenced, and the long-forgotten moments rose back into memory.

Someone remembered a song, one that had been sung in the fields of this land, and across the snow-laden plains beneath the birch:

"I have walked among every grave, searching for my mother's trace ..."

And with every voice that sang it, or perhaps under the shifting light, the song grew more alive, until at last—

She woke.

Experience has shown that once the puppet and the voice sense you pose no threat, and recognize you as nothing more than a helpless, desperate wanderer, they are likely—almost certain—to fade away on their own.

### A Lullaby for the Spirits

> You who walk in the sun, would you stay a while in the dark and speak the vows of shadow with me?

Whenever the castle's master waves her sleeves, the nesting ghosts and spirits spill forth—this is the tale told among those beyond its walls.

Shwing! A scythe swings, sending fragments flying and splattering liquid on the rusted armor. Whoosh! The fire roars, charring several bright scarlet orbs and emitting a pungent odor. In the corridor, white specters shriek as they narrowly avoid a series of rolling barrels.

"Get it together, silly-wigs! We're late for the party, we are!" the pumpkin-headed puppet yells, but the expression carved on its "face" looks more wretched than tearful.

Meanwhile, the ghosts in the fireplace are too busy tending to the flames to notice that the caramel apples have been charred black-brown, and the cider barrels roll into the wall, spitting their contents across the floor and walls ...

And finally, despite all the twists and turns, the fall banquet begins as scheduled, starting with a number of dapper ghosts dancing the waltz. Unbeknownst to them, those watching from outside the window add another detail to their tale of horror: "The master of the castle particularly favors the sound of a thumping splash."

#### "A Vow to the Spirits"

A white veil ravaged by time, found by the interviewee on the floor of a church. Between earnest blessings and sore farewells, it carries many heartfelt voices. Unable to bear the thought of letting them wither to nothing, she solemnly wears it upon her head, allowing their murmurs to resonate in her ears.

"You don't have to cry anymore ... I will be here with you. I swear." The interviewee's comfort proves effective, and the murmurs are hushed.

#### Pumpkin? Honza!

While the author was preparing to begin the interview, a wooden puppet with a pumpkin for a head shouted, "Trick or Treat!"

The interviewee apologized, claiming that this was how her friend expressed enthusiasm and that the eccentric attire and high-pitched tone were intended to create a festive atmosphere. The author presumes the folk tales about a "screaming pumpkin" can be traced to this creature.

#### Festive Ornament

Pumpkin flowers and vines covered in thorns. At the suggestion of the many ghostly friends living in the pumpkins, the interviewee has adorned her hair and the hems of her clothes with them. "The bright color symbolizes the hope and passion that this castle is lacking ..."

Prompted by the enthusiastic suggestions of the interviewee, the author reluctantly tried on a matching bracelet, which, of course, was far from comfortable. The author had no choice but to turn down the subsequent invitation to the "pumpkin costume party."
