## Story

### Children and Young Persons

In a family of limited means, it makes perfect sense for children to assist with housework and lightening the load for the busy adults. When the household income falls short of supporting the entire family, children were often encouraged to do other work to supplement their parents' earnings.

However, after the Industrial Revolution took the children out of their homes and into the factories, problems began to surface.

This problem plagued Britain from the late 18th century and well into the 20th, only to be definitively resolved by the Children and Young Persons Act of 1933.

Yet, for some families, this resolution came at a price, how was the Grove family supposed to make ends meet when children were no longer allowed to work in factories?

### Gently Comes the Thawing Breeze

> When the spring winds blow, their last embers of sorrow are swept away.

"She's gone. 3 months have passed since her last letter." As Flutterpage hurries down the steps, she hears an old woman speaking into the house. She hesitates, glancing back at the tilted mailbox with its noticeable crack—the reason the elderly woman often waits for her by the door.

Stamps are always in short supply during the war, and empty mailboxes have become the norm. Flutterpage had witnessed this quiet disappointment too many times. After a brief pause, she gently knocks on the door, presenting a primrose to the disappointed woman.

"Is this for me? How kind ... thank you, dear." Leaning on her walking stick, the woman steps outside the house. "I'm waiting for news—the kind that blooms like this flower."

Flutterpage lifts her gaze curiously. "You mean ...?"

"War lingers like the coldest winter. May peace arrive with the spring."

"I think I get it. When spring comes, I'll make sure you're the first to know—you can bet on it. Good news travels quick as anything on the wind ... and deliverin' is what I do best."

#### Spring Swallow

A paper swallow cradled by the spring breeze bears a primrose that flutters in the wind, much like the interviewee herself.

During the interview, subtle movements beneath her cloak may reveal the truth of how such delicate paper creations fly.

#### Bounty-Filled Rattan Basket

A rattan basket stuffed with letters sits beneath a small metal plane that powers it, keeping it aloft throughout our interview. We detect traces of arcane power from the plane, mirroring the traces found on the special Foundation badge she wears.

"It's meant to keep us from losing our way," she explains confidently, "I've never needed it, though—and even if I did, I have other means."

#### Vine-Covered Postgirl's Bag

A postman's bag brimming with springtime, curling vines as lively and resilient as the interviewee herself.

She cheerfully opens the bag, revealing an assortment of objects that reflect local customs: strange frogs, a white rooster's feather, her sister's sign-in card, and more. With equal warmth, she offers the writer a piece of candy.

#### "Invitation from the Sky"

A flight device commonly seen on small drones, a masterpiece of aerodynamics. Be careful to avoid birds during use.

Its engine power has been noticeably reduced for safety, but the interviewee seems too excited to care after receiving flight clearance.

#### Chirper-based Communicator

A set of communication devices centered on a Chirper, capable of recording, communicating, and telling the time. With an internal clock as accurate as an atomic clock, the devices require frequent maintenance.

The interviewee says she provides customized wake-up call services: you can choose to be woken by twitters, meows, barks, or even your mum's voice—gentle or fully mad! "This will do ya proud!"

#### New Definition of Craftsman Spirit

Even so, the craftsmanship it carries is genuine—it's not easy to replicate the appearance of handiwork using an assembly line.
