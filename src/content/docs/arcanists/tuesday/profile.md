## Cover

An arcanist's work. Exhibited in the 1990s for 21 years. Completed in the fall, on October 30. Originally exhibited in Texas, USA, and later stored in the St. Pavlov Foundation.

Dimensions: 65 7/20 x 19 3/10 x 12 99/100 in.

Medium: Fear

Fragrance note: Floral - Narcissus, Honeysuckle, Ginger, Basil

Afflatus: Phantom Hospitality \[Spirit\] Motel Owner

## Item

### Maid's Bandeau

A cotton headband commonly worn by motel attendants. Soft in texture and slightly faded from multiple washes.

"The motel owner's daughter" is a more convenient title than "the motel owner." She uses demureness and conformity to cover up and amplify the horror. This is something she has come to master.

### Motel Keys

A jingling set of keys, no different from the ones at the waist of every motel owner.

She has memorized each key and its corresponding door in the motel, like a mother knows her own children, or perhaps even better. Sometimes, the keyholes would change position or shape, almost as if a child is playing a prank. But that won't be a problem for the mother.

### "A Baby"

Wrapped snugly in a white cotton cloth, it is silent most of the time, except for the occasional wiggle or cry.

Raising this "baby" is easy for her; she gives it all the love and affection it needs. There's no doubt that she's a qualified mother—the interviewee claims, smiling.

## Story

### Always in Style

It has always existed.

From the haunted stables and outhouses of the Middle Ages to the ghost-plagued castles of Gothic literature, it constantly changed forms, waiting for more visitors—more nourishment. A ship then carried it across the ocean to the flourishing New World.

In this land, there was never a shortage of legends, both old and new. It became an abandoned manor or a cabin in the woods, joyfully devouring every visitor from the wilderness, along with their screams and cries.

The 20th century ushered in a new wave of stories. No one knew exactly where they came from—a friend of a friend, a transfer student, local oddities in the corners of newspaper. Their themes vary from cars, ovens, videotapes, sewers to cities and the impending new millennium. These tales gained their own names in modern culture: urban legends. In them, it sensed something familiar—the taste of fear and the need for fear.

And so, It quietly laid its foundations in the dim morning light, growing a simple wooden porch and a flickering sign, laying down its creaking stairs. At dawn, drivers on Route 77 suddenly noticed a motel that was a little out of date.

Now the motel needed a manager, of course.

### Dollhouse

Pandora Wilson: It seems as though, more often than not, you prefer to present yourself in the role you play. What do you think role play has to do with fear?

Tuesday: Oh, role play ain't no part of fear. It's merely laying a foundation for the stories.

Tuesday: People will write the bulk of it themselves. They give it a plot and indulge in it. My role is just a guide ... a waitress guiding her guests.

Pandora Wilson: And your motel? It's playing a role too, isn't it?

Tuesday: Actors can always use a proper stage. When people step onto it, they naturally play the roles assigned to them. It is just a stage, nothing more.

Tuesday: Like how people expect to see a simple, hardworking country girl in every farmhouse, or to see multiple rooms inside a suitcase ...

Pandora Wilson: Is that some kind of metaphor? I'm afraid it's not an easy one to understand.

Tuesday: Oh, I'm just describing a motel, or a stage, or maybe a cute little suitcase.

Tuesday: To me, it is just a tiny little dollhouse, a dollhouse that you could pick up with one hand.

### Story of the Jade Vase

> Beauty is but a fleeting show, what truths lie beneath the glow?

A young scholar set off for the capital to take the imperial examinations. Fearing misfortune on the long journey, he hired an elderly servant to accompany him.

While passing through a remote mountain range, lightning split the sky, heralding the approach of a violent storm. Fearing the ravages of the elements, the young scholar spotted an abandoned temple in the distance and wished to take shelter.

His servant tried desperately to stop him, for the temple was rumored to be haunted. Locals called it the Shrine of the Jade Bottle Lady, whose statue held a sacred vessel and was said to delight in tormenting travelers. Those who entered to escape the rain were often frightened out of their wits, and some lost their very souls.

The scholar scoffed at the warnings and stepped inside.

At dawn the next morning, the servant searched the temple. The scholar's belongings lay scattered across the floor, but the man himself had vanished without a trace.

#### Lotus Crown

Hair styled like clouds, adorned with an awe-inspiring lotus, though the coiled silver snake deters many from starting a conversation.

When asked why the lotus throne hides a transforming snake, the interviewee smiled and whispered, "Hush, don't tell—it's something you shouldn't have seen."

#### Silver Waist Charm

Jade and silver beads form a shimmering web that sways with every step and passing breeze. It gathers the worldly loves and hates of each believer, as well as their briefest glimpses of fear.

#### The Three Poisons

A jade-green vial brimming with life, with lush willow branches and nectar sweet as honey. "Why mind the trivial details of appearances? For now, just savor its sweetness," the interviewee urged. "Come, don't be afraid, it's well-behaved."

### Cold Cradle

> She hums a somber lullaby—a mother's way to soothe her trembling, heart-pierced child.

Death, resurrection, new life.

Ascetics in pointed hats, attendants bearing candles, the Madonna robed in splendor ... The procession moves slowly, in silence and solemnity, spreading through the street.

The veiled Madonna walks at the center, the bundle in her arms pulsing with unease. They say it contains a saint's heart, now sealed within an ornate golden reliquary. Not an uncommon occurrence—when a saint dies, her body and her relics become sacred icons and symbols.

She pats the bundle gently, as if soothing a tantrum. She understands what drives this devotion: it is an affection that cannot be named. People have always longed to be closer to what they love. It is an instinct older than memory.

#### Ultramarine Droplets

A blue tear, frozen at the corner of the interviewee's eye.

In the silence of her downcast gaze, the writer lets go of the urge to ask what pigment gives this blue its shade. Even if ultramarine is no longer precious, a mother's tears are worth their weight in gold.

#### Thorned Halo

A headscarf that should be soft and white, adorned instead with sharp, black thorns—their lines like the marks left by a lash.

Its maker believes a mother should teach her children the shape of thorns early, even if it might prick their fingers.

#### In Her Embrace

A bundle, gently soothed without end. The key resting on it trembles with each touch. "It really is a good child—insisting on keeping my key safe in its own way."

A mother always accepts her child's kindness, and the interviewee is no exception.
