## Cover

Arcanist work. Exhibition date and duration unknown. Completed on January 12th in the summer. Exhibited on nameless red soil, in the hands of someone youthful.

Dimensions: 86 7/32x42 33/64x7 3/32 inches

Medium: Obsidian

Fragrance note: Woody, Guaiacum, Redcurrant, Chestnut, Amber

Afflatus: Heart of the Red Rock \[Mineral\], The Original Sacred Flame

## Item

### Descended Fire

An ever-burning, fiercely blazing tuft of hair. Shadows can be faintly seen dancing within the constantly changing flames.

When a flame ignites in the wilderness, the first tribe to master it becomes the ruler of the land.

### Bark Painting

Ancient paintings on eucalyptus bark, chronicling stories of the past. Pictures are the earliest form of writing, yet are always more direct than words.

### 1000 Years of Lone Journey

A beautiful ring, with the warmth of fire and the curvature of a perfect circle. Like someone walking around a mountain, it forms a pattern that has both a beginning and an end, yet is endless. Just like a promise repeatedly fulfilled and like the long, arduous journey of the one who keeps it.

## Story

### Murals of Springs

"I need to carve the center first before I do the edges," a voice says, young and with burning hot hands.

"No, I think you need to carve the edges first, then the center. That way, it will be easier to fix on the wall," another voice says, accompanied by the crackling of burning flames.

This is a debate, predating most of the world's conflicts, before sunlight became known as "sunlight," and before water was known as "water."

"Alright, I think I'll start from the inside," the young voice says.

"Yes, I think I'll start from the outside," the voice of the burning fire says.

This is a painting that came too early, earlier than when people used coal for outlines and ground rocks for pigments. Rings nested within each other, ultimately meeting under the guidance of two hands. This is the mark of a spring, representing water, the holdings of the rainy season, and the essential source of life.

"Hey! We are done!"

"Yes, we are done. Now, we can go drink some water."

The thirst brought by joy is the key to the return journey.

Thus, the beginning of the story is planted.
