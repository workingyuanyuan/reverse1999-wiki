## Story

### Spring Elfin

People are always in pursuit of the unknown and attaching some kind of meaning to it.

For instance, the Spring Fay, who can make people's wishes come true, is hidden in wishing springs within unnamed forests.

In a certain forest where the fog never dissipates, there is an old pot from which spring water gushes out endlessly. As fay maidens used to wander around the spring, the legend of "the fay in springs" came about, which subsequently spread to the nearby villages and towns. They all said that you need only throw a coin into the pot and all your hopes and dreams will come true.

The legend might be false, or it might be a slight deviation from reality.

What we know for sure is that not all fay are that competent. At the very least, La Source isn't.

### Antinomies

> Mathematics is a vast castle one could explore for all eternity.

Everyone has heard the tale of the spring elfin—a mischievous elfin dwells within the waters; by tossing a coin into the spring, one may make a wish to her.

What they do not know is that the elfin dislikes coins just as much as she dislikes people's wishes.

Yet somewhere within a certain labyrinth, people cast coins into the elfin's jar for an entirely different reason: to calculate the probability of a coin being noticed by the jar's owner.

The labyrinth resembles an immense castle, where every corridor is crowded with numbers and symbols. They pile upon one another, spiral into endless repetition, and merge with the walls themselves to form riddles beyond comprehension. It is a playground of the unknown, a place that delights in concealing its profound mathematical nature behind elegant staircases and soaring domes, luring every visitor into quiet fascination.

The spring elfin understands none of it. Still, from time to time, she wanders in, scoops up a discarded coin, and arranges it into an equation—especially after the endless stream of wish-makers at her spring has chattered one time too many and thoroughly exhausted her patience.

#### Beret

A small hat made of wool and linen that holds up the hair.

Supposedly, the hat's purpose is to increase the wearer's height, thereby augmenting dignity and gravitas. But this hat also provides a scholarly air and, of course, a hint of playfulness.

#### Laurel Tiara

A headband woven from fresh bay leaves with flower buds that give off a sweet fragrance. Laurel wreaths often symbolize victory and honor while also granting the wearer a clear mind. Most importantly, the green complements her hair color well.

#### Terracotta Jar

A terracotta pot found on a shallow riverbank, detailed with geometric paintings that exhibit fine aesthetics. Its unexpected lightness makes it easy on the user's wrist.
