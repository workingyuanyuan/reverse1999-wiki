## Item

### Jar of Fireflies

A small, simple jar. Inside, fireflies drift up and down. Judging from how tightly it's held, this jar is clearly more than the "decoration" the interviewee claims it to be.

### "Eagle's Shashka"

A saber reproduced one-to-one using modern craftsmanship. It inherits a name passed down through generations. Its original once followed its master into battle as a deadly blade. Now, when drawn, it serves as a banner to inspire soldiers.

### Gryphon Legion Belt

The insignia was designed by his foster daughter, Moldir—who also, allegedly, chose the name for the Gryphon Legion. The interviewee's expression suggests he is quite satisfied with the design. "This troop deserves to protect a broader world," he adds.

## Story

### A Name Etched in the Wall

The children of soldiers who retired in 1945 grew up in the afterglow of the Great Patriotic War. They donned their uniforms, entered the military academy, and became the celebrated "next generation."

As the shadow of the Cold War descended, the academy's progressive education shook these young inheritors—they would no longer fight for hollow concepts like territory, but pursue something far grander: the "Vision of Balance."

A young man named Igor rose above his peers. His name began appearing in military briefings: commanding cross-border operations, quelling rebellions, directly overseeing high-level missions such as containing arcanists ... The years carved scars and wrinkles into his face like knife marks, yet his gaze grew ever colder, like the dark current beneath a frozen river. He had nearly forgotten—both the ideals the Great Patriotic War had etched into his heart and the myth of peace and order.

Until that day, when he studied the "invitation" on his desk—a yellowed, timeworn envelope bearing the seal of Zeno's Tortoise. The Zeno Military Academy, an international institution capable of maintaining peace, a center of order dedicated to the true "Vision of Balance."

He walked alone into that imposing building. Inside the Hall of Merit, countless names—some strange, some familiar—were carved into the towering walls. He recalled the fierce ambition of his youth: to carve the name his father gave him onto those stone walls, to stand alongside history.

### A Notebook Hidden in His Pocket

***Page 1: Special Purpose***

The commander in chief gifted me a notebook. He hoped I would inherit his habit: recording every challenge from my subordinates. They bring vigilance and wisdom, making the troops more loyal and brave.

***Page 17: A Record of Failure***

Captain Chris pleaded with me to order the flank's retreat. The supply line had been cut—sooner or later, they'd all freeze to death in those mountains.

I began to waver: "Hold for twelve more hours and reinforcements will arrive ..."

He roared: "But twelve hours means hundreds of lives!!"

It was a hard call, but I agreed to the retreat.

\*Note: The flank was lost. Ten times as many soldiers died as a result. ~~Mercy destroyed that battle.~~

***Page 38: Reflections on Sacrifice***

The medical team sent to assist was trapped in the city—an obvious trap. As commander, I could not afford to be foolish.

I rejected all objections and pleas, ordering a full-speed retreat and abandoning the rescue.

We won the battle, but the logistics chief refused to attend the victory celebration. I realized then that my troops would likely lose considerable medical support going forward.

\*Note: An overcorrection born of shortsightedness. I still paid the price for my own foolishness.

***Page 69: Disagreement with the Chief of Staff***

Clearly, morale was low. We needed a chance to breathe.

I refused to execute high command's order to hold our position. Chief of staff Krutov strongly disagreed, protesting with barely concealed sarcasm.

I told him: leak word of our retreat, then blanket the withdrawal route with artillery fire.

Retreat was seen as "cowardly failure" at the time, but later proved an effective tactic for buying time to prepare our counteroffensive—we successfully lured the enemy deep and struck back.

\*Note: Preserving morale is vital, but feigning weakness is an even greater skill.

### The Leader

Pandora Wilson: Sorry for the wait, Mr. Igor. Our preparations took longer than usual because we rarely interview someone with a ... delicate identity like yours.

Igor: That is understandable. I take no offense.

Pandora Wilson: First question—and forgive the indelicacy—why did you agree to an unofficial interview from a magazine?

Igor: I did my research on you—independent principles, wide-ranging topics, and no record of manipulating public opinion.

Pandora Wilson: But surely we're not the only publication like that.

Igor: An old friend of mine often submits to you. I have read a few issues of UTTU myself in my spare time.

### Returning Nevermore

> Thus he leaves the light, back to the eternal darkness.

He is no stranger to light or darkness.

The admiral used to attend those grand, glorious feasts, raising a glass with his brothers-in-arms, celebrating the triumph of an ideal. He knew how to dress for the occasion—a soldier's dress uniform was usually enough, though on rarer occasions, something finer was called for. His children would straighten his collar for him—a small act never asked for.

He has ridden through the dark far more often than he ever has in the light. The warhorse carries its rider, and the rider carries death. "Whatever the cost"—he said it himself, and so did the others raising their glasses at those feasts. His column advanced in silence, passing beneath the gold-lettered names in the Hall of Merit, leaving behind name after name of the fallen. Then they rode on, toward the shadowed forest, and the leopards, lions, and wolves that waited within it.

The admiral knows where he belongs. In the place beyond all light, a door stands open for him. But where do his soldiers go?

A gatekeeper has arrived at the gates of the underworld.

#### Flames of the Ninth Hell

A beacon guiding the way through the depths of hell, its light entrusted to a golden gryphon. The interviewee has kept it close in hand ever since, never once setting it down.

Shapes drift like shadows in the light it casts. According to the interviewee, they are the ones who once walked beside him.

"Those who walk in darkness need more than light."

#### Iron Mask

Stripped of every ornament that once meant glory, leaving only cold, hard metal and a weight that cannot be set down.

The interviewee has forgotten how long he's worn it, or why he first stood guard here. Like accepting the passage of time, he has accepted this instinct to guard the gateway into hell.

#### "Oath Behind the Gate"

This sword is drawn only before the "gate." Wherever its edge points, the living halt, the dead turn, and all things fall back into their natural order.

"Abandon all hope, ye who enter here."

The interviewee, as ever, turns back all who would cross the "gate."
