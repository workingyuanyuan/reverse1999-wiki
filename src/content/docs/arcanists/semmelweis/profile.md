## Cover

An infected's work. Exhibited in the 1990s for 19 years. Completed in summer, on the 19th of August. Initially exhibited in Szeged, Csongrád-Csanád County, within the Republic of Hungary, and later moved to the St. Pavlov Foundation.

Dimensions: 65 3/4 x 19 5/16 x 10 1/4 in.

Medium: Blood Agate

Fragrance Note: Woody - Pepper, Yew, Labdanum, Cedar, Leather

Afflatus: Beguiling Wiles \[Mineral\] Field Investigation

## Item

### Velvet Cloak

A black velvet cloak based around the theme "Born Again," designed by the creative director of Elsa Raleigh's flagship brand at the end of the century. The perfect raindrop lace edges, tasseled frills, and asymmetric design highlight the meticulous attention to detail under an overall simple concept.

However, the interviewee stated they were unaware of the artistic significance behind the item, using it only to shield themselves from the sun.

"It has changed owners countless times, and I will not be its last" — Interviewee Statement

### Portable Ritual Disk

We have repeatedly requested to examine this item closely, but the interviewee has always smilingly deflected our attempts. It is speculated that the item is a portable disk carried by the interviewee for years and loaded with various arcane incantations. Nowadays, it is rare to see the interviewee using such disks, but it still accompanies her regularly.

### White Satin Scarf

Clean, tidy, spotless, and every fold perfectly flat. "Anyone who can keep a scarf like this so immaculate all year round is truly a terrifying entity," the reporters of our agency unanimously agreed.

## Story

### The Age of Oddity

The world entered the last decade of the 20th century. As the yellow tram in Budapest passed Bem Square along the Danube, anyone would have noticed that the statue that once stood in the center of the square had been toppled, the youths holding "Molotov cocktails" had become parents, and their children had begun to consume a new kind of horror story.

Dracula never dies. In the novel, he is repeatedly killed by travelers from the New World, but outside the story, tales of the blood-drinking monster continue to spread.

In a dark and damp attic, a black-haired girl places a letter into an envelope. Soon after, it will be sent to the Budapest branch of the Foundation.

She has made her choice.

### A Misunderstood Fairy Tale

A thick dossier is placed in front of the visitor, white gloves turning the pages with an increasingly heavier motion.

Finally, the lady closes the dossier and neatly returns it to the black-haired girl wearing a white scarf.

"Such a remarkable talent for information gathering. You've passed. In six months, you've collected all the information on every child in the orphanage, even recording their talents for arcanum, one after another.

I must admit, you're naturally cut out for investigation work. But are you sure you want to join the Foundation. Perhaps it's not as perfect as you might imagine."

The black-haired girl goes silent for a moment, then she reveals a gentle smile:

"One of the teachers here often tells us a story—about a cheetah cub who becomes friends with a deer."

"It's an interesting story, and the teacher tells it well. It's just that she always leaves one thing out—cheetahs can't be friends with deer."

"Cheetahs are predators. To them, the whole world is just prey."

"Do you see yourself as that cheetah?"

The lady from the Foundation asks.

"No, I'm just a deer who wants to grasp her own destiny."

The black-haired girl blinks her doelike eyes.

### A Knock on the Door

> Triumph requires a steady hand and a steady heart, whether in battle or in art.

In the late 18th century, Winged Key Archery became an official event in the Uluru Games.

It all began with an arcane artefact: the Winged Key. Its instinct to seek out keyholes caused all kinds of unnecessary trouble. First played in Yorkshire over a wager. An Arcanist claimed he could, in the split second before the key reached the lock, fire a silver arrow through its hole and pin it to the door.

To test their skill, ten keys and ten locks were prepared. The game was on. Though the Arcanist only managed to hit one rusty key and lost spectacularly, the game nonetheless became a popular pastime, and it's been played ever since.

#### Feminine Grace

One of the velvet hats that were à la mode among the ladies of London. In a city where trends change like the weather, knowing which hat to wear for the right occasion is an essential part of a lady's education.

"Just to be clear, the feather on this hat isn't a critter feather as the shopkeeper claimed. It's a plain old ostrich feather," the interviewee commented.

#### Archery Support Kit

A handy set of tools for bow and arrow maintenance, with the dual purpose of making a lady's belt appear more beautiful and elegant.

The interviewee praised its functionality—in fact, she was quite intrigued by the idea of adding more arcane implements.

#### Beechwood's Pledge

The crafting of a fine longbow is as complex as the crafting of a ritual wand. In terms of material selection, both require similar attention to detail.

With her deep knowledge of oak, the lady was immediately drawn to the bow. After a brief touch of the wood, she realized—the bowyer had once studied under her family, as she could hear the faint whispers within the beech.

### The Wax and Wane of the Heart

> Though the moon waxes and wanes, her heart holds true.

The moon hangs high in the night sky, uneasy, restless, and ever-changing.

She arrives by Death's invitation, moonlight draping over her like a delicate veil. Along twisting paths, she lingers near the windows of those destined to die.

No matter how lost or scared people feel, they cannot stop her visit—just as the dying cannot turn Death away, just as the moon must cycle through its phases.

Her song always comes before the funeral dirges, weaving through final words and confessions to create each lament anew. Some who study arcanum believe this is a sacred mourning ritual, perhaps tied to the moon's arcane influence on madness. Semmelweis, however, offers a different view: "Could it simply be an illusion born from the fear of death?"

#### Gilded Copper Hand Mirror

A gilded copper hand mirror decorated with clover and beech leaf motifs. Rather than watch her own reflection, the interviewee seems drawn to the grotesque relief on the back, its warped smile always seeming to follow the viewer.

"There is no doubting the beauty of the mirror, but I find mirrors only show comfortable lies. I'd rather face what's real."

#### Ragged Veil

"It's all a haze, like mist drifting across my eyes. It takes me back to Vienna before the 'Storm' arrived."​

The interviewee speaks softly, lifting her arm as the gray veil slips aside, revealing a golden bracelet encircling her wrist.

"Don't let it fool you."

#### Moon Phase Body Paint

Painted along the curve of her spine, the moon's phases shimmer in pigments made from wolf fur, unicorn horn, and the ink of a moonlit squid.

"The waxing and waning of the moon speaks of a truth," she murmurs. "Chaos and change are the world's constant companions."

With a graceful turn, she hides the artwork from view.

"And, of course, such transformations are never meant to be plainly seen."
