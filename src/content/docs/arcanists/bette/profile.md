## Story

### California Sunrise

In 1895, the gates of the Lumière Factory opened. As the workers filed out of the factory, a breeze with the smell of film stock also blew from Boulevard des Capucines to the west coast of the City of Angels.

The ample sunshine and the typical Mediterranean climate in California had brought more opportunities to Los Angeles. Using the magnificent scenery as their background, countless film companies had gathered there, building an empire of film and television of a new era.

Silent film or talkies? Black-and-white or color?

It was at the end of 1930 and no one cared about such meaningless topics any more. The producers, resourceful and power-wielding, could pluck the stars out of the sky, shape them at will and throw them away when they twinkled no more. No, my friend, don't call it "cruel;" it is plain fact that a sophisticated machine like the movie industry needs to renew its parts from time to time.

The girl with a dream finally opened the golden gates to the factory. She hoped to win a place among the radiant stars, and she made it. Thanks to the little "tricks" they used in the film production, even the most experienced movie critics could not tell the difference between the real star and the stunt double from where they sat.

That was how she became a cheap substitute for those valuable assets.

### A Life in Frames

> Now needed by over ten filming sites, she and her busy character have become one.

Bette is more than used to color now.

The square talkie-boxes will project their vivid pictures. The studio is a tighter, easier box to assemble than the rest. The first mailroom is beige, the second break room is green, and the furniture in each box is done in primary colors. She moves through these clean, modern boxes—see, every era has its own idea of "modern."

It's a brisk, busy era. Ten city stories are in production at once, with twenty-three writers all brainstorming together. The metropolis girls are in their little office boxes, sipping coffee, typing away, picking up phones, and they've always got something witty to say. Even though she's merely a stand-in for the main character, the new cameras faithfully capture her every detail—right down to the color of her plaid skirt. These grids will air in prime time, then set off a fashion craze the very next month.

Box by colorful box, modern society assembles the stories of these girls into new fairy tales and myths. They're showing off their professional side, and Bette finds nothing wrong with that.

At the director's call, she darts to the next box. Umbrella, Mondrian hat, tea-colored plaid skirt—three minutes later, she's become yet another brand-new city girl. Few people know that her silhouette will air on twelve different channels. Between one box and the next, she's indispensable.

#### "Punctuality"

A simple, elegant wristwatch, the kind of thing every urban professional has. The interviewee knew it was the one the moment she saw it.

Her schedule is packed with one shoot after another, and being on time matters more than almost anything else.

#### "Mondrian Style"

A long-handled umbrella that matches the interviewee's hat perfectly—practical and elegant. Ever since Yves Saint Laurent unveiled his Mondrian dresses to the world, that look has become something of a universal formula in fashion. Now, the spirit of *Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow* has found its way onto this all-weather umbrella.

#### Doubled Windsors

A lively yellow, a calm blue—an actress must always be ready to serve her role.

While demonstrating how quickly she could switch ties, the interviewee ended up tying them in a knot. Still, a little slip like that doesn't change how we see her overall.
