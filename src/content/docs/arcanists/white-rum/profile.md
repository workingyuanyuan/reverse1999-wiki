## Cover

An Awakened's work. Exhibited in the mid-17th century for approximately 300 years. Completed in winter, on January 15th. Originally exhibited in England, and active in the Caribbean region. After a long hiatus, it later drifted to São Paulo, Brazil.

Dimensions: 27 3/11 x 14 9/25 x 5 7/10 in.

Medium: Bottle

Fragrance note: Fougère aquatic, seawater, water lily, rum, patchouli

Afflatus: A mind adrift, \[Intellect\], drifting in a bottle

## Item

### Boyle's Drift Bottle

A bottle with the ability to "scale objects proportionally." It is said that Robert Boyle mastered this arcane skill through experiments and imbued it into glass, creating many drifting bottles with magical powers.

The bottle kept her alive, allowing her to come and go freely, but also keeping her forever enclosed and isolated.

### Yellowed Canvas

A piece of canvas torn from a ship's sail, it retains its original size as it has never entered the bottle, now yellowed from wind and sun exposure. It still bears a dodo pattern, along with stains and scars from the past. According to the interviewee, it holds many shared memories.

### About "Anne Bonny"

A former captain who, in her words even now, guards her cherished partner. Sometimes, the partner speaks to her as if talking to herself, telling her, "I'm doing well now."

## Story

### Maritime History

**The End of the Privateers**

From the 16th to the 18th century, amid the maritime discoveries and tense rivalries, governments issued letters of marque to privateers, making them "legal pirates." However, as wars came to an end, privateers reverted back to their old ways. Most pirates would perish in fierce naval pursuits, but a few would struggle on, living a life of plunder and spoil despite being increasingly hunted. Occasionally, legends would surface of a captain who lived on after sinking to the ocean floor, or a pirate who escaped prison only to become a navy sailor, or an ancient pirate ship that hid inside a drifting bottle to avoid sinking.

**The Age of Exploration**

Also known as the Age of Great Voyages, explorers set foot on unknown lands, expanding maps of the known world. Although governments often complained about the immense financial cost involved, they quickly changed their tune when ships returned laden with gold, promoting the development of new trade routes. Ocean voyages meant adventure. Thanks to the spirit of adventure during the Age of Exploration, the world became dramatically more interconnected.

**The Last Dodo**

Extinct between 1688 and 1715.

*(However, a marine archaeologist who claims to have collected dodo droppings insists that a few dodos still live aboard a long-distance ocean vessel).*

### The Spirit of Exploration Lives On

Pandora Wilson: I can't help but wonder how this mysterious bottle of yours can hold someone inside while maintaining stability.

White Rum: I think I grasp your meaning, matey, but not to worry. Even if you were to shatter my bottle to bits, so long as you can put the pieces back together, the original arcane skill won't be affected.

#### "New Journey" Cabin

A "space cabin" made by recasting Robert Boyle's flask into a more aerodynamic shape, topped off with a brand new propulsion system.

The interviewee was dismayed to learn she could not exit the bottle after liftoff, but Laplace's researchers have promised to rectify the issue once a liquid ocean is found on another planet.

#### Solar Sail

A light sail propelled by solar energy. Due to its small surface area and technological limitations, it's more symbolic than practical.

The interviewee was very pleased that the researcher who modified the design respected the traditions of sailing ships.

#### "Her Ambition"

An old protector embarking on yet another voyage—this time, into space.

"Ol' Captain Anne, just think, the stars are full of places no one has ever been!" The interviewee is full of hope. She has already found a new target for the pirate ship.
