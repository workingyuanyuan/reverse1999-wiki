## Cover

An arcanist's work. Exhibited in the 1930s for 25 years. Completed in fall on November 1. Initially exhibited in County Down, United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, and later relocated to Greater London.

Dimensions: 74 11/64 x 31 57/64 x 13 25/32 in.

Medium: Wizened Willow Branch

Fragrance Note: Amber Notes, Spirit Cat Essence, Musk, Myrrh, Ashen Amber

Afflatus: Budding Wizened Tree \[Plant\] Ritual Witch

## Item

### A Pointed Hat

A popular hat choice in London's hat boutiques, worn by many ladies at the time of the interview. However, it was the interviewee who truly made it iconic.

Sometimes, it is not the outfit that defines the individual, but the individual that defines the outfit.

### Dark Purple Cloak

This long cloak, once all the rage among women for its unique collar design, continues to be a favorite for the interviewee long after the trend had been forgotten.

Its hem strategically conceals her legs from the side and rear, sparing her from unwanted attention.

### A Custom Prosthesis

Built to suit the interviewee's body and habits, this prosthetic boasts flexible joints and an almost perfectly lifelike weight and appearance, making it a genuine work of art.

Nevertheless, the interviewee describes it as "an ugly, awkward fire poker."

## Story

### Delayed Freedom, Belated Peace

The 1921 treaty marked the attainment of Ireland's long-sought independence. The shadows of conflict faded, and a compromise that most could live with was put forward. However, opinions remained divided.

While some believed the compromise to be just the first part of a larger plan, others considered it an outright betrayal of freedom. The debate over peace seemed to have no end in sight.

And so, the poor, innocent black swan, entrusting her faith to peace, danced gracefully that day.

Her misjudgment took everything from her—her family, her dreams, her home ...

Leaving only a stiff, clunky prosthetic as compensation.

### The Power of Words

"Poor lass ... you're so young, and yet ... Be sure to come to me if you ever need anything, alright?"

The old woman across the street looks at everyone with pitying eyes, but after losing her son in the war, how could she spare any sympathy for anyone else?

"I'm not your daughter. Save your energy for yourself."

"Afternoon, miss, we live just down the street. We're getting married in a week—would you like to come?"

A new couple? Everyone else is struggling just to get by—and they're inviting a stranger to their wedding, of all things?

"A wedding? You'd be better off saving that money for your rent."

"Shh ... quiet, the witch is here—watch out or she'll put a curse on you."

"She curses anyone who gets too close to her—just look at Mrs. Kate and the Jameses!"

Utter foolishness, but it's not like I care—as long as they leave me alone.

"Your lives are already cursed with misery anyway, with or without me."

### Second Place

Pandora Wilson: It's been a long time since you were out on the field. What's going through your mind right now?

Willow: Honestly, I am bored out of my mind.

Pandora Wilson: Oh, that's unexpected ... I thought you'd have deeper feelings than just boredom.

Willow: Were you expecting lofty dreams of glory? Or for me to speak of a burning desire for victory? Only a child would waste their thoughts on such trifles, and I curse anyone who gives a damn about them—may they never get what they want.

Pandora Wilson: I didn't mean to bring up any of those topics, but you make thought-provoking points all the same. So, what do you really care about when it comes to performing?

Willow: There's a particular person I want to watch my performance.

Pandora Wilson: Would you care to tell us who that is?

Willow: Charlotte. Charlotte O'Hagan.

Pandora Wilson: So, this is about proving something to yourself? And to her?

Willow: Who knows? I just want to dedicate this dance to her, and wish her all the best of health.

### The Last Portent

> Though fate tries to dim her light, she dances on, burning bright.

When the first omen fell, she received a revelation.

Some distant, indistinct prophecy delivered through the morning sun, on glistening dew, and in a sharp sting between her toes. The dancer must perform an endless dance, never ceasing even should her feet run red with blood—not until the gods answer, not until fate reveals another fortunate omen.

But not every dancer is fortunate enough to receive an answer. She plunged herself into merciless training, imagining herself as a die in the most torturous times, wavering between giving up in one moment and eagerly hoping for a response in the next. She continued, suffering through the temptation of rest, tormented by the space between total triumph and utter failure. And then came a knock.

"Charlotte, are you alright?"

This was the inevitable interruption every immersed player must unfortunately face: concern from loved ones. Fortunately, just as she prepared to abandon the ritual and respond, fate cast a second lucky die her way.

#### Bronze Oil Lamp

An unremarkable bronze oil lamp.

The mage has held golden lamps fueled by dragon fat and danced with nothing but a torch in hand. When asked why she ultimately settled on such an ordinary lamp, she replied, "It doesn't interfere with my magic, and it doesn't trip me up while I dance. Just like how my expression right now won't stop you from continuing with these pointless questions."

#### "A Veil Upon My Head"

A piece of gear that's both stylish and practical, capable of blocking a dragon's breath attack—once.

Before the game even started, the adventurer insisted on calling it this to "save time coming up with fancy names for equipment."

#### Bell-Lined Dancer's Belt

A waist chain and anklets strung with bells. Legends say the mage learned a long-lost secret from an ancient being, replacing traditional incantations with dance steps that harmonize with chimes and firelight, wielding the power to drive away fiends. The crisp bell sounds also perfectly mask the "thud-thud" of a prosthetic limb touching the ground.

### Reborn and Restored

> Swallows swoop betwixt the willow, ushering spring to its withered branches.

She hated the rain and sudden shifts in weather. The changing temperature and humidity brought pain to her stump.

She hated spring—the so-called season of renewal, greenery, and new life. She loathed the very symbolism of it. She hated how new sprouts would grow just to mock those that had already withered. She hated it all. To her, every raindrop that struck the eaves and tapped the glass sounded like a heavy sigh.

She spent much of her time leaning against the window frame, watching swallows build new nests. Their graceful movements awakened something buried deep in her heart. She imitated their motions—bending, stretching, practicing. Then one day, when a tender green bud emerged from the old scar on the withered willow, her feelings began to shift. Spring suddenly wasn't so bad.

Its rain still brought dampness and pain, but she started seeing more facets of the world. She couldn't change what had already happened, but at least the future held all sorts of possibilities. Even lost swallows could find a new home beneath the willow.

#### Springtime Swallows

Inlaid with pearls, adorned with enamel pendants, covered in gold curved into graceful shapes—the theme is unmistakable. Spring arrives, flowers blossom, have the swallows returned?

Beauty always comes at a price, but an experienced dancer carries the weight effortlessly.

#### Verdant Shawl

The immortal brings flowing ribbons, dotting the spring waters that mirror mountains.

In Eastern dance, ribbons and sleeves often serve as extensions of the dancer's body. A skilled dancer can move the soft fabrics as if they were additional arms. The interviewee had already perfected this art by the age of eleven.

#### Willow Sprig

Tender willow branches extend from the dancer's sleeve. Unlike her usual somber style, the sprouting green vibrates with life.

Verdant leaves of yesterday remain, and endless layers grow anew.
