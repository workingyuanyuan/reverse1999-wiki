## Story

**Boy:** Boss! Over here! It's right here!

**Boy:** Andrea and the others dumped a bunch of old puppets here yesterday. I saw it myself!

**???:** Those fair-weather types ... As soon as the theater closed, they lost all interest in their craft!

**???:** But then I can't complain too much. Their loss is my gain.

**Boy:** Boss, why don't you just go buy some new ones down at the import shop? My sister's birthday was the day before yesterday, and Mom got her a princess puppet there.

**???:** Idiot! I'm out of a job, remember! Every coin counts now!

**Puppeteer:** Besides, I've got to use my skills as best as I can. Fixing up old ones is easy and cheap!

**Puppeteer:** Fine, fine, a promise is a promise. Here's the candy I said I'd get you. Take it.

**Boy:** Thanks, boss!

**Boy:** But, um, be careful, okay.

The girl stops and turns.

**Puppeteer:** Hmm? What do you mean?

**Boy:** Don't you know this place is haunted?! Last time we came to dump trash here, we all heard someone say "hello," and then the trash started shaking! We went flying out of here after that.

**Boy:** The old folks say it's haunted for sure!

The mountain of trash lies still for now, no sign of "being haunted." The only thing drifting around them is a nauseating stench.

**Puppeteer:** Ha, you're just rattled by some old Shade stories!

**Puppeteer:** Only kids like you and stubborn old fools believe in that kinda stuff. You've watched too many movies about arcanists and exorcisms!

The puppeteer waves off her less-than-useful assistant and bends over to rummage through the trash.

**Puppeteer:** Tch, what is this on here? So greasy. Gross.

**Everecho:** What is she doing?

**Puppeteer:** This one's leg is broken. This one's eyes are gone. Come on, I know they're just wood, but have a heart, people!

The puppeteer picks through the trash pile from one end to the other, scooping up every puppet she spots.

Until at last, she picks up Everecho.

**Puppeteer:** Oh my, this one is a real cutie. Take a look at her little face. This is what a puppet should look like!

After a quick examination, the puppeteer adds Everecho to the rest of her haul and leaves the trash heap behind.

**Puppeteer:** This should be enough to work with. Let's find somewhere else to work before this smell gets stuck on our clothes.

West Wing, The Building

The puppeteer walks a distance away toward a relatively clean corner, sets the puppets down, and lines them up in a row on the ground.

Then she pulls a poster out from inside her coat and presses it flat against the wall.

It shows a cosmonaut flashing a victory sign as she travels into space.

Everecho reads the printed title to herself.

**Everecho:** The cosmonaut?

The puppeteer, head down, opens her bag without noticing the puppet's murmur.

**Puppeteer:** Hammer, needle and thread, water, towel ...

She lays out her tools and starts working away on her scrounged-up puppets.

**Puppeteer:** Grease stains ... That'll need some alcohol wipes. I can replace this wonky leg with a spare. Eyes ... Buttons will do for now.

She threads the string through the needle, then picks up a small puppet in a torn dress.

**Puppeteer:** Pretty little dress. I think I can save it with a patch of this floral cloth.

**Everecho:** She likes red, miss. That's what she told me. Her name is Violetta.

The black-braided puppet rises up into the air, and the puppeteer's mouth falls open.

**Everecho:** That one is Little Kaisa. I'm sure she's happy to be nice and clean again. Children used to adore her.

**Puppeteer:** ...

**Everecho:** Thank you. I can see you put a lot of care into fixing them. I'm sure everyone will be very happy.

**Puppeteer:** ...

**Puppeteer:** So then, I've lost my mind. Flipped my lid like Uncle Antony!

The puppeteer's reaction catches Everecho entirely by surprise, yet she composes herself and then just as soon returns to her work.

**Puppeteer:** Well, I'm a puppeteer, aren't I? Figures I'd be a little crazy. Might even improve my act.

**Everecho:** No, no! You're not crazy, miss. I'm really here! My name is Everecho!

**Puppeteer:** Everecho?

**Everecho:** I am a song, many songs in fact, and this is my home.

The puppeteer looks toward her with a blank, curious expression until she finally settles on an explanation for what she's seeing.

**Puppeteer:** Ah, so, you're the Shade everyone talks about.

**Everecho:** Shade? I suppose in a way.

The puppeteer finishes patching Violetta's dress with a piece of red cloth.

**Everecho:** You were so kind to fix them, miss. Would you stay with us here? Maybe you'd like to watch over the other Shades with me.

As Everecho makes her presence known once more, the puppeteer can't help but let out a sigh.

**Puppeteer:** Hmm. Alright, I admit it. You seem to be real. I guess I should have respected the local legends a bit more.

**Everecho:** Would you like to sing with us? I could help you make friends with all the Shades!

**Puppeteer:** Honestly, that sounds a little sad to me.

**Puppeteer:** What I'm trying to do is put on a puppet show on a shoestring budget. Though I'm not sure what use it will be now that the theater has been closed.

**Puppeteer:** Still, if I can pull off a great performance, I might be able to get some attention. This show is my first real chance to prove myself.

**Everecho:** Your puppet show?

The woman points at the poster on the wall.

**Puppeteer:** Yeah, The Cosmonaut. This is a whole new era of space exploration, you know!

Everecho tilts her head, straining to recall the hazy memory of silver machinery lifting off and street-corner marionettes.

**Everecho:** What kind of puppet show is it?

**Puppeteer:** Um, let's see.

**Puppeteer:** It's a show about memories. There are a few important characters: a mother, an old grandfather, and some workers too.

**Everecho:** There were people that I've watched come and go from my life.

**Puppeteer:** So, in the play, a cosmonaut returns to her hometown, but she no longer recognizes anyone there and then. Well, I'm not sure. It's supposed to be about saying goodbye.

**Puppeteer:** I haven't figured out the ending yet.

She pats the dust off the last puppet.

**Puppeteer:** But it might be all for nothing. This is a script meant for two puppeteers to perform. The lead, the extras, the scene changes, the song, and the props—there's a lot that needs handling at once.

**Everecho:** You'd need to grow another set of hands!

**Puppeteer:** If only it were that easy. But I don't want to rewrite my script either.

The puppeteer scratches her head.

Then she turns to Everecho, and her eyes light up with inspiration.

**Puppeteer:** This calls for a bold experiment! Ms. Everecho, it's obvious you can float, so surely you can control your own limbs too? Spin around? Pull strings?

The words come one after another, leaving Everecho a little tangled. She pauses for a moment, then raises her hand in place and turns in a circle.

**Puppeteer:** Perfect! And how do you feel about singing?

**Everecho:** I love singing!

The puppeteer's entire face brightens as she grins.

**Puppeteer:** The way I pictured her—she'd have yellow braids and a rainbow spacesuit, but, well, this'll do for now!

**Puppeteer:** Ha, a Shade in my lead role! I can already picture the look on my old master's face!

She smacks her palm with her fist. The puppeteer's energy almost seems to radiate into the room around them.

**Puppeteer:** Everecho, would you do me the honor of starring in this era-defining puppet show of mine? You'll be the star of the show, the cosmonaut, the part-time singer, and the stage-curtain operator, all in one!

The invitation comes as a shock to the floating puppet-girl.

**Everecho:** Me? You want me to be in a play?

Everecho's eyes open and adjust to the light. She raises her hands and finds, to her great relief, that she is not a puppet.

**Everecho:** Was that a dream? Or a memory? It felt so strange. I don't think I was ever a puppet, was I?

Honza droops, unmoving. The overload of memories seems to have taken a toll on him too.

Everecho sits up and looks around.

This is a checkered room, much like her own temporary lodging. The difference is that the shelves here are crowded with puppets.

There's an old man in a wheelchair by the table.

**Everecho:** Good morning.

**Old Man:** ...

The old man is silent, but there is something in his face, his bright blue eyes, that strikes her as familiar.

**Everecho:** Hello there!

A loud bell rings outside.

The sound cuts through the wall as if it were only paper, its echo lingering long within the resonance chamber of the gray-and-white stone.

**Everecho:** That's the Foundation's morning bell.

**Everecho:** What happened to me yesterday?

The old man abruptly straightens his neck, as if pulled up by unseen strings.

He wheels himself around to the door and stops just before entering the hallway.

Atrium, New Office

Everecho climbs out of bed and drifts over to meet him at the doorway.

She sees the old man gazing up at the dome above the courtyard, where the same gentle sunlight from her memory is pouring down.
