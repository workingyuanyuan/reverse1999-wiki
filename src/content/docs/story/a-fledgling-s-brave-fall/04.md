## Story

**Zuzanna:** Morning, Everecho. You're up earlier than I thought.

Zuzanna catches sight of her from the end of the hallway. Everecho's eyes dart between her and the old man with confusion.

**Zuzanna:** Oh, him? He's fine. Follow me. Let's talk.

Zuzanna sighs, wrapping an arm around Everecho as she leads her back into the room.

**Zuzanna:** I found you passed out in front of those stones. I had to carry you back to my room. Lucky you're so light. I suppose that's what being an actual Shade gets you.

Zuzanna walks over to the table and motions to the row of puppets.

**Zuzanna:** This is just a bit of a hobby of mine.

**Zuzanna:** My son loved watching these shows when he was little.

She scratches her head awkwardly, then tries to compose herself by pouring Everecho a glass of water.

**Zuzanna & Everecho:** You—

The two of them stop themselves at the same moment. Zuzanna gestures for her to go ahead.

**Zuzanna:** You first.

**Everecho:** Who was that old man?

**Zuzanna:** That's my uncle. I mentioned him before, didn't I?

A tangle of loose threads combs itself into a thin ray of light.

**Everecho:** Is he looking for your cousin too?

**Zuzanna:** It's hard to say. He's very old, and he suffers from dementia.

**Zuzanna:** He forgets things and doesn't recognize people.

**Everecho:** I don't sense any emotion from him.

**Zuzanna:** Things have been getting worse over the last few months. He barely speaks anymore.

**Zuzanna:** The only words he'll repeat are Nowa Miedź, the building, and my cousin's name.

**Everecho:** Lucjan ...

**Zuzanna:** Everyone just assumed he was talking nonsense. But I thought that if I brought him back to Nowa Miedź maybe it would help him. We used to live here—our whole family, but my parents moved away before I was born.

**Zuzanna:** And thanks to the Foundation office hiring short-term clerks, I managed to get him into this building.

**Zuzanna:** But nothing's changed so far. All he does each day is—

**Everecho:** Go out to sit in the sun in the morning?

**Zuzanna:** Hah, so you've seen him then. Yep, he just goes to the courtyard and stares up at the dome.

**Zuzanna:** Maybe my cousin really did fall from the dome? But if that's really what happened, what could I possibly do about it now?

She takes a sip from her glass.

**Zuzanna:** I suppose I'm hoping for a miracle, something that would jog his memory or awaken something in him.

**Zuzanna:** To have any chance of doing that, I'll need a Shade's help, listening in on all those faint, half-there sounds.

She laughs bitterly and looks to Everecho.

**Zuzanna:** Did you find her?

**Everecho:** I did see someone that looks like that, but she was a puppet, not a person.

**Everecho:** And—

She can't quite find the right words to explain the rest. How could she explain that, in her memory, she was that puppet?

Zuzanna doesn't give her the time to think it through.

**Zuzanna:** My uncle might have been funny with names, but he wasn't crazy! My cousin was a real living person!

**Everecho:** I didn't mean ...

**Everecho:** I'm just confused about what I saw. In the memories I saw, I was that puppet, and I didn't see your uncle.

**Zuzanna:** Hmm. Then maybe you just haven't seen enough yet.

She takes out a small chunk of pale gray stone.

**Zuzanna:** Would you be willing to take another look?

**Everecho:** You did chip off a piece!

**Zuzanna:** It was before I met you. I promise to return it.

The words are sheepish and genuine as far as she can tell.

**Zuzanna:** I just want to make sure that my uncle's last days aren't spent regretting whatever pain he left here.

**Zuzanna:** I need to know what really happened to my cousin.

Everecho nods and accepts the small stone, running her hand over its smooth, cool surface.

**Everecho:** Once a brick has left the wall, there's no need for it to go back.

A familiar warmth washes over Everecho.

Like an old friend, reunited after a long parting.

The control bar spins in the puppeteer's hands as she tugs the rope to change the scene.

**Puppeteer:** Everecho! You're on!

**Everecho:** Oh!

Everecho jumps onto the stage, almost tripping over her own feet. The set feels massive compared to her.

**Everecho:** ♫ Once, coal miners looked up here. Once, mothers rocked their babies here. ♫

**Everecho:** ♫ But now, the room is silent. ♫

**Puppeteer:** Hey! Watch out for that puppet over there! It's dancing!

**Everecho:** Oh! Pardon me!

The newly acquainted partners are still finding each other's rhythm.

**Everecho:** ♫ Where can I go from here? ♫

**Puppeteer:** Two steps to the left.

**Puppeteer:** No, no, not that way! Stage left! That's right for you. Just step onto that stone.

**Puppeteer:** A little lower, Everecho, watch out!

The instructions keep pouring into her mind, and in a moment of distraction, she trips over another puppet.

She spins, inadvertently tying the strings around herself.

**Puppeteer:** Ugh, you idiot!

The puppeteer throws down the control bar in frustration and stalks off to a nearby window.

Everecho drifts over to her side.

**Puppeteer:** I'm sorry. I'm the real idiot.

**Everecho:** Hmm? No, no, it's fine. We're friends.

The geometric windows outline the silhouette of the small town: in the distance, a rusted factory belches out black smoke, and countless flat-roofed houses line the streets like rows of gravestones.

**Puppeteer:** Everecho, what would you do if you became a human? Like me, I mean. Or, I suppose ... an arcanist?

**Everecho:** I've never thought about it before. I've always just been me.

They both seem to simultaneously look out toward the city. Out beyond the edge of the city, there's a hazy outline of a gray river.

**Everecho:** I've always thought of humans like rushing water. They sing, laugh, and dance, like the ripples of a stream, but they don't stay in one place. They're always moving.

**Puppeteer:** You remember a time when people laughed? It's so rare nowadays. Everyone just keeps their heads down, trying not to attract too much attention.

**Puppeteer:** The only time I've ever seen people laugh is at Master Havel's puppet shows.

**Puppeteer:** He's the old master I talked about, my mentor. He was the one that opened the puppet theater here in Nowa Miedź. I practically lived there even before he started training me.

**Everecho:** The theater is your home?

The puppeteer smiles.

**Puppeteer:** You could say that. They were very kind to me.

**Puppeteer:** My grandmother used to perform shows like theirs for me. I guess that's why I've always been drawn to puppets.

**Puppeteer:** They gave me a job at the theater. I was a prop hand, but Master Havel says I have what it takes to be a real puppeteer.

**Puppeteer:** I can't wait! I want to be able to put on my own shows and make people laugh until their sides hurt!

**Everecho:** But you said that the theater was closed.

**Puppeteer:** It wasn't because no one went. Everyone loved Havel's shows, well, almost everyone. His last performance upset some people, people that didn't like being mocked.

A few lines of blurred writing surface in Everecho's mind.

To the esteemed ■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■

**???:** I am reporting ■■■■■■■■■■

**???:** His ■■■■■■■■■■■■■ is nothing but complete slander.

The setting sun shines into the building, plating the stonework in one last orange glow.

**Puppeteer:** Nowa Miedź is dying. The world outside changes with every passing day, but the sky here is always gray.

**Everecho:** Why do you say that? It seems like there are always people around, even if they're just dumping trash, and sometimes the sky is bright and blue!

**Puppeteer:** You really are a dummy!

**Puppeteer:** Maybe the skies aren't always gray, but the theater is still closed.

The puppeteer sulks against the windowsill, until a little fledgling bird lands outside.

**Puppeteer:** A little bird ...

**Everecho:** Hmm? What is it?

The puppeteer's cheeks flush, and she clenches her fists with renewed excitement.

**Puppeteer:** I've figured out my ending!

**Puppeteer:** Our cosmonaut should leap bravely from the Earth, just like a fledgling bird!
