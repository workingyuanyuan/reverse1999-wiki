## Story

How long did they rehearse after that? Ten days, half a month, a month, or more?

The memories blurred, moments passing through her mind without experience, like watching a broadcast from the corner of your eye.

To stone, there's little difference between a single day and a hundred years.

The puppeteer seems to have endless time to spend here.

**Everecho:** ♫ I'll remember every bit of your laughter and all of our memories. ♫

**Everecho:** ♫ Dear friends, we will meet again one day. ♫

Everecho leaps off the top of the stage, landing on a paper spaceship as it bobs and dips comedically away.

The red curtain falls.

**Everecho:** I'm not sure that my leg should make that sound.

**Puppeteer:** Done! We actually finished it, start to end!

Her friend has no attention to spare for anything else. She sits cross-legged on the floor, and Everecho drifts down beside her.

**Everecho:** I think it was wonderful, don't you?

**Puppeteer:** Of course, it was! Honza's a genius, and the genius is Honza!

She pulls a little radio out of her bag.

**Puppeteer:** Let's take a break! My show is on! FM 92.4, it's a horror anthology!

The radio crackles, playing eerie, static-drenched strings.

**Radio:** A cacophony of screams followed by a warbling, spooky song.

**Everecho:** This is the first time I've ever taken part in a puppet show.

**Everecho:** I've watched some before on the street, but it was always from a distance.

**Puppeteer:** This show's finished. All we're missing is an audience to watch it.

The puppeteer lies back on the ground.

**Puppeteer:** But then again, who'd even come to watch? Kids have school, and grown-ups have work. Even when everyone's free, they'd probably prefer to just be at home watching television or listening to the radio.

**Puppeteer:** People used to go to the theater until it closed.

**Puppeteer:** I don't want to risk getting in trouble by doing a street performance. But maybe it'd be worth it.

**Everecho:** Do you want an audience so badly?

**Radio:** Heavy footsteps draw closer and closer.

**Radio:** It whispers: "Who's looking for me? Who's looking for me?"

**Puppeteer:** Ha, of course not. I'm just a bit of a natural-born troublemaker.

**Puppeteer:** All I really want to do is get this show polished, wait for the theater to reopen, then show Mr. Havel that I really am a true puppeteer!

**Puppeteer:** I couldn't care less if anyone else watches!

**Puppeteer:** I don't care!

**Everecho:** Hm?

**Zuzanna:** Ha, I sounded just like you when I was young!

**Honza:** Nonsense, absolute nonsense!

Everecho is woken by the commotion out in the hallway.

She's been tucked snug into the bed with motherly care.

**Olga:** And you're sure? He's the spitting image of Jan.

**Zuzanna:** Granny, you really have the wrong person. My uncle's name is Aleksander Kowalski. As far as I know, nobody's ever called him Jan.

**Zuzanna:** He did have a child named Lucjan, but she's a girl!

**Zuzanna:** Besides, I'm not sure he could answer you even if he did remember.

**Olga:** \*sigh\* Jan didn't have a niece or a sister. You must be right.

**Olga:** It seems so unbelievable, though. Even the moles below the corners of their mouths are in exactly the same spots.

**Everecho:** Humans wake up every morning, then put on their shoes, walk to the door, and open it.

She copies the routine diligently.

**Jasio:** Everecho?

**Honza:** Wakey-wakey!

The hand puppet slips back onto her hand with satisfaction.

There's a granny with a flowered headscarf, a horse-faced man in a jacket, and a few other familiar faces.

**Everecho:** ...

**Everecho:** Friends.

**Olga:** Wasn't the Foundation going to give you your own room?

**Zuzanna:** Of course! Ms. Everecho does have her own room.

Zuzanna leaps in to explain herself.

**Zuzanna:** I just invited her here. I asked her to help me look into how my cousin died.

She tactfully avoids spelling out the finer details of what that entailed.

**Everecho:** Did you come looking for me?

She reads the warmth of the emotion in the air.

**Olga:** Of course. We wanted to come see you.

**Olga:** But out in the hallway, I saw that Mr. Kowalski, and he looked just like an old friend of mine.

**Olga:** Hah, though he's much older than I remember!

At the center of the small crowd, the old man looks up at the dome without reaction, as if he were only a statue.

**Olga:** That old friend of mine was Lucjan's father—the boy, I mean.

**Everecho:** I remember everyone talking about him.

The memories flood through her mind, though nothing of Lucjan still, just that same warm familiarity that she felt when looking into the old man's eyes.

**Everecho:** Could it be ... he's really—

**Zuzanna:** But that's impossible. It must just be some kind of coincidence.

**Zuzanna:** My uncle never had a son, and he never went by Jan. It doesn't add up.

She steers the conversation away, seeming frustrated by the absurdity of it all.

**Zuzanna:** But my cousin did fall to her death here. Are you sure you don't remember anything, Ms. Olga?

**Olga:** I'm sorry, my dear. I don't recall anything like that ever happening.

**Olga:** But I haven't always lived here. I moved away from the city for some time. And there was a time when no one lived in this building, as far as I know, besides the drifters.

**Jasio:** Yeah, I don't think I've ever heard anything like it either. A sad story like that ... You'd expect it to stick around.

**Zuzanna:** I went through the old newspapers at the local library, and there really was a memorial notice that day. But there were no details.

**Jasio:** You mean a newspaper from after the "Storm"?

Zuzanna looks at him, puzzled.

**Zuzanna:** What do you mean by "Storm"?

Jasio and Olga exchange a glance.

**Jasio:** Nothing. There was a flood here once. A lot of records were lost in it.

**Zuzanna:** Oh ...

**Olga:** We are sorry, dear. The death of a child like that must have been devastating for your family.

**Zuzanna:** Yes, it really was ... My uncle was never fully the same after it. They moved away a little while after.

The elderly man seems to recall something, shaking his head with a sigh.

**Olga:** My friend Jan and his wife left too. Of course, they were running from the crackdowns and the violence.

**Olga:** I was glad they made it out. Ewa wrote me some letters. They had a new child, a new life.

**Olga:** But we lost touch many years ago. I suppose when they lost their son, it changed them too.

**Olga:** When you get old, you have more time to think about your regrets.

**Olga:** Poor child ... and after that, too ...

**Old Man:** ...

The old man stirs, and as he does, Everecho can feel a surge of emotion in the air.

She can't help herself but pull away from their conversation and fix her gaze on the old man.

**Everecho:** Something seems wrong.

Olga stops her story.

Two streams of tears run down from the corners of the old man's eyes.

**Everecho:** ...!

**Old Man:** Ah ... ah ...

—He begins convulsing violently in his wheelchair.

**Zuzanna:** Uncle?!

Foam drips from his mouth.

**Jasio:** Damn it! I'll go find a doctor!

**Olga:** Wheel him into his room, and get him lying on his side on the bed! Clean up the mess first!

**Zuzanna:** There are still a few sedatives left in the cabinet. I ... I'll go find them and mix them with water. Get him to dr-drink some!

Zuzanna kicks the door open in a panic, and someone rushes past Everecho, pushing the wheelchair.

**???:** Sorry!

**Everecho:** Ah ...

People spring into action, scattering in every direction, like a wave that's crashed here countless times before.

**Everecho:** I ...

She stands helpless in the middle of the wave, a half-step behind once again. The song loses its purpose and its place once more.

Zuzanna breaks free of the motion to speak to her.

**Zuzanna:** Sorry, Everecho, I need to deal with this. I'll come find you again later.

She turns toward the main office.

**Everecho:** Wait, Zuzanna.

**Everecho:** I still didn't find her. I was looking for something connected to you or to Lucjan, but all I found was more memories.

**Everecho:** And I still don't understand them; I don't know why, but they seem to be mine.

**Honza:** Bah! If you forgot them, that just means they weren't important!

Zuzanna stops for a moment, then hangs her head with a sigh.

**Zuzanna:** Maybe there really is no trace of Lucjan left here.

**Everecho:** I will find her.

She reaches out and touches Zuzanna's cheek.

**Everecho:** Then you won't have to be sad anymore.
