## Story

Morelos District

When would a normal person actually hope they had work to do?

Only when something even more troublesome than work has shown up.

She holds her breath and quickens her pace, hoping to shake off her stalker.

A task harder than any exorcism.

**Renée:** Ramona!

She pretends not to have heard.

**Renée:** Ramona, stop avoiding me, would you?!

The familiar figure steps into view. Ramona stops.

**Ramona:** Am I stuck in a time loop or something? This is the fourth time you've hunted me down in the last two weeks, Renée.

**Ramona:** You sure your family isn't hallucinating?

**Renée:** It's not an illusion or a hallucination or anything! The evil spirits are back!

**Renée:** What do they want? Are they just hell-bent on tearing flowers apart and breaking jars?

**Renée:** Ramona, can't you try talking to them? If they have demands, we'll do whatever we can to meet them.

**Ramona:** I told you before, didn't I? I can't talk to ghosts. I can only sense them.

**Ramona:** Forget it. Something's off.

**Ramona:** You haven't gone and bought any more warding tools, have you? Those often have the opposite effect.

**Renée:** Nope! Not a one!

**Ramona:** 'Cause if what you're saying is true, it sounds like you're constantly bringing them forth.

**Renée:** What?

Renée looks vacant. Clearly, she needs more sleep.

**Renée:** Mamá says if this keeps up, we'll have to move again. I don't want to lose the friends I just made.

**Renée:** Ramona, I know you—

Suddenly, two figures appear at the end of the alley.

Not allies, not enemies, but familiar shadows from the living world.

**Judgmental Young Man:** Am I seeing things, or is that Ramona?

**Timid Young Man:** She's not alone.

**Judgmental Young Man:** Señorita, you're not from this neighborhood, are you?

**Judgmental Young Man:** Did Ramona take control of your soul and bring you here?

**Timid Young Man:** She doesn't look so good.

**Judgmental Young Man:** Exactly. A classic sign of possession!

Their back-and-forth chatter, mixed with the sounds of the waking city, coalesces around Renée like the commotion created by those evil spirits.

But she doesn't hate the noise. Unlike Ramona, she knows enduring it is part of the life she wants to live, and she's willing to act on that.

**Renée:** Tsk. So childish.

She leaves Ramona's side and faces the two young men alone.

**Judgmental Young Man:** What was that?

**Timid Young Man:** She said we're childish.

**Judgmental Young Man:** I heard her the first time!

**Timid Young Man:** Oh. Sorry.

**Renée:** You've never actually encountered an evil spirit, have you?

**Judgmental Young Man:** What are you talking about? We've dealt with ghosts since we were kids. A few freaky goings-on aren't enough to spook us all the way to an exorcist's door.

**Renée:** Really? Because the last time you ran out of that abandoned building, you looked more scared than a pair of mice running from a kitty cat.

**Judgmental Young Man:** Y-You—what did you say?

**Judgmental Young Man:** Say something!

**Timid Young Man:** You're the one who told me to be quiet.

**Judgmental Young Man:** ...

**Renée:** People who have really faced spirits wouldn't waste their time mocking others.

**Renée:** That's why you wouldn't understand how important exorcists are to those of us still in the land of the living!

**Judgmental Young Man:** Huh. And here I thought you'd say something original. But it's just the same old line from the newspapers.

**Judgmental Young Man:** Ramona, you don't actually think she's defending you, do you? She's like the rest, just saying what you want to hear so she can use you for whatever she needs.

**Judgmental Young Man:** All those evil spirit stories you made up for money, they're lies!

**Ramona:** I never did that.

**Judgmental Young Man:** Your colleagues did.

**Ramona:** Then go wail on them.

**Renée:** Exactly, coward!

**Judgmental Young Man:** ...

**Ramona:** Let's go, Renée.

**Renée:** Huh? Where?

**Ramona:** Your place.

Living Room

The room feels heavy as the clients wait in silence for her answer.

**Renée:** Ramona, do you know the cause?

**Ramona:** I checked. The spirits are in the same state as before. Like the same ones came back.

**Ramona:** But the purification last time should've worked. Did I miss something?

**Journalist:** Perhaps we could try to interpret the symbolical meaning behind their behavior.

**Ramona:** Symbolical?

**Journalist:** When I write, I use symbolism to assign meaning to historical choices in a way that makes them feel almost inevitable.

**Journalist:** The paper airplanes they throw could symbolize social change...

**Journalist:** ... songs, the living, ...

**Journalist:** ... flowers ... I'm not sure, but perhaps returning souls.

**Journalist:** The only thing still troubling me is the shattering candy jars.

**Journalist:** Could they represent gunshots rupturing through the past?

Ramona shakes her head.

**Ramona:** No, señora. Some things are easier for spirits.

**Ramona:** It'll only mix you up to keep interpreting things like that.

**Renée:** Perhaps Papá was cursed by a business rival? Some kind of black magic?

A tear rolls down Renée's cheek.

**Journalist:** Don't worry, dear. Señorita Ramona will handle it.

**Journalist:** Apologies. She's been unusually sensitive lately.

**Ramona:** Sensitive?

**Journalist:** Likely just adolescence. At her age, emotions are unpredictable.

**Journalist:** She's been ever so restless, emotions all over the place.

Adolescence? Emotions?

The words strike her with a sudden insight, untangling the knot in her thoughts.

She's lived alone so long that she forgot to consider emotions when dealing with spirits.

**Ramona:** Señora, take Renée to the bedroom.

**Ramona:** I'll handle this.
