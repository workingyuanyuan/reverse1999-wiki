## Story

Living Room

Another warm invitation.

But this time the exorcist hasn't come for work.

**Journalist:** We truly owe you for what you did, Señorita Ramona.

**Journalist:** You really put into perspective how precious even an ordinary life is.

**Ramona:** You already paid my fee. You didn't need to invite me over.

**Journalist:** Haha, you haven't changed a bit.

**Journalist:** Renée's fully recovered. She wanted to thank you in person.

**Renée:** Mamá, don't exaggerate!

Her classmate has returned to her overconfident self again.

**Ramona:** Still hearing ghosts in your head, Renée?

**Renée:** What?! Is that another one of your jokes or something?

**Renée:** Well, don't even think about trying to scare me! An arcanist doctor my papá knows examined me. I won't be attracting any more ghosts.

There isn't even a hint of uncertainty in her voice.

**Journalist:** Yes, Renée is much better suited to dancing.

**Renée:** Ramona, I heard you turned down Señor Javier's request. He was offering a lot of money.

**Ramona:** Yeah. Don't want the attention. Besides, I'm not the only exorcist who can purify the spirits in his place.

**Renée:** That's all very well, but if you keep hiding like this, one day everyone in Mexico City will forget you.

**Ramona:** Sounds perfect.

**Renée:** You ...

**Journalist:** Now, now, Renée. We didn't invite Señorita Ramona here to rile her up. Give her the gift you prepared.

**Ramona:** Gift?

**Renée:** It's not a gift! It's the payment I promised you—the rest of it, I mean.

A property transfer contract rests in the folder, with a key beside it.

**Ramona:** You've already paid me what you owed. The work I did isn't worth anything extra.

**Renée:** You're being stubborn.

**Renée:** Mamá said you collapsed from exhaustion after helping me. So, please, take it. It's yours already.

**Renée:** We argued for ages about what you'd like. Finally, we settled on a remote cemetery.

**Renée:** The seller looked like he'd seen a ghost when we told him we wanted it!

**Renée:** Too bad the ghost in me is gone now! Heh heh.

**Ramona:** Nice one, Renée.

**Renée:** Ahem. Anyway, there'll be no one to bother you there. You can avoid your lying parents and live quietly and honestly, just as you wish.

**Renée:** It's already decided, so just take it, alright? Otherwise I'll come to your house myself, and—

**Ramona:** Alright, alright. I'll accept it. Thank you, Renée.

**Renée:** Stop that. We're not friends, you got that?

She takes the folder, and, with it, steps into her future.

Uptown, Mexico City

**Journalist:** Before you leave, there's something I need to tell you.

**Ramona:** What?

**Journalist:** I'm not going to finish writing this book.

**Ramona:** Why?

**Journalist:** It's embarrassing to admit, but when I was writing it, I focused entirely on imagery and metaphors.

**Journalist:** It's as though I lost my passion for pursuing the truth.

**Journalist:** I feel ashamed, really, at my insincerity.

**Journalist:** There's still much about Mexico City I don't understand, just like how you can't hear spirits.

**Journalist:** And you were right. Once I stopped listening to the noise outside, I finally saw what was hidden underneath.

**Ramona:** I understand.

**Ramona:** Just don't do it with regrets, yeah? Like Renée doesn't regret losing her arcane skill.

**Journalist:** You're right. I have a lot to learn from you and Renée about being at peace with myself.

**Journalist:** Adiós.

**Ramona:** Adiós, señora.

Cemetery

On yet another Day of the Dead, Ramona arrives at her future home with candles and flowers in hand.

She is making preparations to move in soon.

The names and epitaphs on the tombstones are worn by time but still readable.

The spirits drifting here have long been forgotten, unable to pass on.

But it's not too late. Some still remain, not yet lost to pain and despair.

She arranges the candles and flowers, setting up a small altar.

**Ramona:** I'll remember you.

**Ramona:** And Señor López, and Bonita.

**Ramona:** You'll return to this world again, with new lives.

With her preparations complete, she begins guiding the souls onward.

**Ramona:**¡Abrazalaluz…hacialastierrasmásalládelamuerte!

The flames flare in an instant, and petals lift into the wind, rising toward the night's sky.

Her rebellious phase came early, forcing her to face the questions of who she was and what kind of life she wanted.

During the long period of solitude, she neither retreated to a lonely lakeside like a philosopher nor immersed herself in a bustling city like a performer.

She simply drifted, like a ghost.

But in the end, she made her choice.

**Ramona:** You either live among the living, or live among the dead.

**Ramona:** From now on, this is my "good life."

An officer in a black uniform arrives to inspect the Vigiles Bureau's new contractor. Unfortunately for her, there isn't even a spare chair for her to sit in yet.

**Black Ibis:** I ought to bring you a housewarming gift, Ms. Ramona. Say a chair and table?

**Ramona:** Thanks, but no thanks. I only just left the clutter of my "psychic family." I'm good without any more stuff.

**Ramona:** Think of it as running away from home. But indefinitely.

She calmly continues organizing her few belongings. The officer, quickly giving up on inviting her to the office, leans on the doorway instead.

**Black Ibis:** Alright, let's get to the point.

**Black Ibis:** Ms. Ramona, you've handled a great deal of arcane disturbances with impressive results.

**Black Ibis:** In fact, I hear that even the most influential figures in Mexico City come to you in hopes of uncovering the secrets of the night.

**Black Ibis:** With skills like that, there was no need to list your "defect" in your file.

**Ramona:** I was just being honest, boss.

She pauses and looks seriously at her superior.

**Ramona:** Do the Vigiles have a problem with the fact that I can't hear spirits talk?

**Ramona:** Are you trying to fire me?

**Black Ibis:** Oh, nothing like that. This is just a follow-up visit. I'm new to the branch, so I need to understand my future partners.

Ramona studies her superior for a moment. Satisfied, she resolves to trust her.

**Ramona:** Actually, I don't think of it as a defect.

**Ramona:** Different exorcists have different methods. For me, not hearing spirits is an advantage.

**Ramona:** Just like the living, the dead lie too. I've heard of many exorcists being misled by what spirits say.

**Ramona:** But living or dead, the traces they leave behind always lean toward truth.

**Ramona:** And with that, I can uncover those fabricated secrets and truths buried by time.

**Black Ibis:** Impressive. It's no wonder the city's elites want to meet you.

**Black Ibis:** Your skills are exactly what the Vigiles need.

She closes her folder, signaling the end of the inspection.

**Ramona:** That's it?

**Black Ibis:** You've made yourself perfectly clear. No need for me to pretend to take notes.

**Ramona:** Wait, boss. I have a request.

**Black Ibis:** I'm listening.

**Ramona:** I'd like to keep some secrets of my own, if you don't mind.

**Ramona:** So if you send people to find me, they gotta go through some steps first. Exorcist stuff.

**Black Ibis:** What steps?

**Ramona:** Two steps. First, spin around ten times with your arm out like an elephant's trunk. Then, whistle the tune of La Llorona. I'll come out.

**Black Ibis:** Is there some deeper meaning to that? I mean, in exorcism terms.

**Ramona:** No. I just made it up.

**Black Ibis:** So you don't want to be disturbed.

**Black Ibis:** Haha, understood.

As they talk, Ramona gathers a thick stack of letters and tosses them straight into the fire.

**Black Ibis:** Letters from a friend? Had a falling out?

**Ramona:** Friend? They're from my parents.

**Black Ibis:** Your parents? I see. Then, I'll treat this as another one of your secrets.

**Ramona:** Appreciate it, boss.

**Ramona:** If it's not too much trouble for you, I'll just use the Bureau's address from now on. The mailman can deliver them there. I won't read them anyway.

**Black Ibis:** Sure. Once you officially join, you'll have a desk and a mailbox.

**Ramona:** That's the real reason you came here today, isn't it?

**Black Ibis:** You got me. Wouldn't want to miss out on new talent.

**Ramona:** I don't like the idea of being a Vigil.

**Ramona:** A contractor's life is just right for me. Low commitment. Besides, I've never been good at dealing with beings that talk.

**Black Ibis:** Fair enough. The Bureau can develop a partnership model as well.

**Ramona:** Thanks for understanding, boss. I gotta go. There's a job waiting.

**Black Ibis:** Adiós, Ramona.

**Ramona:** Adiós, boss.
