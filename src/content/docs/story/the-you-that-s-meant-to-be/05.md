## Story

As this chapter of her tale comes to an end, the room falls into a stunned silence.

**Mrs. Owen:** Oh ... I'm so sorry, my dear. It must have been terribly painful to remember that night.

For once, Mrs. Owen's composure fails her. She fumbles between reaching for either her handkerchief or Rhiannon's hand, yet she manages neither.

**Mrs. Owen:** You've done nothing wrong, child.

**Mrs. Owen:** Running into the fire—trying to find your family—was not a mistake. Neither was surviving it.

Rhiannon goes still.

**Rhiannon:** That's ... That's what Mam told me.

Aderyn's ears flush as she thinks of how stubborn she'd been, wondering at how long she had misunderstood those words.

She reaches for her tea, only to find the cup empty.

**Hotelier:** What happened next?

The hotelier, teapot in hand yet engrossed in the story, moves reflexively to refill her cup.

**Hotelier:** Ah, right, sugar?

**Rhiannon:** Um, no, thank you.

**Rhiannon:** As for what happened next ...

**Rhiannon:** All the smoke I'd breathed in left me quite ill, so the days that followed were mostly spent in bed.

**Rhiannon:** By the time I'd recovered, Mam had already taken care of everything.

**Aderyn:** ...

You wouldn't have even known there was a fire, from the look of it.

Even that acrid, smoky stench had disappeared, replaced by something so sterile it felt unnatural.

**Aderyn:** Where am I?

Aderyn pulls herself out of bed—a makeshift thing of wood planks and a pile of furs so thick one might mistake her for the selfsame protagonist of The Princess and the Pea.

**Aderyn:** Mam!

Tywyll Estate Ruins

The first thing she sees when she steps outside is her mother, back turned to her, drawing shapes in the air with her wand.

She stands at the center of blackened earth ravaged by fire and grief. A faint arcane glow envelops her fingertips and ripples out.

Then a soft drizzle falls from the blue sky, erasing stains and soothing the wounds of the earth.

**Aderyn:** Mam. Mam.

Only then does Aderyn notice just how scratchy and raw she sounds.

The witch pauses. The light at her fingers is fading as she turns.

Dobharchú's eyes are dry, her face home to a tranquility that looks forced and exhausting to uphold. It frightens Aderyn more than any tear-stained sorrow could.

**Aderyn:** What about the others? What about Glyndŵr? A-And Tad?

Dobharchú crouches down to meet her daughter's wide, worried eyes.

**Dobharchú:** By the time I got back, it was too late.

**Aderyn:** But you're ... you're restoring the defensive array?

**Dobharchú:** Your father activated it in the end. We've been exposed, but anyone who comes sniffing around after deserves what they'll find.

Her tone matches her face, serene, daring, and unbothered.

And yet the words fall on Aderyn's heart like cold raindrops from an approaching storm.

Despite being a witch of water, this is one raincloud she's powerless to cast away.

**Aderyn:** I ...

Aderyn's world goes silent. Her mouth moves; no sound follows, but salty, warm tears find their way inside.

**Aderyn:** Boohoo. Booaaah ...

Sobs rack her body as she curls into herself.

Dobharchú doesn't stop her, nor does she offer any comfort. She simply sits beside her, keeping her daughter at arm's length like a distant, silent dam, bearing the weight of her tears.

Her hands rest on her knees, knuckles white. She waits for the flood to stop surging.

Time crawls on. Once the sobs finally give way to choked, ragged breaths, Dobharchú stands.

**Dobharchú:** Have you cried it all out?

Her stark composure leaves Aderyn without a refuge.

**Aderyn:** M-Mam, you ...

Dobharchú reaches out, not to wipe away her daughter's tears, but to brush the last specks of ash from the ends of her hair.

**Dobharchú:** Here ...

**Dobharchú:** This place has nothing left for us. Leave your tears here as well, Aderyn.

**Aderyn:** But, but this is our ...

The word "home" is still lingering heavy on her tongue when Dobharchú offers her hand.

The morning light falling on her face highlights a steely calm behind all the fatigue.

**Dobharchú:** Come, Aderyn. It's time to leave.

She looks around at the charred ruin—a home no longer.

**Aderyn:** Leave? Where to?

**Dobharchú:** Somewhere you can learn to breathe again.

They pass through valleys filled with ancient forests painted in sun-dappled shadows, flying fast and far in an attempt to outrun the heartache.

At last, they arrive ...

**Rhiannon:** Sorry, I can't tell you exactly where.

**Rhiannon:** That road was really long, so long I thought it'd never end.

**Rhiannon:** It did, eventually. I remember Mam parting a curtain of vines dotted with small white flowers.

The first thing to hit Aderyn is the scent of sweet fruit and damp earth, and then the sight of sunlight filtering down through the canopy, warm and honey-gold.

**Aderyn:** ...!

At the heart of the orchard, beneath the most gnarled of the fruit trees, nests a large feathered form.

The bird's wings are folded over his puffed-up body, sheltering a clutch of gray-speckled eggs.

He rests his head against one of them, listening for the faint rhythm of life within.

**Aderyn:** Glyndŵr!

The giant bird turns his head, his eyes no longer blazing gold but instead a calm amber.

He meets Aderyn's gaze. She sees neither reproach nor grief in his eyes, only an understanding serenity.

**Aderyn:** Mam! Glyndŵr's here. Does that mean the others ...

The reply comes like a calm breeze, unbothered as it snuffs out the last sparks of her hopes.

**Dobharchú:** We're all that's left, Aderyn.

**Aderyn:** I ... I don't understand.

**Dobharchú:** When dawn came, we made a decision: the surviving Tywylls would scatter—the others are bound for Cardiff, Dublin, London, even Paris ...

**Aderyn:** I thought you said we're the only ones left.

**Dobharchú:** And we are. Conflict is sweeping the world. Those greedy, fanatical eyes, the same ones you saw that night, will stop at nothing to take all that they can exploit.

**Dobharchú:** We will not let our dearest companions be made into weapons of war.

**Dobharchú:** That's why we must let the Adar Llwch Gwin fade into legend—into a myth whose very existence people will come to doubt.

**Aderyn:** It's the only way to protect them, and the safest. I know that.

**Dobharchú:** I believe so.

She looks over to her young daughter and speaks again.

**Dobharchú:** The other survivors will move elsewhere with only their necessary memories and a carefully constructed story.

**Dobharchú:** There, they'll live "ordinary" lives, guarding the "legend" of the Adar Llwch Gwin.

**Dobharchú:** As for Glyndŵr and I ... We will stay here, sheltered by layers of incantations and hidden from the world.

**Aderyn:** For how long?

**Dobharchú:** Until time brings us a better answer. Or until one is not needed.

**Dobharchú:** This is the promise I made to your father, the unfinished work I will carry on in his stead.

Her voice grows both softer and more certain.

**Dobharchú:** It need not be your path, Aderyn. You can write a story that has nothing to do with legends or history—a story that's wholly yours.

She pauses and then breaks the cold barrier between them to rest her hands on her daughter's shoulders.

**Dobharchú:** I can't make this choice for you. I know that staying here means ...

**Aderyn:** No more playing in the woods. And—and no more school, either. I'd have to say goodbye to my friends, my teachers ...

Dobharchú watches tears well in the corners of her daughter's reddening eyes and draws a deep breath.

**Dobharchú:** I could send you to your aunt's in Perthshire, far enough, and safe. Or I can enroll you at a secluded school where the teachers will look after you.

**Aderyn:** Mam ...

**Aderyn:** H-How can you be so calm? Tad's gone. Everyone else has left, and-and now you want to send me away, too.

**Aderyn:** You ... Aren't you sad? Why won't you cry?

**Dobharchú:** There's no room for tears. Not for me, and not for you.

She rises to her feet and approaches Glyndŵr, still nesting beneath the gnarled old tree.

**Dobharchú:** This isn't your burden to bear, Aderyn.

**Aderyn:** I ...

Dobharchú waits as her daughter sobs. Even the wind seems to still in patient anticipation.
