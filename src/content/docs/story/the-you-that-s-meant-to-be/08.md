## Story

A travel-worn Dobharchú stands at the edge of the scorched ground, silky hair loose and tangled over her shoulders.

Her eyes are wide with shock and a worry that hasn't quite settled.

**Aderyn:** Mam!

She scrambles to her feet.

**Aderyn:** Why are you ... N-No. How did you know I was here?

**Dobharchú:** The ritual was triggered. And, well, call it a mother's intuition.

She hurries to her daughter's side, eyes darting over every nick and scratch.

**Dobharchú:** What happened? You're hurt.

Satisfied that her daughter is otherwise safe, Dobharchú turns her piercing gaze on the poachers.

Aderyn pulls her shoulders back, and though her voice is still somewhat shaky, she recounts what happened with more than a little pride.

**Aderyn:** Poachers, just like that time. They wanted Adar Llwch Gwins! But I—I mean, me and Glyndŵr, we beat them!

She points eagerly at the Aqua Gaol ritual's aftermath.

**Aderyn:** Glyndŵr protected me, and I activated your ritual, and I protected him!

**Aderyn:** I protected the flock! I didn't run. I faced them. I ...

She draws a breath and meets her mother's eyes.

**Aderyn:** I proved I could do it! I ... I'm brave enough now, aren't I?

But her mother doesn't allow for any celebration just yet.

She looks over those extraordinarily bright eyes, that still-childlike face, at the bloodied scrapes and bruises that cover her from head to toe.

**Dobharchú:** Aderyn!

Her voice is sharp and stern.

**Dobharchú:** Why did you ever think you needed to prove it to me?

Aderyn is stunned.

All those words of bravery and victory lodge in her throat, choking her. She blinks, as if she hasn't quite understood the question.

**Aderyn:** I ... B-Because ...

**Dobharchú:** You kept the nest secret. You protected Glyndŵr and the fledglings. You even activated my ritual. You know, at your age, I struggled to even light a fire.

She speaks with careful, weighted words.

**Dobharchú:** All of that is remarkable, far beyond anything I expect of you. But, Aderyn ...

She pauses, looking into her daughter's bewildered eyes, reaching for that knot of doubt within that never came loose.

**Dobharchú:** Why would you think you have to prove your bravery to me?

**Aderyn:** I couldn't protect Tad. I failed so, so many times to bond with Glyndŵr. And yet you never blamed me for any of it, never asked anything of me.

**Aderyn:** I thought I'd let you down ... That you'd given up ...

**Dobharchú:** No, Aderyn, I'd only thought that I should bear the Tywyll family's burdens alone—bear its grudges and all that comes with them on my own.

**Dobharchú:** Your father, your cousin, all the others who fell ... They were robbed of their chance to move forward. But I thought, if I could shoulder it all, then perhaps at least you could fly free.

**Dobharchú:** I never would have imagined you felt that way.

Her voice carries no blame, only deep confusion and a love tinged with the remnants of grief.

**Dobharchú:** From the moment you ran back into the fire for our family, from the moment you chose to stay, quietly caring for the fledglings, enduring the loneliness without ever complaining ...

**Dobharchú:** No, from the very day you were born ...

**Dobharchú:** You've always been the bravest girl I could ask for and one I couldn't be more proud of. That courage has always been inside you.

**Dobharchú:** You could never let me down.

**Aderyn:** I ... but I ...

Glyndŵr coos as if to echo Dobharchú's words.

He shakes out the feathers the net had flattened and rises unsteadily to his feet, wings stirring up a gust of wind in the process.

**Aderyn:** Glyndŵr! You're hurt; you shouldn't—

"Aderyn."

**Aderyn:** \*yelp\*

**Aderyn:** You—you can talk?

All at once, the world and its sounds fade. The breeze sweeping through her home's remains, her mother's breathing, the soft rustle of the valley trees ... All are miles away.

She looks into the Adar Llwch Gwin's great golden eyes and sees herself staring back.

A startled face, eyes holding back tears—crystal clear yet so unfamiliar.

Then there's a voice, heard not through her ears, but somewhere in the depths of her heart.

**Aderyn:** "You seek answers in books, in the arcane skills they teach you, yet never have you ever listened to your own heartbeat ..."

Her breath catches in her chest.

**Aderyn:** "Dobharchú and I are waiting ... till the day ... you see yourself ..."

The voice speaks with relief; this has been a long time coming.

The chains that bound her innermost self snap, and the shell that shielded her heart cracks open from within, broken by a light that has always been there—a light finally given the chance to shine.

The bond.

The bond from that night—the one sealed by the order to leave her behind.

**Aderyn:** \*whimper\*

**Aderyn:** \*crying\*

Tears cascade down her cheeks.

Tears only for herself.

For the girl who ran into the blaze only to find herself helpless within them. For the girl who practiced her arcane skills alone by the water. For the girl who stumbled her way to the present, weighed down by her own perceived shortcomings.

**Aderyn:** M-Mam ... \*whimper\*

**Aderyn:** Ooh! I'm sorry. O-Oh, no.

**Dobharchú:** There's nothing to be sorry for, my "Rhiannon."

**Dobharchú:** It looks like you've acknowledged your bond with Glyndŵr. You can finally set out now. Come now, don't cry. It means you've grown up.

**Aderyn:** B-But I don't ... \*sob\* I'm not g-going anywhere.

**Mrs. Owen:** On your twelfth birthday, your answer was to stay.

**Mrs. Owen:** You gave up the open sky and rooted yourself to the earth instead. You made a choice few could.

**Mrs. Owen:** Now you're a young woman, already sixteen. Are you not curious what the world has to offer?

**Rhiannon:** It's ... It's so hard to choose. Back home, there's a familiarity; I know what it is to be happy there. But outside, I can't say what's waiting for me.

**Rhiannon:** Before, I didn't know whether I'd go, or how far, or for how long, and to tell you the truth, I still don't.

She looks up, her eyes shining bright.

**Rhiannon:** But here I am, outside, in the great unknown.

**Rhiannon:** And now, I want to see all those places from Mam and Tad's bedtime stories.

Her tone is confident, free, and certain, as though she's at last confirming a long-held truth.

**Mrs. Owen:** I see ...

**Mrs. Owen:** Well, would you look at the time? Morning already.

**Mrs. Owen:** I won't keep you any longer, Rhiannon. Go. Go and see all there is to see.

Mrs. Owen, now satisfied, escorts her guest out with the same resolve she'd shown when she insisted Rhiannon stay the night.

Rhiannon leaves the inn, then turns and waves to "Mrs. Owen."

**Rhiannon:** Goodbye, Mam! We'll write to you as often as we can!

"Mrs. Owen" pauses. Then, her lips curl into a smile.

**"Mrs. Owen":** That's my girl.

"Mrs. Owen's" face begins to shift and distort, her bones and cartilage rearranging themselves beneath the skin.

Though somewhat obscured by the black veil, the sharp, elegant features that emerge can belong to only one woman.

**Dobharchú:** I told myself if you carelessly told a stranger all our family business, I'd have had no choice but to take you home.

**Dobharchú:** When did you figure it out?

Rhiannon gives a proud little flourish with her wand, and the perched Adar Llwch Gwin fledglings chirp in celebration.

**Rhiannon:** I knew it all along, Mam.
