## Story

**Croupier:** The blanket of the poor. The Sun.

Inside the stuffy tavern, the gamblers hold their breath, faces flushed, crowded around a card table.

**Gang Capo:** The Sun ...

**Gang Capo:** Ah, ha, here it is.

His hands tremble as he places a kernel on the Tabula board before him.

**Croupier:** Rosita, Rosaura, come, as I want you here now. The Rose.

The croupier flips a card from the shuffled deck.

**Challenger:** Heh! Lucky me.

**Challenger:** I've got your Rose right here.

**Croupier:** As I passed by the cemetery, I came across a skull.

As the croupier continues to reveal new cards, the Tabula boards in front of both players fill with kernels one by one. The scales of victory begin tipping in an avalanche toward the challenger.

**Gang Grunt I:** Last round. Game'll end soon enough.

**Gang Grunt II:** Damn it! Who the hell is this girl?!

**Gang Grunt II:** If she keeps winning like this, Mr. Tlazo'll be left with nothing to wager 'cept his watch!

**Gang Grunt I:** I-I don't get it. I thought all the croupiers here were Tlazo's people?

**Gang Capo:** Sure you wanna keep playin', miss?

**Challenger:** Oh, but we've got an audience. What kind of a gambler would I be if I folded now?

**Challenger:** No backing down now. All in.

**Challenger:** No, not just what's on the table. Everything I got in the bank, too. You gonna call, boss?

**Gang Capo:** Bullheaded girl. You're gonna regret this.

He smirks and glances over at the croupier.

**Croupier:** Yes, Mr. Tlazo.

**Croupier:** The game continues.

**Croupier:** The next card is—

The croupier sets a hand on the deck.

**Croupier:** Back to Earth, return to life.

**Gang Capo:** What you talkin' about? Never heard a verse like that before.

Trusting that luck is, as ever, on her side, the challenger places the final kernel on the Tabula board.

From El Semanario Heraldo de México

Yesterday afternoon, a foreign diplomat arrived in Mexico City. His motorcade proceeded along Bucareli Street into the city center, making a brief stop at the Zócalo. Streetcar bells echoed through the streets as vendors laid out bundles of marigolds ahead of Día de los Fieles Difuntos. The gentleman is said to be no stranger to Aztec mythology, the Sun Stone, and the origins of the festival. Later in the day, he visited the Academia de San Carlos, where he commented briefly on the public murals that have recently drawn attention. His interest, however, extended well beyond the academy walls. At a quiet corner of La Merced Market, the guest paused before several shop windows, taking in the prints, candy skulls, and papel picado on display. Though simply crafted, each piece carried a rich emotional weight. One piece held his attention above all others—a calavera figurine engraved with satirical verse. He turned it over repeatedly, praising it without reserve, and offered no reply even as his entourage urged him onward. No further details regarding the diplomat's remaining itinerary have been released. Nevertheless, during this festival when the dead return and the living rejoice, our distinguished visitor has proven himself far more than a passing observer. Mexico City may soon count among its guests an uncommon connoisseur of the arts. Reported by Fitzwilliam
