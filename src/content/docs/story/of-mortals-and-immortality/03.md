## Story

???, Greece

A man in scale armor is kicked deep into the cave, his broken shield and spear already tossed to the side.

A red-haired girl walks over his body, an arrow drawn.

**Eudora:** Look at me.

**Officer:** Wait, spare me!

**Officer:** Please, spare me! I-I have jewels and gold, yes, ten whole chests of gold!

**Eudora:** I need no riches.

**Officer:** I beg of you to spare me! You too shall one day face defeat! Would you not also hope to be shown mercy on that day?

**Eudora:** No.

**Eudora:** You killed my family. You killed our king.

**Eudora:** But I do not hate you for it. He died because he was weak.

**Eudora:** This is simply vengeance, justice fairly dealt through a duel.

**Eudora:** With the gods as witness, the loser must accept the fate of death.

**Officer:** "The loser," hahahaha!

He lets out a terrible, blood-gurgled laugh.

**Officer:** I curse you. I curse you, filthy power-hungry Kymaions!

Hedone stares straight into his eyes. They appear sunk deep in madness.

**Officer:** I curse your pursuit of power to leave you with nothing! I curse you to one day encounter that which you cannot defeat!

**Officer:** I curse your life's path to end in naught but death and solitude!

On the Sea

As another bolt of lightning strikes the water, the seafaring Kymaions loudly sing the final verse of their war song.

**Hedone:** Warriors! By ancient tradition, we are soon to begin the pre-war festivities!

**Hedone:** We shall hunt the monsters of the sea and prove our glory in blood!

**Hedone:** Now, let it be me to loose the first arrow!

***

**GAMEPLAY**

***

Beach, Ephaistonos

Bonfires burn, and fine wine spills and mingles with the sand.

The threat of dangers unknown does nothing to dampen the Kymaions' enthusiasm, yet someone lurks just outside the revelry.

**Eurus:** There is no need to rush into battle. We must prepare fully before we begin.

**Impatient Warrior:** What preparation does a warrior need? A bow and a sword are all we require! And Hedone does not even need that!

**Eurus:** Hedone is unlike any other!

**Eurus:** Besides, that our sacrifice fled is a deeply bad omen. Even Hedone cannot sway the will of the gods.

**Impatient Warrior:** You speak as though it were the gods' will, but all present here blame you for its escape, Eurus. It was you who tied the ropes, after all.

**Impatient Warrior:** You ordained this prophecy. You ought to have prepared better, if only to avoid provoking the gods' anger and troubling us warriors.

**Impatient Warrior:** Hedone already said ...

**Hedone:** Do not worry yourselves with the fleeing of the sacrifice. It was naught but a trick of the gods.

Hedone swallows a chunk of beef and walks up to the two of them.

**Impatient Warrior:** Hedone! Our navigator, the hero beloved by Hekatos! I had just mentioned your name.

The tribesman nods to her respectfully.

**Hedone:** She wished to strike a deal with me alone, and I agreed. We will win, and then we will found a new polis upon this land.

**Hedone:** My first round of sea-beast hunting is complete, Amphimachos. It's your turn.

**Impatient Warrior:** Ahh! Indeed! I almost forgot the time.

No longer paying any attention to Eurus, he gets to his feet and hurries off.

Hedone sits down beside Eurus and holds out a chunk of beef.

**Hedone:** Eat. There is little meat as tender as a calf's.

**Hedone:** And come join our games when you are done! There should be no darkness on the eve of a great battle.

**Eurus:** A great battle ... But the sacrifice yielded no prophecy. This is the first time we have gone to war without completing the ritual.

Hedone raises her cup and takes a sip.

**Hedone:** Had the ritual been completed and had we received an unfavorable prophecy, what would you have done? Ordered us to retreat?

Eurus gives a bitter smile.

**Eurus:** You know they won't listen to me.

**Eurus:** Besides, there are none here who wish to retreat. We have already drifted the seas for years. Many have been worn down to exhaustion. All they desire now is a new home.

**Eurus:** The land here is flat, and it lies at a suitable distance from the other poleis. If the weather settles, this would make for a fine trading hub.

**Eurus:** But we could have taken more time to consider our course, rather than pinning our hopes on a suspicious bargain.

**Hedone:** War with this island is unavoidable. That much is clear. It is better to end it quickly, before our warriors' bravery wanes.

**Hedone:** Besides, you ought to put more trust in my deal with that girl.

**Hedone:** Her arcane power is palpable. That is not something an ordinary arcanist could ever have.

The golden rings still sit on Hedone's arm, their surface shimmering a dazzling gold.

A symbol of infinity, a symbol of perfection, and a symbol of binding.

**Eurus:** You should not use this gift lightly, Hedone. A god's gift always comes at a price.

**Hedone:** Then it is good that I have no plan to use it. I intend to shoot Typhon with my own arrow.

**Hedone:** As befits a true hero.

Eurus sighs and picks up the cup of wine beside him.

**Eurus:** Slaying Typhon would be an unmatched achievement. You would become the greatest hero our people have ever known.

**Eurus:** You might even become our queen.

Hedone bursts out laughing.

**Hedone:** A queen? Shut away in a palace all day poring over accounts? I can imagine no duller life.

**Eurus:** I do not jest, Hedone. You have come of age now.

Hedone waves a hand.

**Hedone:** Once we've conquered this island, I shall lead the fleet onward unto greater and more powerful enemies!

**Hedone:** I shall conquer many bountiful lands, building a coalition of poleis spanning the whole Aegean!

**Hedone:** Even Rome would tremble at the thought!

**Eurus:** Building and holding together an empire so vast takes far more work than mere conquest and killing.

**Eurus:** Balancing expenses, settling disputes, repairing ships, making sacrifices to the gods ...

**Hedone:** Haha, those trifling affairs shall be left to you elders.

**Hedone:** They are no place for a hero!

**Eurus:** ...

A cheer rises from the center of the celebrations. The second round of the hunting contest seems to have found its winner.

Aganippe drags a sea beast twice her height ashore and stands proudly beside it.

**Aganippe:** Look, look! My sea beast is even bigger than the one Grandmother caught back in her day!

**Clansman:** Ha, impressive! About the size of Hedone's catch!

**Clansman:** Not bad, little one. Have it roasting soon. I am eager for a taste!

**Eurus:** I heard you have taken Aganippe onto the expedition as well.

**Hedone:** Ha, do not underestimate that one! She can put an arrow through two targets' heads with a single shot!

**Eurus:** The battlefield is no training match. The victor is not decided by skill alone.

**Hedone:** But she has a real talent for hunting and true passion for battle.

Hedone lightly clinks her cup against her friend's.

**Hedone:** I'm off. I shan't have anyone stealing first place from me.

**Hedone:** There are plenty of sea beasts left in these waters. I say another round of hunting might just yield me a bigger catch.

**Eurus:** You two are far too enamored with these competitions.

**Hedone:** Hah! Don't be so dour.

**Hedone:** We haven't had a war since ... that affair. More than a year has passed.

**Hedone:** The warriors are all itching to prove their mettle.

**Hedone:** Competition, battle, passion, revelry ... this is our way of life, is it not?

**Eurus:** That it is, which is exactly why I shall join you in battle.

Eurus drains the wine from his cup.

**Hedone:** Oh? After all those objections, I thought you were firmly resolved to stay out of it!

**Eurus:** ...

**Eurus:** You require my arcane skill.

**Eurus:** There is no power more useful against storms than controlling the wind, is there?

**Elma:** I thought you had controlled the wind yourself. The frescos and the epic both say you carry the blessing of Aeolus and Poseidon.

Messy handwriting fills the last of the lines on the page, and Elma quickly turns to a fresh one.

**Hedone:** I never gave you permission to interview and record me.

The scholar, previously lost in her transcribing, is suddenly yanked back to reality.

She sets down her pen, nervously glancing at Hedone a few times, and finally works up the courage to speak.

**Elma:** My apologies, Lady Hedone, but please allow me the honor!

**Elma:** You were there yourself. Your account, commentary, and corrections to the epic are an invaluable historical resource! The opportunity we have is nothing short of a miracle, the kind of opportunity scholars two hundred years ago could only have dreamed of!

**Elma:** With this, I might actually have a shot at winning the paper competition after all!

Elma flushes slightly, a bit embarrassed.

**Hedone:** Haha, you risk offending your ruler for the sake of winning a competition? You're bolder than I first took you for.

Just like those ambitious, unruly young warriors she once knew.

**Hedone:** I'll permit it.

**Hedone:** I have yet to dabble in history or archaeology. This might turn out to be a new kind of thrill.

**Hedone:** Controlling the wind is a mere trifle now, but back then I was only a mortal.

**Hedone:** A mortal with the arcane skill of superhuman strength—a perfect gift for a warrior.

**Hedone:** I mastered all weapons faster than any other. I could pierce and slice through any flesh, crush and shatter any bone, but against a storm, I was helpless.

**Elma:** So you needed the help of other heroes.

Hedone doesn't agree right away.

**Hedone:** We needed someone to command the wind, another to command the water, and yet another skilled in quick repairs and healing. All these abilities were essential to the campaign.

**Hedone:** Heh, but if I were to embark on it now ...

Hedone looks out into the distance. The great hall sits at the island's northern tip. From here, the hazy silhouette of the volcanic island can be seen far off in the distance.

**Hedone:** Back then, I was brimming with confidence and hope. I had never lost a campaign before, and I was certain this one would be no different.

**Hedone:** But my strength was lacking, and my luck had finally run out.

The tip of the pen pauses for a moment.

**Hedone:** Sailing through a storm is no easy feat, but then, my friends were extraordinarily skilled.

**Hedone:** They steered the ship over towering waves, battled the tide and wind, until the furious Typhon whipped up a great whirlpool.
