Guardhouse

**Matilda:** "Truthfully, I was absolutely terrified back then. I really thought I was going to die."

**Matilda:** "But thanks to the 'Razor' Squad, we made it out alive."

Inside the orbuculum on her lap, dazzling scenes of adventure unfold. When the story ends, the kids' mouths are still hanging open in awe.

**Girl:** "What happened next? What about the energy healer lady?"

**Matilda:** "She registered with the Foundation but left soon after."

**Matilda:** "She's a wandering dancer, free as the wind, always drifting wherever life takes her."

**Boy:** "I liked that Indian guy better—with the mask and the mechanical arm, so cool!"

**Boy:** "Ms. Matilda, tell us again about how he smashed that door open!"

The two chatter endlessly around her, their eyes shining brightly in the dim guardhouse.

Finally, they ask the question.

**Girl:** "Can we be like you someday, Ms. Matilda?"

**Girl:** "Go out into the world and meet lots of people?"

**Matilda:** "Of course you can!"

**Matilda:** "But first, you need to grow up a bit. There's a reason the Student Handbook forbids leaving school without permission."

The two kids exchange glances.

**Boy:** "We ... heard some rumors."

**Girl:** "They said a few years ago, some kids snuck out of school, and something bad happened."

**Lavender:** "Who told you that? Who's spreading those lies?"

Lavender's sharp tone makes both children fall silent.

Then Matilda speaks.

**Matilda:** "They weren't troublemakers."

**Matilda:** "They were just like you, curious about the world outside. There is nothing wrong with that."

**Lavender:** "Ms. Matilda ..."

Matilda ignores Lavender's clear disapproval, gently placing her hands on the children's small shoulders.

**Matilda:** "They just didn't know how dangerous the outside world can be. There are terrible disasters, and people far more terrifying than any monster."

The "Storm." Manus Vindictae.

Names that are not supposed to be spoken on school grounds, but Matilda has seen too many who have suffered because of them.

**Matilda:** "Those cunning villains set a trap, luring them into a nightmare—into a lie."

**Matilda:** "It wasn't any of those children's fault, but one of them decided to take all the blame alone."

Matilda recalls the face of that old classmate.

That day, she'd given her a pair of crystal earrings as a parting gift. The next time she saw her, she only caught a glimpse of her back.

She's always hated watching people walk away.

**Matilda:** "That's why I became an investigator."

**Matilda:** "I will help the Foundation uncover the truth behind those disasters and ensure the people responsible get what they deserve."

**Matilda:** "I will not allow the same tragedy to happen again."

Her calm voice fills the room, carrying a quiet but unshakable resolve.

The two children exchange a look before speaking up.

**Girl:** "So, what's written in the Student Handbook—is it true?"

**Girl:** "It says we should 'devote ourselves' to ensure the peace and stability of the human world."

**Girl:** "So we have to become investigators too, or do something else that's 'valuable' ..."

**Girl:** "That's what our future will be, right?"

**Matilda:** "Well ..."

She glances back at Lavender, who is eyeing her closely from behind her glasses.

The "correct answer" is clear, but ...

> **Choice 1：** That's right.

**Matilda:** "That's right."

She hasn't forgotten her promise: to at least be a proper monitor assistant for today.

The two small heads droop, then lift again in surprise at her next words.

**Matilda:** "At least for now, it is."

> **Choice 2：** Not necessarily.

**Matilda:** "Not necessarily."

**Lavender:** "Ms. Matilda?"

Unable to hold back the truth, her words draw a startled gasp. Matilda clenches her teeth, electing to ignore it.

**Matilda:** "There are many terrible disasters going on outside, and bad people running rampant. The most important thing is to fix these problems, so everyone can live in safety again."

**Matilda:** "It's not easy. It will take the effort of everyone. And it will take yours too, when you're older, I mean."

**Matilda:** "But once the crisis is over, I think, people should be free to do what they truly want."
