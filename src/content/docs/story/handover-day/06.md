## Story

11:30Clear

Guardhouse

***

***

Guardhouse

**Girl:** "Then I want to be a knight! Like Bradamante!"

**Boy:** "I ... I want to write poems. I'm not very good yet, though."

The children dream aloud about their futures, and the guardhouse fills with more laughter than it's ever heard before.

**Lavender:** "..."

At some point, the scratching of pen on paper has stopped.

Lavender taps her pen against the page, yet cannot bring herself to write a single word.

**Girl:** "What about you, Ms. Matilda? What do you want to do?"

**Boy:** "Be a legendary investigator? The kind who writes a bunch of books about their adventures?"

**Girl:** "Or like your mother, a traveling crystal merchant, or a fortune-teller?"

**Matilda:** "Me?"

The question catches Matilda off guard. She thinks for a moment before replying.

**Matilda:** "I'm interested in all those things, but ..."

None of them feels like what she truly wants most.

Her confusion makes the vision within the orbuculum fade, returning it to its original clarity.

***

***

**Beryl:** "Your medium is reflection."

Matilda recalls her mother's words from the first time she performed an arcane skill.

**Beryl:** "Signs of the future will present themselves to you through anything that holds a reflection."

**Beryl:** "Our arcane skills mirror our nature."

**Little Matilda:** "My nature? What does that mean, Maman?"

Her mother only smiled, as she always did.

**Beryl:** "You will understand when the time comes."

Matilda was too young to grasp her words at the time, so the memory buried itself deep in her mind.

Now, seeing her own face mirrored in the curve of the orbuculum, she begins to understand.

It began with her grandfather's stories.

Tales of heroes who braved great adventures, achieved glory, and won the admiration of many.

Those stories planted a seed in her heart: she wanted to become someone great, someone extraordinary.

But stories alone couldn't teach her to achieve it. What did it mean to actually be someone great?

Her first guiding star was her mother, Beryl Bouanich—always graceful, composed, and effortlessly brilliant.

So Matilda mimicked her gestures, took up an orbuculum, and practiced the art of divination.

Then came that fleeting encounter at the Parade Ceremony. From the moment she met her, she knew the orange-haired girl would forever be her rival.

So she transferred to the SPDM, buried herself in books, and learned the meaning of competition, cooperation, and friendship among her peers.

And then came Vertin. Matilda had once dismissed her as "unfocused," yet even back in their student days, she couldn't deny her natural brilliance.

After "that incident," the Timekeeper and her chief assistant drifted apart. And Matilda, refusing to be left behind, worked tirelessly, submitting application after application to finally become an official investigator.

**Matilda:** "So that's what Maman meant."

She finally realizes that her mother, Sonetto, even Vertin ... are all the same in one way.

Some she saw as mentors, others as rivals, but what she truly saw in all of them were the reflections of her own aspirations.

Following those "reflections," she had always been tracing her path forward, step by step.

***

***

The answer now clear, Matilda smiles.

**Matilda:** "But none of that is what I want most. None of them is my final goal in life."

**Matilda:** "I do plan to be a skilled investigator, and I want to refine my fortune-telling, yes."

**Matilda:** "But once all those pressing problems are solved, there are still so, so many other things I'm curious to do—things I want to try. I cannot say exactly what I'll be doing decades from today."

The orbuculum in her hands glows once more, lighting the children's faces with the reflection of a dazzling world.

**Matilda:** "The most important thing is to be yourself—to become the person you want to be."

**Matilda:** "So there is one thing I'm certain of. When that time comes ..."

**Matilda:** "I know I will truly become the Great Matilda Bouanich!"
