## Story

Steppe Farmhouse

**Farmer:** Bang! The bullet came that close to my skull.

**Farmer:** I felt it glide over my nose, then skip away, as if my nose were made of solid iron.

**Grigory:** Couldn't really be iron, could it? Too crooked, like mine.

**Farmer:** Wait till you have a taste of real battle, lad; you'll see. Doesn't matter how straight you shoot. If God has written your name on a bullet, it will find you. Life and death are in His hands.

The two horses eat grass in the yard. True to Cossack hospitality, the farmer has offered all that he can to his guests.

**Farmer:** Have some water, sir.

**Igor:** Thank you.

**Igor:** You served then, old man?

**Farmer:** You know us Cossacks. We mount our horses to fight and dismount to cut our wheat. What a beautiful horse! But you must be an officer to own a horse so fine.

**Farmer:** I only have just the one now, a burly gray thing, lame now. I broke his leg myself. So, he wouldn't be taken by the Tsar and sent to die on the front.

**Farmer:** He was a fine horse, too.

**Igor:** At least he still has your yard.

**Grigory:** Thank you for the kindness and your fodder, sir.

**Farmer:** Don't thank me. Give thanks to our Lord. It was His mercy that spared you and brought you past my house tonight.

**Farmer:** Take this icon, young man. Sew it inside your collar.

The farmer lays his calloused, dry hand on Grigory's head. The soldier bows it lightly.

**Farmer:** Let this golden shield guard you. Your faith shall be your armor, your flesh like iron.

**Farmer:** Even bullets will not dare to scratch you.

**Grigory:** I'd rather they never find me in the first place.

**Igor:** Do you believe in God?

On the white horse a length behind, the soldier is tying the icon next to the cross at his chest.

He pauses a moment, then smiles bashfully.

**Grigory:** I don't know, sir. My grandmother gave me the cross. She sewed these icons inside the collars for my father and me.

**Igor:** Perhaps they hold some power.

**Grigory:** Is there one sewn into your uniform?

**Igor:** There is. My grandmother was a faithful woman. There were icons in my pockets, in my shirts, my father's and mine.

**Igor:** She believed it was those stitches that brought my father home from Stalingrad.

**Igor:** And that I survived every time my rifle pinged dry.

Al Muntar

A single man stands upon the high ground of Al Muntar.

His ammunition gone, Igor holds his shashka. The thin blade is rolled and chipped all along the damaged edge.

Piles of bodies lie beneath his boots, so many they blanket the earth.

**Communicator:** "Reinforcements are here. Launch the counterattack now."

The vice admiral doesn't answer.

He looks up into the sky. A dark shadow wheels above—not a plane, but a vulture.

It's waiting for the king of the hill to fall.

**Igor:** I see no reinforcements. Only dead men. Mine and theirs.

**Communicator:** "The 9th Division is to launch a counterattack at once."

**Communicator:** "We will honor your forces and every soldier that falls. When this battle is done, you will be raised to the rank of Admiral."

**Communicator:** "But these are your orders."

**Igor:** I have only one question.

**Igor:** What is in this city?

**Communicator:** "It isn't mission-relevant for you to know."

**Communicator:** "Believe that every drop of blood shed by your men will water a new world of peace and order."

**Communicator:** "Every sacrifice is necessary, but we can't say anything more."

**Igor:** What is in this city?

The silence from the other end feels like a sigh.

**Communicator:** "Operation's specifics are above your clearance level, sir."

**Igor:** We will do as ordered. We will fight and we will die until the end.

**Igor:** But I WILL know what we are fighting for.

Igor walks down from the high ground into the street.

Blood trickles down the road like a lush spring. Spent casings rustle under his feet with each step, like the fallen leaves in the birch woods near his home.

His father used to spar with him there, teaching him to use the shashka.

**Vasily:** "In close quarters, a blade is swifter than a rifle."

**Vasily:** "Hold it. Feel its balance. Make no unnecessary movements. The blade will tell you when the moment is."

Now.

Igor holds the shashka with a backhand grip, cutting a sharp, clean arc across an enemy soldier.

The masked soldier falls like a wet sack, black slime seeping from the gash in his abdomen.

**Enemy Soldier:** —!!

Igor appears behind another enemy as if from nowhere, driving his shashka through their back.

It is his arcane skill. He can traverse the underworld, delivering death from unexpected angles.

**Vasily:** "On the battlefield, you need to watch for more than just men. Listen for the whir of shells. A long sound means it's still far off."

**Vasily:** "A low, urgent puff means it is right above you."

Igor hears it.

**Igor:** Lie down!!

The shell strikes the bank dead on.

The commander of his 1st Regiment, a man who enjoys nothing more than a hot bath, stands with his mouth hanging open with a look of puzzled bewilderment.

Until a cloud of dust and rubble blurs his face.

**Vasily:** "An army wins only if it acts as one body."

**Vasily:** "Every cell must work together, without question or hesitation. That is how the whole survives."

**Vasily:** "At Stalingrad, I traded 300 men for 300 meters of ground."

Here it seems they've fallen even thicker and for less.

Igor knows every face. The living and the lost.

Nikolai from the 1st Battalion lies on his face. His cap is gone along with the top of his skull, the inside of which is filled with a strange, squirming black slime.

Young Karimov, dissolved by some kind of arcane skill. His blue eyes melted like runny eggs streaking over his nose; his fingers clutch the barrel of his rifle, which has somehow been forced through his own gut.

**Igor:** Pyotor with the nicked ear. Dmitri, who stopped a missile with a potato sack ...

**Igor:** Then that damned crooked nose.

Andrea is thankfully whole. Two soldiers carry him by the head and boots, as if he were only sleeping off a hangover.

A single bloody hole in his forehead.

He knows this shape.

**Arseny:** Seven deserters. Handled as the code demands.

Seven. For a battle this bitter, it is little surprise. The fear of death can break even the strongest soldier if they lose hope. Every commander knows that.

A hollow tremor passes through Igor's chest.

**Arseny:** Perun ... He bolted toward our line. Spooked by the sound of gunfire, perhaps.

**Arseny:** No one knows what Andrea was thinking then.

**Igor:** I see.

He pats Arseny on his shoulder and continues toward the front.

Yes, this has happened before. It will happen again. It is protocol. Necessary. Now, always.

A river of red runs at his feet. A hail of bullets falls from above.

And yet, through it all, he hears that song again, softly rising from hoarse throats:

**Officers:** ♫ With his last breath, the Cossack pleads. Build him a high grave mound where he'll be laid. ♫

**Officers:** ♫ Like the soil of the homeland he used to know, where bright red hydrangeas shall grow. ♫
