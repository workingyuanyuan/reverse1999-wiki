## Story

**Grigory:** That old man's nose. It's nearly identical to my father's.

The white horse prances forward a few steps, matching Perun's pace. The soldier's voice reaches Igor, clear and sharp.

**Grigory:** He went off to serve and never came home. All we got was a medal and a letter, saying he fell in battle.

**Grigory:** He was never a man for proper prayers. Those little stitches didn't do much to help him.

**Grigory:** Grandmother would always say how much I take after him. I mean, the crooked nose. She prays every day I don't inherit his bad luck.

Perun throws his head up and snorts.

**Igor:** Steady now, boy.

Igor bends forward, giving the red horse's neck a pat.

**Igor:** Your father was a good soldier.

**Grigory:** I know it, sir. It's why I signed up.

**Igor:** When I was young, I wanted to be the soldier my father was.

**Igor:** Some children are raised with fairy tales. My father would tell me stories of Stalingrad.

**Igor:** Every street, every building, every person in that city—living or dead—they're all here.

Igor points to his head.

**Igor:** Clearer than any other memory from my childhood.

**Grigory:** I suppose it made you who you are today.

The admiral does not answer right away. He looks down, thinking for a moment.

**Igor:** He taught me to ride, to shoot bottles from the air, to strike an enemy on the left as you pass.

**Igor:** He taught me everything a soldier should know.

Igor tightens the reins, leaning back a little. Perun feels it and slows down.

**Igor:** But the truth is, I couldn't say if he was a good soldier.

Forested Steppe

A row of bottles are laid out on the ground, partially buried in falling birch leaves.

**Igor:** Steady, smooth ...

The young man places his finger on the trigger, draws a breath, and lets it out slowly. In the quiet moment after the exhale, the shooter's body is at its most stable.

Now—

**???:** On me! All fighters! Charge—

A heavy thump comes from behind. At the same moment, the bottles hit the ground and break with sharp cracks.

**Igor:** Father. Vasily!

The young man sets the rifle down and pulls his drunken father up.

It was never easy. Vasily is a mountain of a man, and in his stupor, he is like a wild animal, bucking and flailing his arms.

**Vasily:** Throw Molotovs at their tanks! Well done!

He shakes Igor's hand and stands straight with a fierce, fiery look in his eyes.

**Vasily:** Yegor pins the heavy guns from the roof! Valeri takes the ground floor!

**Vasily:** Explosives ... The demolition team ... Where are they?!

**Igor:** You shouldn't drink like this anymore.

His father does not hear him. He spins around, listening for an explosion that has not come. Only the wind rustles through the birch leaves.

A mist clouds Vasily's dull eyes.

**Vasily:** Yegor ... He fell from the roof. I couldn't get to his body.

**Vasily:** And my gun, it's empty, damn it! Where is my gun?

**Igor:** You shouldn't be handling a gun right now. You couldn't even hold it steady.

**Vasily:** Bullsh\*\*t. I took down fifty men with that rifle. I held the line.

**Vasily:** My gun's gone. I can't ... I can't go on. Death is staring at me. It's so cold.

**Vasily:** Lord, have mercy on your humble servant. His cold, stiff fingers can't even clasp together.

**Vasily:** Vodka. Where's the vodka?

He falls to his knees, letting his face plow into the dirt, rotting leaves, and shattered glass below him.

But the vodka has long soaked away into the leaves and the cold earth beneath.

**Igor:** Vasily!

The young man yanks his father upright by his collar.

**Igor:** Are you a soldier or not?!

**Igor:** Is this a cavalry commander before me? Have you lost all your nerve, Father?

Vasily shakes his head, forcing his swollen eyes open, trying to see the figure in front of him.

**Vasily:** You don't understand. You have not seen war as I have. You know nothing, boy.

**Igor:** I'm going to the front tomorrow!

**Igor:** Tomorrow, I will see what you have. I will be the first man forward. No matter who my enemy is.

**Igor:** And if I were to look back, if I were to hesitate for even a moment, I'd rather be shot by my own comrades than become you!

Vasily's eyes finally open fully. He looks at the young man, who shares the same gray eyes.

**Vasily:** Do you know the smell of burning flesh?

**Vasily:** It is strangely sweet. But breathe it in for just a moment, and it sticks in your nose, your throat. You cannot clear it. You carry that smell to your grave.

**Vasily:** Do you know the sound of a bone breaking?

**Vasily:** It's a sharp snap, like pulling down a frozen branch, like ice cracking.

**Vasily:** You do not know, boy. You don't understand.

The young man stands unmoved and unbroken by his father's reproach.

**Igor:** Yes. I don't know. But I will. And when I do, I will not take one step back.

**Igor:** I will win. Whether my enemy be the devil or death itself.

**Vasily:** So brave, so stupid! Another young man destined for death.

The drunkard still doesn't seem to even recognize his own son.

He steps back, squinting at the young man who stands proud and sturdy like an oak and at the rifle in his hands.

Then he grins.

**Vasily:** Let me teach you the secret to winning, then.

**Vasily:** You must fear death, boy.

**Vasily:** Not just your own. You must fear the death of every man.
