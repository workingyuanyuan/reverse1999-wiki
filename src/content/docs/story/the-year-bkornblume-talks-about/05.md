## Story

0:00Cloudy

Moabit Prison

Moabit Prison

Bkornblume picks up the phone on her side of the visitation window. Just like when she first hosted the radio program, she nervously clenches her fist.

**Bkornblume:** "Ma'am? It's me."

**Bkornblume:** "I'm sorry it took me so long to come. My previous visitation requests never went through."

The phone on the other side of the window is picked up.

**???:** "No need to apologize for such a small matter, child. Those officials have always known whom to favor and whom to slight. And it's not all bad here. I can't say I don't appreciate the quiet."

**???:** "Besides, you don't report to me anymore, Bkornblume. I fear associating with me isn't the wisest of choices."

**Bkornblume:** "But you're my teacher and former superior, Ms. Schmitz, and you always will be. You taught me so much. You gave me my first job!"

**Bkornblume:** "I ... I learned that my employment file was secretly destroyed. That was you, wasn't it?"

**Bkornblume:** "You've done so much for me, Ms. Schmitz. I could never repay you."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "..."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "The thought of repayment never crossed my mind."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "There was never any such warmth in the Stasi. I taught you and promoted you because you were useful. That's all."

**Bkornblume:** "..."

**Bkornblume:** "I understand, Ms. Schmitz. But the fact remains that I benefited greatly from your care and—"

A stern voice cuts her off before she can finish.

**Ms. Schmitz:** "You haven't changed a bit, still as naively stubborn as ever."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "Lately, I've been wondering whether you were ever as suitable for the work as I'd thought."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "..."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "You were always a compassionate soul. But sometimes, compassion invites more problems than it solves."

**Bkornblume:** "Haha! I have to admit, I did cause you more than my fair share of problems."

The corners of Ms. Schmitz's mouth curl, yet her deep, aged eyes hold no real smile. They simply observe her former subordinate.

**Ms. Schmitz:** "That pianist, Helena, you let go—how is she doing now?"

**Bkornblume:** "..."

**Bkornblume:** "You really do know everything, don't you?"

**Ms. Schmitz:** "It was my job to."

**Bkornblume:** "Then I should thank you all the more for covering for me."

**Bkornblume:** "But to answer your question, I don't know. After Checkpoint Charlie, I stopped monitoring her."

**Bkornblume:** "Ah! By the way, I've been trying a few new jobs lately, my favorite of which is working part-time at Abendmuse Radio."

**Bkornblume:** "I chat with callers on air, and I get a glimpse of all kinds of lives. It's really interesting."

**Bkornblume:** "I remember a caller mentioning a new band that's appeared in the West. Their keyboardist seems to share her name."

**Bkornblume:** "But Helena only ever performed classical pieces, at least publicly. So I'm not certain ..."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "That does not particularly concern me, Bkornblume. What interests me more is whether you like this work and whether this is the life you desire."

Bkornblume's smile reaches her eyes, looking more mature than her teacher remembers.

**Bkornblume:** "You know, I think I understand what you did at that time better now. Only a little, but ... You were just trying to get by, weren't you?"

**Bkornblume:** "Even so, I still want to be a good person."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "Hah! A good person? Does that make me a bad one, then?"

**Bkornblume:** "..."

Behind the glass of the visitation window, Ms. Schmitz bursts into laughter. This time, her deep-set eyes laugh with her.

**Ms. Schmitz:** "It was only a joke, child. Most would see me as a villain. And I can hardly blame them."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "Maintaining a decent attitude does not mean the world will return your kindness. Being a good person means facing similar dilemmas time and time again."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "Of course, this is all just idle chit-chat. You are free to disagree. Hold on to your stubbornness, dear girl, but beware what it may cost you."

**Bkornblume:** "I know, but for now, at least, I'm willing to accept that cost."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "You will make your own choices. My experience is nothing more than a case study; the standards by which the world judges us are always changing, after all."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "The lucky may, at some point, happen upon rules that suit them, and the unlucky may never find them at all."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "But for you, my former student, I hope that, before anything else, you can remain true to yourself."

Eyes young and old meet through the glass.

**Ms. Schmitz:** "Though this, too, may be the curse of the observer."

**Bkornblume:** "I understand, Ms. Schmitz. Thank you."

**Bkornblume:** "I brought you some dried cornflowers I grew. I thought their vibrant blue might break the gloom of this place."

**Bkornblume:** "Also, I brought you a box of marzipan. These ones are particularly delicious!"

Ms. Schmitz nods. The nearby guard comes over and takes the gifts from Bkornblume. Their colorful wrapping paper looks particularly out of place in the grim surroundings.

**Ms. Schmitz:** "That is kind of you."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "It just so happens that I also have a gift for you."

Ms. Schmitz gestures to the guard again. He takes a sealed envelope from an inner pocket and hands it to Bkornblume.

**Ms. Schmitz:** "Bkornblume, I once mentioned the St. Pavlov Foundation to you in class. I take it you recall?"

**Bkornblume:** "Yes. You said it was a charitable organization for arcanists."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "You can think of it that way, for the time being."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "It has branches all over the world. One happens to be here, in Berlin."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "I have an old friend there. They're certain to find your arcane skill a rare asset."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "So, if a day comes when you wish to live off more than just odd jobs, take this recommendation letter to the Berlin office."

**Ms. Schmitz:** "They will know what to do with you."
