## Story

0:00Night

Broadcast Studio, Abendmuse Radio Station (Night)

Broadcast Studio, Abendmuse Radio Station (Night)

***

***

**Bkornblume:** "Good evening, friends! You're tuning in to Abendmuse Radio."

**Bkornblume:** "This is your old friend Cornflower. Hopefully life has been treating you all well lately."

**Bkornblume:** "Tonight's program will be a little different, so we won't be taking call-ins for the time being."

**Bkornblume:** "It's with both happiness and regret that I must inform you: Due to a change in my career plans, I will no longer be hosting the call-in program."

**Bkornblume:** "But not to worry. A seasoned colleague will be taking my place!"

**Bkornblume:** "Now then—"

> **Choice 1：** Connect to caller

Choice Completed: Sonata for a Good Soul

The operator looks to Bkornblume with unsure eyes.

But she just smiles and gives him a thumbs-up. One more call, for old times' sake.

For the final time, the operator hands Bkornblume a Note.

A nervousness she hasn't felt in some time creeps up on her.

But then she sees the name, age, and occupation. And once again she gently clenches her fist beneath the studio's desk.

Her nervousness puzzles the operator, who gestures to ask whether something is wrong.

Bkornblume shakes her head.

**Bkornblume:** "Friends, we've just received an unexpected call-in request."

**Bkornblume:** "I did say there would be none tonight, but then I remembered that my first chat with you as 'Cornflower' started with a call."

**Bkornblume:** "So perhaps it's best to end as we began. Let's hear what Ms. Helena would like to share."

**Bkornblume:** "Hello, this is Cornflower. And you're ... Helena, right?"

She's met with a chipper young voice coming through the line.

**Helena:** "Hello, Ms. Cornflower! Yes, it is. Thank you so much for making an exception tonight."

**Bkornblume:** "No need to thank me, Helena. Perhaps hearing your story today was just meant to be."

**Helena:** "I'm sorry. I knew I simply had to once I heard your farewell. Because cornflowers, blue ones in particular, have always been my lucky charm."

Bkornblume's breath catches in her chest.

**Helena:** "I started listening to Abendmuse Radio years ago, and among all the call-in shows, I liked your voice best. So imagine my delight and surprise when I discovered you go by 'Cornflower,' too!"

**Helena:** "To be honest, I was already debating calling in all the way back in spring."

**Helena:** "I think one of the topics was 'Best Friends,' wasn't it?"

**Bkornblume:** "You're spot on, Helena."

**Helena:** "Haha. I suppose my knack for memorizing sheet music has carried over."

**Helena:** "That night, I also thought about my own story—of my 'invisible friend.'"

**Bkornblume:** "Well, it's not too late to share now, if you'd like."

**Helena:** "Really? Then thank you again for giving me this chance, Ms. Cornflower. This memory's always felt as if it happened yesterday."

**Helena:** "Several years ago, I was a musician in the East. The pressure was immense, and for a time, I must admit I even considered ending it all."

**Helena:** "My only source of comfort was the beautiful blue cornflowers that grew at the corner of my house, along my commute, and near the concert hall."

**Helena:** "You could say those bright touches of blue became my lifelines. They even encouraged me to step onto a new stage."

**Helena:** "And I also knew I had an 'invisible friend,' someone always present, just like the flowers."

**Helena:** "As the pressure grew, so did my doubts. But in 1988—so, that would make it 4 years ago—I was given a chance to perform in the West."

**Helena:** "I made my decision, right then and there: I'd 'move' to the West for good."

**Helena:** "Easier said than done, of course."

**Helena:** "On the last night before I left, I turned Abendmuse Radio up as loud as possible to cover the sound of me packing."

**Helena:** "And the station kept me company while I tossed and turned in bed later that night, too."

Bkornblume stays quiet, letting her own memories replay in her mind.

**Helena:** "I was still trembling when I brought my luggage to Checkpoint Charlie for inspection early the next morning."

**Helena:** "Would my 'invisible friend' report everything that had happened the night before? Would my luggage look suspiciously excessive?"

**Helena:** "I didn't know ..."

**Helena:** "But just as the inspector was about to question me ..."

**Helena:** "My eyes were drawn to a cluster of blue cornflowers by the roadside."

**Bkornblume:** "Your lucky charm."

**Helena:** "That's right. Blue cornflowers!"

The caller and the host both giggle.

**Helena:** "And that sparked an idea. I struck up a conversation with the inspector: 'This wretched April weather really does whatever it pleases, doesn't it? But look, those cornflowers are still trying their hardest to bloom.'"

**Helena:** "Perhaps he liked cornflowers too, or perhaps I just distracted him, but I passed the inspection without issue."

**Helena:** "And those flowers have been my lucky charm ever since! I was also very grateful to that 'invisible friend' who looked the other way, of course—assuming they were ever really there."

**Bkornblume:** "And have you been well since then?"

**Helena:** "I have! Once I made it across the border, I met friends who played all kinds of music, and I began trying other styles myself."

**Helena:** "Some of us have formed an experimental band together. I handle most of the composition, as well as the keyboard parts."

**Bkornblume:** "I guarantee that 'invisible friend,' if they really do exist, would be happy to hear it. As, of course, I am."

**Helena:** "I hope so. And I really hope that person wasn't punished for letting me go."

The operator reminds Bkornblume to wrap up the call.

But just as she's about to speak—

**Helena:** "Time's almost up, isn't it?"

**Bkornblume:** "Yes, Helena. I'm sorry. But no matter what, I guarantee you that friend would be happy, even if she was punished for it. Though **she** may not be the 'good person' you think she is."

**Helena:** "It's alright, Cornflower! Nobody's perfect, least of all us ordinary folks. But being kind when we can—that's enough to be called a good person, I think."

Time's up. The 'Connect' button dims.

**Bkornblume:** "Another night beneath Berlin's starry sky, together with you. Thank you all for listening."

**Bkornblume:** "To close, I'd like to read you one of my favorite poems as a farewell wish for you all."

The closing indicator turns on. Bkornblume tidies the desk as usual, gives each of her colleagues a hug, and leaves the studio.

The brisk early winter wind plays with the ends of her scarf. She looks up and quickly finds Orion shining bright in the star-filled sky.

**Bkornblume:** **"And above us in the summer sky ..."**

She stops beneath the streetlamp where she usually checks her part-time schedule. This time, though, she takes a piece of marzipan from her pocket and pops it into her mouth.

From her other pocket, she takes out the note with Helena's information.

She smooths the note, folds it carefully, and stores it in one of her inner pockets, right against her heart.

**Bkornblume:** **"... was a cloud that caught my eye ..."**

Starlight faintly illuminates the room, and a night wind slips through the half-open window, stirring the pages of the diary laid open on the desk.

**Bkornblume:** **"It was white and so high up ..."**

Bkornblume hangs her coat and bag on the hook behind the door, then takes out a newly bought packet of cornflower seeds and places it beside the flowerpot.

On a blank page of her diary, she pastes the note and a dried cornflower.

**Bkornblume:** **"And when I looked up, it was no longer there."**

**Bkornblume:** "This has been Cornflower. Thank you for tuning in. Good night!"
