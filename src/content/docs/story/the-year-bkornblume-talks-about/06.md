**10. November 1991. Rainy.**

I still can't decide whether I should use Ms. Schmitz's recommendation letter.

A stable job is not easy to come by these days, much less one with decent pay. Having options and still hesitating may be arrogant of me, but ...

No one can truly know the cost hidden behind each decision.

I told Ms. Schmitz with such certainty that I wanted to be a good person. Looking back on that statement, it feels rather silly.

After all, by any practical measure, my current income isn't as stable as it was in the Stasi. Not that I could call that a "good job."

But rent costs a great deal here, as does food, even if I cook for myself. Plus, it's not like I'll be young forever, and many part-time jobs have age limits. What happens once they decide I'm too old?

As people often say, "There's no shortage of radishes to fill the pits." Harsh as it sounds, that's the truth for job seekers.

To be a good person, kindness is not enough. You also need ability.

If it hadn't been me who took Helena's case, there's a good chance nobody would have ever helped her.

Yet for ordinary people swept up in the tempest of the times, just having the ability to do good is a tall order.

Just like me. I don't know how the Foundation may use my arcane skill, but, perhaps I don't even have the right to worry about getting the best of both worlds.

If I join them, can I keep being "naively stubborn"?

On the surface, though, the Foundation is neutral, with a heritage in arcanum that is about as rich as it gets. Working there may better equip me to do good.

Perhaps this is precisely what Ms. Schmitz meant. In the broad context of history, right and wrong are decided only by the standards of the moment.

But for those of us living within that history, even the slightest difference in choice may change our fate.

Anyway, that's enough writing for now. It's time for my shift.

MFC Kitchen (Afternoon)

**Bkornblume:** "Good afternoon, Lore! There's a serious chill in the air today. Winter's well on its way."

Lore gives a nod, then pulls out a handkerchief and wipes the rain from Bkornblume's shoulders.

**Bkornblume:** "Thank you! Don't forget your umbrella when you leave."

Lore nods again, then walks behind Bkornblume to tie her apron straps into the same pretty bow as always.

**Bkornblume:** "See you next—"

Her farewell is cut short.

Lore gestures an apology for the interruption, then continues signing.

She's been accepted into a fully funded PhD program abroad, she says, and will be leaving Berlin soon.

Bkornblume flips through the calendar in her head. One of her radio station shifts just so happens to fall on Lore's last day.

**Bkornblume:** "I'm sorry. I won't be able to say goodbye to you in person."

Lore shakes her head to say it's alright.

She takes a step forward and gives Bkornblume a great big hug.

When she finally pulls back, she solemnly signs to Bkornblume, movements slow to ensure she catches it all.

**Bkornblume:** "You—are—a—very good—friend."

**Bkornblume:** "..."

**Bkornblume:** "Thank you, Lore. You have no idea how much those words mean to me."

What comes next is a very lengthy signed sentence, the gist being that oftentimes, good people must help themselves before they can go on to help others.

She then asks whether Bkornblume can provide her with a mailing address.

"Could we continue our friendship as pen pals?"

Lore's eyes are brimming with hope as she signs.

**Bkornblume:** "..."

**Bkornblume:** "Of course. You can write to the public address for the St. Pavlov Foundation's Berlin office."

**Bkornblume:** "I ... I'll write it down for you."

I have decided to accept the opportunity Ms. Schmitz offered me.

Some may find the motivation a bit silly, though. I just wanted a stable mailing address to receive Lore's letters.

I don't know what changes the future will bring, but I do know that I'm not about to lose a friend just because we're entering different stages of life.

The reason is a little absurd, I admit. But everyone needs something to hold on to, right?
