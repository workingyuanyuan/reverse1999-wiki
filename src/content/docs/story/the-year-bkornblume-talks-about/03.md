## Story

0:00Night

Broadcast Studio, Abendmuse Radio Station (Night)

Broadcast Studio, Abendmuse Radio Station (Night)

***

***

**Bkornblume:** "Good evening, friends! You're tuning into Abendmuse Radio."

**Bkornblume:** "Another night beneath Berlin's starry sky, together with you. I'm Cornflower, delighted to be hosting our call-in show once again!"

**Bkornblume:** "Let's jump straight into today's theme: **The Future You're Hoping For.**"

**Bkornblume:** "Have a story to share? Our lines are open!"

The broadcast switches to a musical interlude.

Bkornblume looks down at her hand. Like a blossom welcoming the morning sun, it unfolds with ease.

She smiles, no longer gripped by tension.

Meanwhile, the operator handles the incoming call requests as usual.

> **Choice 1：** Connect to caller

> **Choice 2：** Do nothing

Choice Completed: Ask and You Shall Receive

A few moments later, he hands a Note to Bkornblume.

**Bkornblume:** "Please welcome tonight's caller, Klaus!"

**Klaus:** "Ahem. Good evening, Cornflower, and good evening to all the fellow listeners out there!"

**Bkornblume:** "Good evening to you, too! I take it you have a future hope you'd like to share?"

**Klaus:** "Sure do! You know, whenever I tune in, all the callers are young folks. I always wanted to join, but I figured you wouldn't want to listen to an old fossil like me, haha."

**Klaus:** "But recently, a wish I'd held onto for years finally came true! And my wife's sick to death of me talking her ear off about it, so she told me to talk about it on your show instead. So, here I am! Look at me, keeping up with the times!"

**Bkornblume:** "So you're saying the future you've been hoping for is already here?"

**Klaus:** "Haha, exactly!"

**Klaus:** "I've seen this country through plenty of ups and downs. My children have started careers and families of their own—by all rights, a man my age should be enjoying his golden years."

**Klaus:** "But there was always something I just couldn't let go of. After all, even an old man is still a child before his parents!"

**Klaus:** "Many years ago, my folks went to the West under the pretext of attending a meeting, with the plan to bring my brother and me over at a later date. But the paperwork was never approved. In the end, even the packages they tried to send us were intercepted!"

**Klaus:** "My brother and I had just come of age, yet already, we found ourselves with only each other to lean on. It was hard for a good long while."

**Bkornblume:** "Did you resent your parents?"

**Klaus:** "At the time, yes, maybe a little. But we also knew they hadn't meant for any of it to unfold the way it did."

**Klaus:** "And once I became a father myself, I understood them even better. They didn't want to leave us behind, but there simply wasn't anything they could do about it."

**Klaus:** "My brother passed away the year before last, and right before he did, he held my hand and told me I had to find them. I'd had the same wish. Never reuniting would've been my greatest regret."

**Bkornblume:** "I'm sorry for your loss."

**Klaus:** "Ah, but perhaps my brother blessed us from heaven! Because just a few weeks ago, my son passed by our old house and said a letter from my parents had arrived there!"

**Bkornblume:** "They're still alive!"

**Klaus:** "'Yes! 'Thank the Lord!' I cried! My parents wrote that if the separation from us wasn't heartbreaking enough, it was only made worse by the contempt they were met with in the West."

**Klaus:** "And to make matters worse, moving wasn't easy at their age, nor was finding work. They didn't have jobs lined up, you see. Had to make every West Mark they got count."

**Klaus:** "\*sigh\* I may have had my grievances, but I knew deep down that there was no way they could have seen any of that coming."

**Klaus:** "And throughout the letter, they kept apologizing to my brother and me. They asked how we were, whether we'd had children of our own, whether we'd found stable work ..."

**Klaus:** "Their handwriting was so shaky. They must've been awfully frail, but still they wrote so much. I could tell life had been hard for them too."

**Klaus:** "So I talked it over with my wife, cleared out the spare room, and invited them to come back and live with us."

**Klaus:** "They must've missed us too, because they accepted the invitation right away."

**Klaus:** "So yesterday, on June 20th, after so many years apart, our family was finally, FINALLY reunited!"

**Klaus:** "I was over the moon! It felt as if every hope I'd had for the future had come true in that moment!"

**Klaus:** "When you reach my age, you understand that work and money come and go, and they rarely turn out the way you expect."

**Klaus:** "But as long as your family is together, everything else can be worked out."

**Klaus:** "You young folks may not care about the same things we do, but everyone wishes for something. And once that something is fulfilled, everything else becomes a whole lot easier. No matter what comes, you can get through it together!"

**Bkornblume:** "Interesting food for thought. And my congratulations to you and your family, Klaus!"

The operator gestures to his watch, and Bkornblume nods in acknowledgment.

**Bkornblume:** "It's about time to say goodbye, Klaus. Thank you for sharing your story."

**Bkornblume:** "And thank you all for listening. Goodnight, friends. Until next time!"

Bkornblume tidies up the notes on her desk and pulls her bag over her shoulder.

The moment she pushes open the studio door, the warm early-summer air embraces her.

She pauses beneath a streetlamp and takes out her diary and turns to tomorrow's packed part-time schedule.

**Bkornblume:**"What does the future I'm hoping for look like, I wonder?"
