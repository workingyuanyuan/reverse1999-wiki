## Story

Countryside, Texas

**Collaborator:** Grace, give me the toy!

The collaborator crawls on the ground, eyes locked on the wind-up toy in Grace's hand. It's her only hope.

But it's lifted just out of reach, and with it goes her chance at survival.

**Collaborator:** You—!

In a flash, Kimberly snaps the collaborator's neck.

**Ms. Grace:** What a shame that the contract item only answers to its holder.

**Ms. Kimberly:** Why?

**Ms. Grace:** Hmm? Why what, Kimberly?

Kimberly flicks the blood from her claws. She tries to glare at Ms. Grace, but her brows can't help but furrow in confusion.

**Ms. Kimberly:** Why? Why did you do that? Why didn't you stop me? W-Weren't they your allies?

**Ms. Grace:** Oh, Kimberly. That last body's barely even gone cold. Why all the questions now that the deed is done? Besides, you got what you wanted, didn't you?

**Ms. Kimberly:** I wish you were all dead. If it weren't for that stupid toy, your neck would be next!

**Ms. Kimberly:** But, still, I don't understand you. Surely this isn't how allies are supposed to treat each other?

**Ms. Grace:** Tsk-tsk, such a temper. Can't you at least try to please me like you did your other masters?

**Ms. Kimberly:** How am I supposed to figure out what you want, you-you freak!

**Ms. Grace:** Ah, so that's how you are, is it? Seems my interests are beyond a naive, rude little girl such as yourself.

**Ms. Grace:** I'm glad to see you at least understand the word "ally," though. Perhaps there's still some conscience left in you after all.

**Ms. Grace:** Unfortunately, I don't have allies.

**Ms. Grace:** And as for what I want, I have an order for you.

She waves the toy back and forth, and Kimberly's trembling eyes can't help but follow.

**Ms. Grace:** Tell me about your sister, Mata Hari.

**Ms. Kimberly:** What? That's it? That's all you want?

The fear on her face turns to surprise, then into a bright, eager smile.

**Ms. Kimberly:** She was the best sister one could ever hope to have in the world! She taught me how to sing, and do makeup, and all kinds of fun games!

**Ms. Grace:** What about her work? I'm sure a legendary spy must have been quite busy.

**Ms. Kimberly:** Work? I don't know. She never told me anything about that. Maybe she didn't need to work?

**Ms. Grace:** How else would she get food, Kimberly?

**Ms. Kimberly:** When she was around, people always just brought it to us. And they didn't want anything in return, either, well, other than a dance.

**Ms. Kimberly:** So, maybe her job was dancing?

**Ms. Grace:** \*sigh\* This is pointless.

**Ms. Grace:** The next question isn't an order.

**Ms. Grace:** If you were free, where would you go? What would you do?

**Ms. Kimberly:** I'd snap your neck!

**Ms. Kimberly:** No, no! Sorry, forget that. I think I'd return to São Paulo. I want to go back to the Heartfelt Home.

**Ms. Kimberly:** I think there ought to be some people there who still remember me.

**Ms. Grace:** São Paulo. What a coincidence. That was your home?

**Ms. Grace:** When was that?

**Ms. Kimberly:** It's hard to say. I just remember the whole world was at war. All the chaos gave me the chance to slip away to São Paulo.

**Ms. Kimberly:** I lay low at the Heartfelt Home. Then the war ended, and I decided to stay there with the old folks.

**Ms. Grace:** That would've been around 1945. About 45 years ago.

**Ms. Grace:** The people you knew are probably long gone.

**Ms. Kimberly:** I know humans and arcanists don't live long, but there must be someone still alive! And it's not like I have anywhere else to go.

**Ms. Grace:** I don't just mean the natural passage of time. São Paulo's seen multiple "Storms."

**Ms. Grace:** The place you remember may have already been reconstructed into an artificial version of what it once was—something that no longer belongs. Or perhaps you're the one who no longer belongs there. Knowing that, would you still go back?

**Ms. Kimberly:** Whatever do you mean? I lived there. I remember it. Even if things have changed, it's still São Paulo, isn't it?

**Ms. Grace:** ...

**Ms. Grace:** A lot of people would envy your optimism, Kimberly.

She starts waving the toy again.

**Ms. Grace:** Don't tell anyone about what we just discussed. You exploited a loophole in the contract to kill everyone except me. That's it.

**Ms. Grace:** Let's head back to the motel. Starting tonight, you'll have a room all to yourself.

**Ms. Stranger:** And just like that, Manus Vindictae's plan on Route 77 crumbled, all because they mishandled the succubus.

**Ms. Stranger:** After that, Zeno found the ritual array and Anjo Nala's contract item in the deserter's room. Those were supposed to go back to the Foundation as part of standard evidence processing.

**Ms. Stranger:** But nobody anticipated Igor's betrayal. And, in some strange turn of fate, the contract item ended up in Brazil.

**Ms. Stranger:** Luckily, Vertin performed exceptionally well in São Paulo. She destroyed the contract item and stowed Anjo Nala away in the Suitcase—a better outcome for her than returning to the Foundation's control, I think.

**Ms. Stranger:** It all worked out in my favor, too. Since everything pointed to São Paulo, no one paid much attention to my carefully planned failure on Route 77.

**Ms. Stranger:** I'd been fully prepared to accept my punishment, but nothing ever came of it.

**Ms. Stranger:** In fact, I continued to rise through Manus Vindictae's ranks. Quite the plot twist, don't you think?

**Mesmer Jr.:** You seem to have been particularly interested in Mata Hari.

**Ms. Stranger:** Of course. She was the most legendary female spy of them all, the inspiration for countless novels and films.

**Ms. Stranger:** Even spies have their idols, Ms. Mesmer.

**Mesmer Jr.:** So, after speaking with her sister, what did you think?

**Ms. Stranger:** I was disillusioned. Even her own sister knew nothing about her life as a spy.

**Ms. Stranger:** Either the job made her trust no one or it was so ugly she couldn't even bring herself to talk about it with her closest family.

**Ms. Stranger:** After that, I gave up all my fiction-inspired fantasies.

**Ms. Stranger:** Not that I wasn't a good enough spy, but the romance was gone.

**Mesmer Jr.:** Was that the Mothmen's training—the clockwork mechanism?

**Ms. Stranger:** Without a doubt. My love for spy stories must've been triggered by one of their "lights."

We're observing an uptick in unexpected behavior.

She's beginning to perform actions that align with her memories and personality on her own.

That'll improve her ability to impersonate others—a useful flaw.

But it also means she risks losing control.

Again, we'll deal with it when the time comes.

**Mothman:** We're observing an uptick in unexpected behavior.

She's beginning to perform actions that align with her memories and personality on her own.

That'll improve her ability to impersonate others—a useful flaw.

But it also means she risks losing control.

Again, we'll deal with it when the time comes.

**Ms. Stranger:** Maybe it was the naive, romantic Lily projecting herself onto those stories.

**Ms. Stranger:** Or perhaps it was Grace, enjoying her life of status and privilege.

**Ms. Stranger:** No matter when that interest began, or for whom, it died that day.
