## Story

**Beryl:** Monsieur Eloi's words had pierced my mother, allowing her weakness to pour from the wound.

**Beryl:** Before then, she had never once asked me to read her fate.

**Beryl:** But she looked upon all she had gained—her life of comfort—and drew false courage from it.

**Beryl:** It was on that day that she decided to confront it.

**Beryl:** So she asked me. And I answered truthfully.

**Beryl:** As fate would have it.

**Guest III:** Monsieur Damien, I haven't seen your wife in nearly two months now.

**Guest III:** Still resting at home, is she?

**Damien:** Yes ... she's quite fragile. She'll need more time until she's herself again.

**Guest III:** Oh my. I'll be sure to keep her in my prayers.

**Guest III:** And Mademoiselle Beryl? May I see her today?

**Damien:** My apologies, madame. Ever since her mother fell ill, Beryl's been in low spirits.

**Damien:** But I can relay a message to her for you. I'm certain she will be glad to ease your concerns.

He glances toward the closed door deep within the showroom, then resumes his polite smile as he greets the next customer.

Books and crystals press into the narrow room, encircling a small figure at its center.

This was where Clairine's career began. She kept it deep within the parlor, like a medal earned in her battle with life.

**Beryl:** "The Infinite contracts inwards, and the light of purification descends ..."

**Beryl:** "Before all things it reveals itself—the Sephirot of the Infinite ..."

**Beryl:** After that, I no longer offered my divination. The future had already realized itself. I was to move to the next place.

**Beryl:** "Life" is only a phenomenon. Once placed beneath "fate," it becomes transparent, its ending visible at a glance.

**Beryl:** But what is "fate"? Why does it hold this absolute power?

**Beryl:** The teachings of the Kabbalists became my reference.

**Beryl:** As for Madame Clairine ... she was an interesting person.

Bouanich Residence

Beryl watches the moonlight through the window. The sound of a fierce argument drifts past her ears and thins into the night.

**Clairine:** Damien, are you trying to kill me?!

**Clairine:** I said you must not return home now!

**Damien:** But this is my home too—and your daughter's, mind you!

**Damien:** We're a family, Clairine. We ought to stay together—to face this coming disaster as one ...

**Clairine:** No. Your presence will only push me closer to death.

**Clairine:** The wretch may have followed you in already—hiding in the kitchen with a knife, waiting for midnight to strike ...

**Damien:** Clairine, stop. You're imagining things!

**Damien:** No one followed us. And didn't you already throw the knife away?

**Damien:** Leave the parlor—I can deal with that. But what about our daughter? Are you going to abandon Beryl too?

Hearing her name, the girl turns.

Clairine rushes to her barefoot, drops to a crouch, and grabs her by the shoulders.

**Clairine:** Beryl, you understand, don't you?

**Clairine:** Everything I've done—you understand why I did it!

**Clairine:** Besides, I have to live if I'm to continue to love you, isn't that right?

She examines her mother's twisted, frantic expression.

**Beryl:** Papa.

Damien stares at his daughter in surprise. Her eyes rest on her mother with a faint, unreadable interest.

**Beryl:** We had best not disturb her. Maman will be fine.

**Beryl:** Madame Clairine had escaped hardship through her will alone.

**Beryl:** Facing death, though disordered and desperate, she revealed the same core that had once sustained her.

**Beryl:** For fifteen days after hearing the prophecy, she had tried to remain composed.

**Beryl:** For the rest of the month, she did all she could to prevent it—repairing doors and windows, dismissing the servants ...

**Beryl:** By the second month, she no longer dared step outside, isolating herself even from her husband and child.

**Beryl:** She carved herself an island in the river of fate, swearing to stay far away from its swirling currents.

**Beryl:** Her efforts were so moving that I almost felt a trace of longing for what she fought for.

**Beryl:** Before her death, all our conversations had been reduced to a single repeated exchange.

**Clairine:** Beryl, has my fate changed?

**Beryl:** No, Maman.

**Guest IV:** How fortunate—to come on a day Mademoiselle Beryl is willing to divine!

**Guest IV:** But is your mother well? She's been ill for so long now. It must be two years now, mustn't it?

**Beryl:** My mother has never stopped resisting her "disease." Perhaps ... she may recover after all.

**Guest IV:** That's just wonderful!

**Beryl:** So, Madame Solène, what troubles you?

**Guest IV:** My husband hasn't returned home in several days ...

A vision strikes abruptly. It flashes before Beryl's eyes.

Her mother draws open the curtains. Harsh sunlight falls upon her pale face.

**Clairine:** A fine day indeed.

**Clairine:** Perfect for a little walk, I'd say.

After years of silence, her voice is hazy and mumbling.

She turns back into the room, takes an old dress from the wardrobe, and changes out of her yellowed house robe.

**Guest IV:** Mademoiselle Beryl?

She gazes outward, watching the woman in the vision.

Clairine sits at the dressing table, carefully arranging her hair.

She smiles at the mirror, releasing the tension long locked in her brow.

She looks at peace, as if nothing in the world could trouble her.

She leaves the room and walks toward the front door.

**Clairine:** Beryl, is this the last you'll see of me?

**Clairine:** Then let me tell you—this is my choice.

She speaks directly into the open air.

Then she opens the door and steps out of the home she has not left in two years.

**Guest IV:** Mademoiselle, are you alright?

Street, Paris

Clairine walks along the street toward the parlor.

**Former Customer:** Oh! Madame Clairine, you've recovered, have you?

**Clairine:** Thank you for your concern. I am well—in fact, I've never felt better.

She gracefully greets them, then takes her leave.

She enters a narrow alley. She must pass through it to reach the street with the parlor.

**Beryl:** ...

But Clairine would never emerge at the other end of that alley.

**Guest IV:** Mademoiselle Beryl!

The diviner returns to herself amid Madame Solène's anxious calling.

No one present senses what is about to occur.

**Beryl:** ...

**Beryl:** Yes. This is fate.

**Beryl:** My apologies, Madame Solène. I was distracted for a moment.

**Guest IV:** Oh, that's quite alright. Are you well?

The young diviner smiles faintly.

**Beryl:** Just fine, madame.

**Beryl:** Fate is unchangeable.

**Beryl:** Madame Clairine failed. On that day, she "chose" her own end.

**Beryl:** I was grateful to her ... not only for her love as a mother, but because she confirmed for me the true nature of fate.

**Beryl:** Fate is a woven net, and we the prey caught between the fibers.

**Beryl:** And yet, it leaves us just enough space to breathe—enough to make us believe we still have the freedom to choose a path, which is, in fact, the only one left to us.

**Beryl:** The romanticism is charming. But is there truly any difference between those who believe in free will and those who surrender to fate?

**Beryl:** Like my mother, who exhausted every avenue, even withdrew herself from the world, only to delay the ending by a fraction.

**Beryl:** That brief, fierce strength that once shone in her would continue to play out in countless others.

**Beryl:** I enjoyed observing them, and yet I knew none would be the exception.

**Beryl:** Fate has many names. Absolute force. Absolute will. The supreme law ...

**Beryl:** The endless light from the radiant Sephirot.

**Beryl:** Beneath its glow, there is no difference between "me" and them. Thus, naturally, I have no need for "me."

**Arcana:** How dost thou view the "Storm"?
