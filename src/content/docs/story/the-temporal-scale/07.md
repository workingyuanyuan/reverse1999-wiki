The Foundation's halls are buzzing with excitement.

Yet within a certain conference room's walls, the atmosphere is tense.

**Irinei:** Our joint project has already proved quite fruitful, Ms. Kenna.

**Irinei:** I think we all needed this—some good news to lift our spirits.

**Irinei:** However, we do have one small problem.

Irinei pauses. He must choose his words with care.

**Irinei:** At this point, we still can't say for certain which part of the building generated its immunity.

**Irinei:** Rebuilding the dome could risk damaging its original structure. We may end up jeopardizing the very immunity we're trying to retain.

**Irinei:** We know too little to push for a project of this size. If we're to do this safely and responsibly, we should wait until we have a better understanding of the building, or better yet, of the anomaly itself.

**Irinei:** Let me assure you, your investigators' discovery already speaks well of your abilities. There's no need to rush things to prove yourself within the department.

Kenna, not at all afraid of Irinei's questioning, simply tucks a lock of hair behind her ear.

**Kenna:** Respectfully, I didn't advocate for this project for my own benefit, Mr. Irinei. Think about how much this anomaly has wiped out. We need to make up for what we once had and who we've lost.

**Kenna:** Zeno is barely holding on with what they've managed to pull together, and Laplace is overwhelmed by the sheer surge of anomalous data. This location of Nowa Miedź may sow the seeds of our recovery, but we need a permanent presence there.

**Kenna:** How can we "get a better understanding of the anomaly itself" when we don't even have a safe place to work?

An issue they've pushed from their minds, now laid bare.

**Kenna:** And what if the Temporal Anomaly happens again? Where do we run next time?

**Irinei:** I'm suggesting patience, not stagnation. The two are not to be conflated, Ms. Kenna.

COMBAT:

Ms. Kenna, I've kept my actions from no one. We're all on the same side here, facing the same enemy.

There's another problem. How do we keep the project from the building's residents? Surely they'll notice the construction.

There'll be no need for that. The residents saw the anomaly just as clearly as you and I did.

As for the other residents of the city—the construction site won't draw their attention. Its location is reasonably secluded.

***

Even so, the project will hardly be a breeze. We don't have the manpower, to begin with.

Three investigators and nine months—that's all I need. Cornerstone and Grundriss have already gathered the necessary workforce among the locals.

**Irinei:** I have to say, it is remarkable how many locals they've managed to recruit for this construction on such short notice.

This is by no means the outcome everyone had hoped for.

Whispers fill the conference room, intermixed with the occasional cough and shuffling of papers.

**Kenna:** They are indeed full of surprises. I wasn't entirely confident they'd find anything at all.

**Kenna:** But looking at the progress we've made now, I'd say luck is finally on our side.

One corner of her mouth curls into a smile.

**Kenna:** It'd be a shame to stop now, don't you think?

**Kenna:** And I'm certain I don't need to spell out the edge this building will give us over our enemy if we turn it into another safe zone against the anomaly.

As silence falls in the conference room, seconds seem to stretch on forever.

There is no straightforward choice. The attendees wait for guidance, a sign, anything. Unfortunately, none present are gifted with the power of premonition.

**Lucy:** I believe you both may be overestimating the gulf between your positions.

It's Lucy, listening from a corner, who breaks the stalemate.

**Lucy:** The rational course forward is to break the project into smaller phases, allowing for necessary reassessments before we proceed with each step.

**Lucy:** First, we must determine the viability of building a new dome. In the meantime, Laplace will perform tests on the stone samples to identify the origin and nature of its immunity.

**Lucy:** Then we move to the second phase, sourcing the resources necessary for accurate reconstruction.

**Lucy:** This should ensure we minimize risk and put our limited supplies to their most efficient use without stagnation.

**Kenna:** Point well made, Madam Lucy. I agree entirely.

**Kenna:** And you, Mr. Irinei? Any thoughts?

**Irinei:** ...

**Irinei:** Six months.

**Irinei:** Take it phase by phase, like Madam Lucy said. If at any point there appears to be a risk to the structure's immunity, then the entire project will be terminated immediately.

**Grundriss:** No. I'll need the plans first. We'll go through them line by line before anything else. We can't leave any room for error.

**Grundriss:** Damn it, Cornerstone. I'm an engineer, not a psychiatrist.

**Olga:** Now, now, Lucjan, we've talked about this before. You're in no position to do this kind of heavy work.

**Lucjan:** Right. You're right, boss.

**Olga:** \*sigh\* The poor child was separated from his parents during the deportations.
