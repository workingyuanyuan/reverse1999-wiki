## Story

1. July 1996.

Partly Cloudy.

With thanks to Madam Constantine, we've finally received

approval to resume our project.

Unfortunately, if Laplace's latest report is accurate, the

Temporal Anomaly could strike again at any moment, meaning that

we need to work as if we have no moments to spare.

Ugh, if only we had more manpower. We could really use about a

thousand more hands!

**Grundriss:** 1. July 1996.

**Grundriss:** Partly Cloudy.

**Grundriss:** With thanks to Madam Constantine, we've finally received approval to resume our project.

**Grundriss:** Unfortunately, if Laplace's latest report is accurate, the Temporal Anomaly could strike again at any moment, meaning that we need to work as if we have no moments to spare.

**Grundriss:** Ugh, if only we had more manpower. We could really use about a thousand more hands!

**Irinei:** Like it or not, those are the facts.

**Irinei:** Your budget was already stretched thin. Whatever little you've received only came through thanks to Ms. Kenna's pleading with multiple departments.

**Cornerstone:** Tch, how much could a few posters possibly cost?!

**Cornerstone:** Don't tell me we're so broke we can't even afford to print a few papers!

The voice on the other end is cold as ice.

**Irinei:** I'm afraid so, Ms. Cornerstone. Your request for more workers, which, I should add, wasn't fully compliant, may have miraculously been approved. But that doesn't mean money will just keep rolling in.

**Irinei:** More hands or more handbills—you can have one, not both.

**Cornerstone:** ...

As Cornerstone hangs up, her usually fiercely upright back has taken on a hunch.

**Jasio:** We could take to the streets with megaphones? That would save on posters, at least.

Cornerstone has already taped several sheets of paper to her drawing board before Jasio can finish speaking.

**Jasio:** Uh, what are you doing?

**Cornerstone:** Grundriss and the others already have the recruitment stalls ready. The only thing missing is the posters.

**Cornerstone:** Now we just have to show that same strength we showed in our video!

She takes stock of her art supplies, lining them up in a tidy row.

**Jasio:** Wait, surely you're not going to ...

**Cornerstone:** Yes. I'll draw the posters myself.

He catches her steely gaze and accepts that there's no changing her mind.

**Cornerstone:** Someone give me a pour-over, would you?

The next day

The next day

**Grundriss:** Took you long enough.

The fog lifts just enough to unveil the stall. Grundriss stands in front of it, tapping her foot.

**Grundriss:** You were supposed to have those posters ready by nine. And look at the time now. It's—ah, 8:59.

**Grundriss:** A minute early. Not bad.

**Jasio:** Hey, if it weren't for Ms. Cornerstone, I'd have been here even earlier, maybe even had a tipple while I waited.

**Jasio:** Apparently, we don't even have the cash to print a few posters, so she decided to draw them herself.

**Grundriss:** She what? How is that even possible? She only had a few hours.

**Grundriss:** Wow!

The posters are overflowing with fine details. Even the borders are intricate, like carved frames holding stained glass.

More impressive still, each poster is one of a kind.

**Grundriss:** These aren't even posters anymore. They're ...

**Jasio:** Art, yeah. True art. Not that I understand any of it.

**Grundriss:** ...

**Grundriss:** So she stayed up all night just to paint these decorative borders?

**Jasio:** Well, according to her, they're "essential elements for visual impact."

She can't help but roll her eyes.

**Grundriss:** I shouldn't have asked.

**Grundriss:** Anyway, they'll definitely grab some attention.

Two hours later …

Two hours later …

In two hours, the only visitor to the stall has been the wind kicking up the tablecloth.

**Grundriss:** Mmm.

**Grundriss:** Should we try our luck in the city center instead? If this place even has a proper city center, that is.

**Jasio:** City center or not, true art or not, no poster is more appealing than a train ticket out of here.

**Jasio:** With the factories shutting down one after another, there isn't much point in sticking around.

**Jasio:** They'll be going to Warsaw or Gdańsk, maybe even Germany, if they have the chance.

**Jasio:** The ones who stay here are either too young, too old, or simply too afraid to leave.

**Grundriss:** I think I get it.

**Grundriss:** So, which one are you?

Jasio looks up and puffs a smoke ring toward the sky.

It makes a weak attempt at rotating before dissipating into the gray above.

**Jasio:** Me? Probably the third kind. I don't know where else to go, so I stay put.

**Grundriss:** \*sigh\*

The two fall into silence.

The squealing of tram wheels on iron rails sounds in the distance. It echoes off the buildings—one echo, then two, then ...

**Grundriss:** Looks like we won't be seeing any results today.

With a huff, she gets to her feet and gathers the posters into a neat stack.

**Grundriss:** Let's just pack it in and go.

Suddenly, a burst of light and shadow nearly blinds them.

Multi-colored lights flicker, diffused in the thick fog.

**Cornerstone:** Afternoon, everyone!

Cornerstone, a huge neon sign hoisted on her shoulder, paces over.

**Grundriss:** ...?

**Cornerstone:** Look what I found collecting dust in the back of the Old Oak! Returned to its former glory, thanks to yours truly.

**Grundriss:** Didn't you just pull an all-nighter drawing those fancy posters? You should be in bed already!

Cornerstone squints.

The colorful lights on her shoulder continue to flicker, illuminating her cheek red and blue.

**Cornerstone:** Architects don't need sleep. Haven't I already told you that?

**Grundriss:** ...

**Grundriss:** Wait, there are no cables attached. How is it glowing?

**Everecho:** Hello, Ms. Grundriss.

Everecho's head pokes out from behind Cornerstone, and she gives a small wave.

The neon lights shining in her golden eyes make them look like fireflies.

**Grundriss:** ...

**Grundriss:** Where's Honza? Aren't you two always together?

**Everecho:** He's ...

The fireflies vanish, chased away by lowered lids and eyelashes.

**Everecho:** He's caught a cold, so he is staying away from people for now.

**Grundriss:** Hm? I didn't know puppets could catch colds.

**Grundriss:** Given a "cold" usually refers to an infection in a living organism with some form of respiratory system, immune response, fluid circulation, and—

**Cornerstone:** Read between the lines, silly. Now quit prying into the girl's private matters and come give me a hand.

\*thud\*

Cornerstone stands the sign upright, turning the stall and its surroundings into what looks like a makeshift disco.

Holding it steady with one hand, she raises a megaphone in the other.

**Cornerstone:** Alright, everyone, let the recruiting begin!

With the neon sign and beautiful posters together, the stall finally manages to attract some attention.

First there are a couple of visitors, then a dozen.

**Citizen I:** This a long-term gig?

**Citizen II:** Do I need a license or anything?

**Citizen III:** Is the pay daily or monthly?

More and more passersby are drawn to the stall.

The neon lights reshape their once indifferent faces, turning eyes accustomed to disappointment into ones ready for action.

**Cornerstone:** Ahem, please form a line and wait for your turn! One at a time!

**TV Correspondent:** Excuse me, miss! We're from the TV program Public Voices. We'd like to request an on-camera interview!

A microphone is thrust through the crowd, and a camera's red light glows.

The lens captures neon lights illuminating would-be workers' faces and how an ordinary evening has been turned into one worth recording.

In an office on another end of the continent, the atmosphere is unusually lively.

**Kenna:** What's going on? Why are you all crammed into this tiny room?

**Foundation Staff Member I:** Our girl's on TV. That's why!

**Kenna:** TV?

**TV Correspondent:** Perhaps you've heard trucks passing along the old railway at night or seen lights flickering back on at construction sites. Yes, after years of decline, it appears Nowa Miedź is finally springing back to life!

**TV Correspondent:** But, this time, it's not a new highway or a new mall. What we've managed to gather so far is that it's a top-secret, top-priority project. A true Nowa Miedź mystery.

**TV Correspondent:** Lucky for us, we have an anonymous representative for this mysterious project right here with us! Any hints as to what you're working on?

**"Anonymous Representative":** I'm sorry, but our confidentiality agreement won't allow us to disclose any specifics. All I can say is ...

**"Anonymous Representative":** This unprecedented project may forever change how we think about energy usage and public safety!

**"Anonymous Representative":** If you have a passionate, earnest heart and want to help build a future worth fighting for, please join our construction team!

**"Anonymous Representative":** Pick up your phone now and dial 41123.

**Foundation Staff Member I:** Pfft! "Unprecedented project"? "A future worth fighting for"? Is she serious?

**Foundation Staff Member II:** She's not wrong, in a way. If they actually pull this off, there's no doubt it will be an unprecedented achievement.

**Kenna:** ...

Kenna doesn't look away from the screen, but her shock soon gives way to a laugh.

**Kenna:** Hahah. She did complain about how hard it was to find workers last time we spoke.

**Kenna:** Looks like she found a solution to her problem. A bit of publicity might just work wonders.

The atmosphere relaxes. It seems the neon lights have seeped their way through the screen and into the office, too.

A crowd gathers around the little TV, making comments as they watch.

Before long, the excitement attracts others from the hallway.

**Constantine:** Oh! What fun have I missed this time?

**Irinei:** It's the Nowa Miedź project. They're actively recruiting from the civilian population.

**Constantine:** Is that so? I suppose that is a clever workaround for their tight budget.

**Constantine:** But recruiting from the locals? That's quite the bold move, one that I'm sure doesn't strictly comply with our confidentiality protocols.

**Irinei:** ...

**Irinei:** That's just the start of our worries. Training new workers under these circumstances carries dozens of other potential risks.

**Irinei:** I can't even begin to imagine how they'll understand and follow our unique construction needs and safety protocols.

Constantine waves a hand at the comment. Her gaze is entirely focused on the smiling faces through the glass.

**Constantine:** All valid concerns, Mr. Irinei. But rest assured, the investment is limited. This won't affect our broader operations.

**Constantine:** I look at this project as a kind of seed.

**Constantine:** You'll never know what it might grow into until you've put it in the ground. Isn't that so?
