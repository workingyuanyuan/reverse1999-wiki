23. April 1996.

23. April 1996.

Sunny.

Construction commences today. The schedule is as follows:

7:30 AM: Arrived on site, checked all safety measures, and ensured the hoardings and notices are up.

8:00 AM: Site induction with the workers ...

**Grundriss:** Right, everything's set. We're off to a smooth start!

**Grundriss:** You can do this, Grundriss. It's just a job like any other.

Now steadied, she snaps her notebook shut.

**Grundriss:** The workers aren't as skilled as the professionals in the Foundation, but they'll do fine.

**Grundriss:** As long as I stay calm and focused ...

The atrium is empty.

**Grundriss:** Oh, where is everybody?

A breeze whisks up a couple of yellowed leaves, smacking Grundriss square in the face.

**Grundriss:** Did I get the time wrong?

Four hours later …

Four hours later …

Noon rolls around, and the workers finally mosey their way in.

They line up in the sunlight, eyes unfocused. Last night's drinks have clearly done a number on them.

**Jasio:** Morning, miss.

**Grundriss:** Far from it, Mr. Jasio. It's 12:34. You're late, and so is everyone else.

**Grundriss:** Your manuals had clear instructions that work starts at 8 AM sharp.

**Construction Worker I:** \*burp\* What manual?

**Construction Worker II:** No need to get all riled up, Ms. Grundriss. We can work till midnight to make up for lost time. Eh, how's that?

**Grundriss:** Stay calm now. No good shouting on the first day.

Grundriss takes a deep breath.

**Grundriss:** I'll let it slide this time. But going forward, if you're even one minute late, you'll be penalized.

**Grundriss:** And no drinking or card games on site. Helmets stay on. That's nonnegotiable.

**Construction Worker I:** Huh? We didn't sign up for all these extra rules.

**Construction Worker I:** Let's just stick to the book, miss.

She glares daggers at him.

**Grundriss:** We are sticking to the book, every word of it. You'd know that if you'd bothered to open your manual.

**Construction Worker I:** ...

**Grundriss:** Alright. Let's not waste any more time.

**Grundriss:** Before we start, allow me to give you a quick rundown of our goals here.

She turns and scrawls something on the wall using a piece of chalk.

**Grundriss:** A project like this typically follows the following phases: pre-design evaluation, design, pre-construction and construction, handover, and operation.

**Grundriss:** The design phase is where the architect makes and refines the plan and builds a model of what we aim to achieve.

**Grundriss:** Pre-construction and construction—the phases most pertinent to you—involve material acquisition, site preparation, foundation construction, structural works, and so on.

She pauses so everyone can take all the info in.

**Grundriss:** Right now, we are at the very start of our project. So, our primary focus will be on design and site preparation.

**Grundriss:** Since we're on a tight schedule, the two phases will be carried out simultaneously.

**Grundriss:** Our architect and her team will be developing a feasible plan and model for the dome.

**Grundriss:** While we, the construction team, procure the key building material: the Echo Stones.

**Construction Worker I:** The Echo Stones? We've got plenty of those lying around all over the place, don't we?

**Grundriss:** We'll need more. The loose ones are only about enough for a millstone and a few cutting boards, but we've got an entire dome to build.

**Grundriss:** And for that, we'll need the ones that are still in the walls, too. It will be a two-step process. First, we mark the stones we need; then, we take down the walls.

**Grundriss:** Is everyone keeping up so far?

She turns back at them and is met with nothing but blank stares.

**Construction Worker II:** Ehhh. Break down the walls. Got it.

**Grundriss:** That's right. Good.

She sighs. This is going to be a long day.

**Grundriss:** Now, I'll show you how to tell the Echo Stones apart from regular bricks.

**Marcin:** Eh, we'll save you the trouble. Everyone knows the noisy ones are the Echo Stones.

**Grundriss:** Not quite. Sometimes they're quiet, but I found a way to ensure we don't miss any.

She approaches a wall, looks it over, then knocks it with her knuckles.

\*knock\*

**Grundriss:** Hear that? The bricks that make the sound are the Echo Stones.

**Grundriss:** And look closely. They each have a faint tint of steely green compared to regular concrete.

She takes out a marker pen and draws an "X" on the Echo Stone.

**Grundriss:** Just mark them first. Then I'll make the arrangements for demolition.

**Grundriss:** Next, there are a few safety rules to follow. Refusal to comply with any one of them—

**Grundriss:** Will result in a penalty deducted directly from your salary.

Two hours later …

Two hours later …

**Grundriss:** I'm not repeating this for a fourth time: Secure those scaffolds before getting on!

**Construction Worker I:** We did! See? Standing just fine!

**Grundriss:** It's deflecting by more than 30 cm! I don't even need my arcane skills to see it.

**Grundriss:** Hey! You over there! Get down this instant!

**Grundriss:** Don't you know that's a load-bearing beam?

**Construction Worker II:** This thing? Oh, okay. I'm coming down.

**Construction Worker II:** Hey, who's pulling me? Stop it!

***

COMBAT:

***

The sun descends below the horizon.

Amid the noisy construction, Grundriss sits alone, her eyes glazed over.

**Bkornblume:** Long day, Ms. Grundriss?

**Grundriss:** Don't get me started. These people ... Half the time I'm not even sure they're listening. At this rate, there's no way we'll finish on schedule. And I hear Cornerstone is struggling with her plans as well.

**Grundriss:** They all seemed so motivated, until they actually got down to doing the work. I even hoped we might finish a month early. How stupid of me.

**Grundriss:** If things go on like this, I'll be forced to send another report back to the Foundation.

**Bkornblume:** ...

Bkornblume sighs and sits beside her.

**Bkornblume:** I've worked with people like them.

**Bkornblume:** They're not the most ideal for your work. It's true. But they're far from the least. Just ordinary people. Nothing more.

**Bkornblume:** You're under a lot of pressure, I know. But you're expecting them to put their lives and all their bad habits aside to match your vision.

**Bkornblume:** That's not realistic. This isn't a fairy tale, and they're not perfect heroes.

**Grundriss:** I'm not asking them to be heroes. But don't they understand this might be their only chance to survive another anomaly?

**Bkornblume:** They know. But it's not present to them; it's an abstract threat that feels too distant to fear.

She pats Grundriss's shoulder before turning her gaze to the workers.

**Bkornblume:** You can't think about something so large when you're just surviving the day-to-day. You know the man who was struggling to put up that scaffold? He works a second job as a bouncer at a nightclub.

**Bkornblume:** And the one sitting on the ground there has a permanent injury he sustained working in an oil refinery some years ago.

**Bkornblume:** And the one with the hammer—his child is sick at home with a fever.

**Grundriss:** I had no idea. Maybe I have been too hard on them.

**Bkornblume:** Oh, sorry. I just made that all up.

**Grundriss:** ...

Bkornblume does a long stretch, then gives Grundriss another pat on the shoulder. Her scarf can't conceal the smile reaching her eyes.

**Bkornblume:** But you understand my point, don't you?

**Bkornblume:** They're struggling. Maybe they've made things harder for you, but they're not your enemy. You should spend more time with them, like you did with me all those years ago.

**Bkornblume:** That might be the best way to reach them. It's worth a shot, at least.

**Grundriss:** ...

**Grundriss:** Yeah, worth a shot.

**Cornerstone:** I'm afraid it's bad news, Lucjan.

**Cornerstone:** I've checked and double-checked my calculations, but my conclusion remains the same.

In her makeshift office, Cornerstone looks to her newly appointed "assistant" with remorse.

**Cornerstone:** Even taking down all the walls we can from the west wing, there won't be enough material to rebuild the dome using only Echo Stones.

**Lucjan:** I-I never finished fifth grade, miss. I don't think I can help much with your calculations.

**Lucjan:** Math was always my worst subject, too. I barely passed my last exam by just a few points.

He brings Cornerstone her requested cup of piping-hot coffee, which is his sole duty as an assistant.

**Cornerstone:** Don't worry about it. Sometimes just having someone to talk about the problem with is enough.

**Cornerstone:** \*sigh\* I'll just have to do what I can. Finish the model first, then tell Grundriss to disband the workers once they've gathered all the Echo Stones in the west wing.

She looks to the east wing, currently serving as a place for the residents to sleep and relax.

**Cornerstone:** Could we make up the difference by tearing down the east wing too? Ah!

A swift hand snatches Cornerstone's coffee.

**Grundriss:** \*glug\*

She downs the whole cup in just a few gulps. If it scalds her, she doesn't show it.

**Grundriss:** Ah. Much better.

**Cornerstone:** What's that smell? Have you been drinking?

**Grundriss:** Bkornblume suggested I get to know the workers. So, I did.

**Grundriss:** I didn't drink, but Marcin, goodness, that man is a walking liquor bottle.

**Grundriss:** There's a bunch of them I need to speak to still. I feel like I'm spending more time fixing people's heads than managing these demolitions. Anyway, I should get back to it.

As she reaches the doorway, she hesitates and turns back.

**Grundriss:** Oh, and I overheard you just now. Not the smartest idea—if these workers are dismissed, it'll be almost impossible to find them again.

**Grundriss:** And I wouldn't expect them to remember much of what they've learned once they leave.

**Cornerstone:** So, what do you suggest?

**Grundriss:** We'll need to tear down the walls in the residential wing.

**Cornerstone:** Then, where will they live? Have you asked Olga about it?

**Grundriss:** Seems like you've got some work to do. Come on, project director, time to play your part.

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Cornerstone:** I'm glad to see you're finally feeling motivated.

Grundriss manages an exhausted shrug.

**Grundriss:** Well, it's too late to back out now, isn't it?

**Grundriss:** I warned you that we'd be getting ourselves in deep with this. Now we have to deal with the consequences. Sink or swim.

**Grundriss:** Speaking of, how's the design coming? Any progress?

Cornerstone accepts a fresh cup of coffee, courtesy of her assistant.

**Cornerstone:** Practically none.

**Grundriss:** ...?

**Grundriss:** Go on. Explain it all to "Dr. Grundriss." Just another problem to fix.

**Cornerstone:** It's the lighting. Something about it has me totally stumped.

**Cornerstone:** Everyone talks about the light pouring down like a waterfall on sunny days. But that's impossible. The triangular structure here would block it.

**Cornerstone:** What's more ...

Her gaze drifts to a corner as she speaks.

**Cornerstone:** I think someone here doesn't want me to finish my work.

**Cornerstone:** Paper, pencils, rulers ... all my stationery's been disappearing.

Though she keeps her voice low, the statement still reaches Lucjan's ears.

**Lucjan:** Really? Any idea who it was? I'll talk to them.

**Cornerstone:** Shh.

She presses a finger to her upturned lips.

**Cornerstone:** Don't worry. I've already hatched a plan.

Night falls, bringing the lengthy day to an end.

In a corner, some old tables and chairs have been used to create a temporary rest area.

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Cornerstone:** Light ... shadow ...

Cornerstone, exhausted from the day's work, has curled up under her blankets and finally drifted to sleep.

Something appears from a corner.

It creeps beneath the dim light, casting a looming shadow.

Then, like a net, the shadow envelops the sleeping figure.

**???:** ...
