## Story

The two keep a bit of distance as they walk, one staying behind the other.

**Grundriss:** I think the building is just up ahead.

**Grundriss:** Do you remember what it looked like?

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Cornerstone:** Silence.

An unexpected answer.

**Grundriss:** Silence? In what way?

**Cornerstone:** I couldn't say exactly. That's just the closest to the feeling that I can get. There was a song, though.

**Grundriss:** What kind of song? Do you remember the lyrics?

**Cornerstone:** ...

Cornerstone stares at Grundriss, her lips parted for a long while. But no words come out.

**Cornerstone:** Sorry, it was too long ago.

**Cornerstone:** I only remember it the way I remember this city. Blurred childhood impressions, light and shadow. Nothing solid.

**Cornerstone:** The streets feel familiar, the faces too. But when I look closer, I don't recognize anything.

**Cornerstone:** Here I am, standing right in the middle of it. And yet, it's never felt further away.

**Grundriss:** I get that.

The two fall back into silence.

In the stubborn spring fog, all Cornerstone can make out ahead of her is Grundriss's silhouette, shrinking as she walks further and further into the haze.

**Grundriss:** This is the building I saw.

They come to a halt.

The building sticks out in the fog, as if it were welcoming them.

A great, primal force lurks inside.

Like a beast slumbering within a cave.

**Cornerstone:** Architecture is more than just finding a harmony between beauty and utility.

**Cornerstone:** Buildings are what ground us—remind us that we're not just a bunch of abstract thoughts bouncing around in our own heads. In the spaces they create, we become aware of our own existence. But here, I feel exclusion.

No cracks, no colors; all subjectivity has been firmly shut out.

What remains is but a single reality—a looming structure and the two tiny figures at its feet.

**Grundriss:** Let's go in first, shall we?

**???:** 'Twas night, and three puppets sang.

Distant footsteps echo into the depths of the building.

**Honza:** Hmm. Breathy-wheezy, Honza hears breathing.

**Honza:** Everecho! Come and look-see.

**Cornerstone:** Take note of these shapes. Triangles, rectangles, circles ... used everywhere. Interesting choice.

**Cornerstone:** Are you familiar with Platonic Forms, Grundriss?

**Grundriss:** Not really. Not interested, either.

**Grundriss:** The reception here is hopeless. Still no connection to Bkornblume.

**Honza:** One, two, three, shadows slip into the mortuary!

**Honza:** Four, five, six, who will shut their eyes and lie there fixed?

**Everecho:** Bkorn ... Bkornblume ...

**Everecho:** I know her.

West Wing, The Building

The building's interior is silent, save for the occasional dripping of water.

The guests pass through the light and shadows, walking toward a destination seemingly forever out of reach.

**Grundriss:** A hunk of iron would pick up more signal than this thing.

**Grundriss:** \*sigh\* I was hoping to gather the data inside a week and then catch the earliest flight out of here.

Her gaze drifts to the megaphone hanging at her hip.

**Cornerstone:** Take a breath, partner. It's not so bad.

**Cornerstone:** Meeting with Bkornblume wasn't our main goal anyway. We're here to investigate the Point Irreversible.

**Cornerstone:** And if you think about it, haven't we already begun?

Cornerstone has already found herself immersed in the meetings of light and form.

**Cornerstone:** Strange. The purpose of this building is rather obscure.

**Cornerstone:** Was it meant to be residential? But the circulation and lighting don't seem to match. A church? I don't see any ceremonial features. And the layout is hardly efficient enough to be a workplace.

**Cornerstone:** If this place had been built entirely at random and served no practical purpose, there wouldn't be any clear architectural form or function. By all rights, it should've been a nightmare to build.

**Cornerstone:** But I don't see any construction-related issues either.

**Grundriss:** You don't? There are cracks and corrosion everywhere. This building must have been abandoned for at least 30 years.

**Grundriss:** Maybe it's not obvious to you, but I see danger. We'd better not stay long.

**Grundriss:** Do you hear that? Something's buzzing, like there's a fly.

Cornerstone tears her attention away from her sketchbook and listens.

**Cornerstone:** Mm? A little.

She approaches a wall and raps her knuckles against it.

\*knock\*

**Cornerstone:** Take a look at this texture. It looks like raw concrete, but if you reach out ...

She brushes her fingers against the cool gray surface.

**Cornerstone:** It's warm to the touch, as if it's been soaked in sunlight.

She presses an ear to the wall.

**Cornerstone:** Ah! There they are, the "rats" in the wall.

...

Stasia, shhhh. Listen.

It's the whisper of silence.

**Cornerstone:** ...!

**Cornerstone:** That voice ... That's ...

Cornerstone stumbles back.

**Grundriss:** What is it? I've never seen you look so shook-up. What did you hear?

**Cornerstone:** I can't say. I don't know. It doesn't make sense. Maybe I'm starting to feel that lack of sleep.

\*squeak\*

A strange noise—shrill yet somehow sluggish, like a rusty old crank being turned—echoes from the corner.

Then it turns again.

And again.

**Cornerstone:** Who's there? Show yourself!

Cornerstone takes a step forward, her hand hovering in front of Grundriss behind her.

**Honza:** You've been shot, Everecho. Start bleeding already. You'll be bloody-red and deathly-dead.

**Everecho:** I don't like blood.

**Grundriss:** Bkornblume! Finally!

### Start from Philosophy

**Cornerstone：** I'm still thinking about Platonic solids.

**Grundriss：** Oh, here we go again. Philosophy's too deep for me.

**Cornerstone：** It's actually pretty simple ... Plato believed that everything we see is just a "shadow." Their real "essence" is hidden from us.

**Cornerstone：** Like ... you see a circle drawn on the ground, and no matter how you look at it, it's never quite perfect, right?

**Grundriss：** Of course. No one can draw a perfect circle.

**Cornerstone：** In your mind, what would a "perfect circle" look like?

**Grundriss：** Hmm ... smooth edges, perfect, symmetrical?

**Cornerstone：** Exactly. This "perfect circle" in your mind is what Plato calls a "Form."

**Grundriss：** I don't really understand. What does that have to do with the buildings here ...?

**Cornerstone：** You may have already noticed that the buildings in Nowa Miedź are covered in triangles, squares, pentagons ...

**Cornerstone：** They remind me of the prototypes of those Forms Plato listed in Timaeus.

**Grundriss：** That's not unusual—aren't most modern buildings made up of geometric shapes? Straight lines, squares, triangles, they're everywhere.

**Cornerstone：** No, it's not the same. The geometric layout here doesn't simply seem to be pursuing any clear function—

**Cornerstone：** The shapes exist in a more ... natural way. Maybe it's a kind of design language. A language of materials ...

**Cornerstone：** I need to understand it ... understand it again.

**Cornerstone：** Everything has to start over from the beginning.
