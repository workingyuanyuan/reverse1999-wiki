## Story

Bkornblume rendezvouses with her companions, and together, they walk through the dilapidated building.

**Bkornblume:** I've been here for almost a week now, but I've never once met this Olga.

**Lucjan:** She just got back. People say she owns a big house outside the city, and she has more than enough money to leave anytime she wants. But she chooses to stay here.

**Lucjan:** I don't care if she's rich or poor like the rest of us. She's kind. That's all that matters to me.

For the first time, a glimpse of childish joy appears on Lucjan's face.

As they make their way, the expected stench of mold hits their senses, but so does an unexpected sound.

**Grundriss:** Is that music?

**Jasio:** Yeah. We used to have a group of young musicians with us. But then ...

Jasio shrugs and changes the topic.

**Jasio:** They left a few things here. People complained about the "mess" at first, not so much these days.

He stops in front of a worn door.

A sliver of light pours through the gap between it and the frame, along with a solemn tune.

**Jasio:** Here we are, ladies. Welcome to the Old Oak.

The Old Oak

Something between a restaurant and a bar stands amid the dilapidation.

Diners sit in small groups, silent as mannequins, while white smoke rises and swirls through the neon lights' glow in a colorful spectacle.

**Jasio:** I'd say those musicians did a decent job with the place—somehow crammed a bar and a stereo system into this tiny room, even a mic. No idea where they dug that up.

**Cornerstone:** This is the Nowa Miedź I know. We may not have all the answers, but as long as there's vodka on the table, we'll muddle through.

**Grundriss:** Didn't you leave when you were eight? Seems a little young to form a sentimental attachment to vodka.

Lucjan squeezes his way through the crowd as they talk.

**Lucjan:** I just spoke to O—the bartender. She said Olga will be here in a minute. In the meantime, you're welcome to have a drink.

**Lucjan:** If there's one thing we're not short on around here, it's vodka. Żubrówka, Wyborowa, Chopin ...

**Bkornblume:** Oh! I've always wanted to try Żubrówka.

**Cornerstone:** Look at this place. I have to say I'm very impressed with how you've put this all together.

**Cornerstone:** It bolsters my confidence in our upcoming project.

Cornerstone nods as she takes it all in.

**Cornerstone:** Those long tables pushed together over there ... That's the bar, is it?

**Cornerstone:** Come on. Let's find out how well they handle their own liquor.

**Amiable Old Lady:** Evening, young ladies.

Hunched over busily behind the bar is an old woman wearing a headscarf.

**Grundriss:** Good evening. Are you the—

**Amiable Old Lady:** The bartender? Think of me as the stand-in for now.

Without even looking, she swivels around and grabs a bottle of vodka from the top shelf.

And with a twist of the cap, a clean, somewhat sweet fragrance pours out.

Then apple chunks, rosemary, and cinnamon are dropped into a wooden mortar and crushed with a pestle.

And in a few short moments, the drinks are ready.

She places the light-yellow cocktails on the bar as if she's done it countless times before.

**Amiable Old Lady:** I call this one Apple Pie. Please, enjoy.

**Cornerstone:** I like apples. I like cocktails. I love this already.

Cornerstone picks up her glass and takes a sip.

The blend of apple and cinnamon instantly brings her back to her days sitting in her grandmother's kitchen.

**Cornerstone:** Mm! Amazing! It tastes exactly like apple pie!

**Cornerstone:** "Stand-in"? My left foot! Normally, I'd be craving a java refill by now, but this liquid dessert's woken me right up. You're the real thing, ma'am.

The old lady can't help but give a girlish giggle, a contrast to the deep crow's feet etched into the corners of her eyes.

**Amiable Old Lady:** Oh, hahaha. Thank you very much, dear. There's no higher praise for a bartender than a customer's smile.

**Amiable Old Lady:** I do hope you enjoy your stay here in Nowa Miedź.

With that, she turns around and gets back to tending the bar.

**Cornerstone:** A building full of mysteries, a strange puppet, and a grandmotherly old lady slinging the best cocktail I've ever had ... It all feels so unreal.

**Cornerstone:** But no. This is reality. It's just that I haven't seen much outside the Foundation since the anomaly changed everything.

While Cornerstone muses about the experience, Grundriss places her notebook on the table.

**Grundriss:** Since we're waiting for Olga, let's make good use of our time.

**Grundriss:** I have some questions for you. Now, to be blunt, these might not be easy to answer.

**Grundriss:** But it'll help us and the Foundation get a better understanding of what we're dealing with.

**Grundriss:** Of course, you are under no obligation to respond to any question you're uncomfortable with.

She pops off the cap of her pen and looks to Jasio and Lucjan.

**Grundriss:** Now, would each of you be able to give me an account of what you experienced during the anomaly?

**Grundriss:** I'm not expecting anything formal. Just tell me what you felt, what you noticed.

**Jasio:** ...

This topic is clearly a terrifying one.

After a prolonged silence, Jasio finally speaks.

**Jasio:** It was unsettling.

**Jasio:** Everything looks more or less the same but feels completely different.

**Jasio:** Same old streets, same old shops, but the people passing by? Total strangers. Heck, even the stray dogs don't look the same.

**Jasio:** It wasn't long after that I realized that I'd lost everything: money, connections, my past, maybe even my future too.

**Jasio:** People assume I'm new around here, but I know every corner of this city like the back of my hand.

He tilts back his head and downs his cocktail in one big gulp.

**Jasio:** I had a job, I had benefits, a life here—all of it melted away like a puddle under the sun.

**Jasio:** And I'm left stuck in this "new" world.

**Jasio:** But I've been changed too. Now I'm just a drunk, clinging to useless memories of a long-gone future.

**Cornerstone:** It's like the city's moved on without you, isn't it? Like you don't belong anymore.

**Grundriss:** ...

A long moment later, Grundriss pulls herself back from her thoughts.

Her pen remains hovered over the paper, not yet sure enough to be set down.

**Grundriss:** I'm glad to see I wasn't the only one who felt that way.

**Grundriss:** They said we survived the rain, but we were wiped away too. We're just the ones that remember the world that abandoned us.

Nobody dares speak after that.

And in the silence, an invisible force brings their emotions together to rise and mingle with the night's lights and shadows.

An hour later …

An hour later …

Before they know it, it's already midnight.

Thanks to the vodka, the lights have grown softer, their once-clear boundaries melting away. Now, everyone is chatting and clinking glasses like old friends.

**Jasio:** So you've lost your homes too? I thought big organizations like yours would take care of that.

**Grundriss:** Even the Foundation couldn't have anticipated a disaster of this scale.

**Cornerstone:** I haven't had a chance to check on my apartment in Zurich. I hope it's still there. You've no idea the hoops I had to jump through to get those Corbusian windows installed.

**Lucjan:** Ms. Bkornblume told us you're an architect. You must've designed all kinds of houses.

Lucjan, sipping a glass of milk, seizes the chance to indulge his curiosity.

**Cornerstone:** I did, actually. Before joining the Foundation, I was a residential architect.

**Lucjan:** Could you maybe design a house for me? Just a small one would be enough.

**Lucjan:** That way, when my parents come back, we can have a real home together.

His voice lowers to a near whisper.

**Lucjan:** I-I don't have any money right now, but I'll make some, enough to pay for everything.

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Cornerstone:** Usually I don't take commissions from children. Forgive me, you don't make the most reliable clients. But—

She looks at the people in the bar.

She doesn't recall any of their exhausted faces appearing in her childhood, yet they're all so familiar.

If she hadn't left, they would've been her neighbors and friends. Nowa Miedź was, after all, always a small city.

**Cornerstone:** Maybe this is a chance to give back to the city that raised me.

**Cornerstone:** Go ahead. Tell me what you want. I think we still have some time to sketch something out before Olga arrives.

**Lucjan:** Mmm.

Lucjan looks down at his glass with a pensiveness beyond that of a boy his age.

**Lucjan:** My mom really likes nature. So, the house should be near the woods.

**Lucjan:** And I always like watching water run down from the hills. Maybe it could have some big windows overlooking a stream.

**Lucjan:** No, wait! We could build the house right over it. That way, I can look down and see the water running underneath my feet.

**Grundriss:** There it is. Let your client list out their demands, and there's no holding back their imagination.

Cornerstone, however, doesn't flinch.

**Cornerstone:** Hmm, but I like that. In fact, this design of yours reminds me of an architect famous for bringing nature into his buildings.

**Cornerstone:** I've got just the idea.

Cornerstone pulls out her sketchbook and flips to a blank page.

Her strokes are quick but not rushed.

Before long, a clear sketch takes form.

**Cornerstone:** There it is, your very own "Fallingwater"!

**Lucjan:** Wow!

**Cornerstone:** I based the design on the work of a famous architect, a man called Frank Lloyd Wright. But yours is a little smaller, and I adjusted the cantilevers a bit.

**Cornerstone:** That way, we can make better use of the hilly terrain for support.

**Cornerstone:** It's less extravagant but more durable and, above all, cheaper to maintain!

Cornerstone can't help but ramble on about her ideas, not that Lucjan minds.

**Lucjan:** Ah, it's beautiful, like a little palace. You really are amazing!

**Lucjan:** But I bet it'll take forever to save enough for a house like that.

**Cornerstone:** You'll get there. And when you do, just let me know.

She slides her business card to her future client.

**Cornerstone:** I might even give you a discount on my usual fees. But let's be clear. I call all the shots.

**Cornerstone:** So? What do you say? It's a dream for both of us. You get your house, and I get to see my design approved without any client revisions for once.

Lucjan tucks the card, a promise of a dream fulfilled, into his pocket.

**Lucjan:** Yes, ma'am. That's a promise.

**Resident I:** Would you look at that! I've never seen such a nice house! Could you draw one for me, too?

It seems the draft has pulled the attention of the bar's other patrons.

Some rise from their seats, some scooch over with their drinks in hand, and some are pushed away by the lively crowd. Even the loners in the corners can't help but crane their necks in hopes of catching a peek.

**Resident II:** All white and facing the sea.

**Resident III:** I just want a small place with a garden, maybe a few trees.

**Bkornblume:** I've been thinking about getting a new safe house, hidden, secure, maybe with a small patch for growing cornflower.

**Cornerstone:** Then we'll open up a window and let some light in.

And just like that, the heavy atmosphere of the bar is rewritten.

Now, they happily discuss houses, windows, gardens, and ocean waves.

As if speaking on them could will the sketched homes into reality.

Perhaps they might bathe gray walls in morning sunlight, or welcome the sounds of the tide into spacious rooms, or urge the trees in the garden to bear fruit right on schedule.

**Cornerstone:** Listen. I'll do my best to design what you want. But remember, it's only a draft. Don't you go building it without talking to an engineer first!

Cornerstone, now fully surrounded, feels as though she's a child on the swings again.

She finishes her cocktail and slams the glass down.

**Cornerstone:** No pushing now! One at a time!

**Resident I:** \*snore\*

The tide of excitement has finally receded.

Of their party, one has succumbed to the liquor, another retreated to her dark monitoring room, and the last has fallen asleep on the job.

**Grundriss:** Build me ... Irinei's office ...

Cornerstone leans back in her chair, head tilted toward the never-ending triangles that shape the ceiling.

**Cornerstone:** There you are again, Plato's triangles.

**Cornerstone:** When joined together, you can support the entire structure above us, forgoing the need for pillars.

**Cornerstone:** Even better, you provide an excellent way for designers to hide lighting and ventilation systems from view.

**Cornerstone:** But how did you manage to support that dome?

She taps her pencil against her sketchbook, and her vision turns hazy, caught between paper and geometry.

...

**Amiable Old Lady:** Your coffee, Stasia.

Snapped out of her reverie, Cornerstone leans forward and takes a sip.

**Cornerstone:** Thank you. Mm! Perfect acidity. Naturally processed beans, I take it?

**Cornerstone:** You've got excellent taste.

The old lady gives a gentle chuckle.

**Amiable Old Lady:** Hahah. How does it feel to be back in your hometown after so many years?

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Cornerstone:** It's like designing a building.

**Cornerstone:** You think you know everything already until you're there. That's when you find all the things you didn't know that you didn't know.

**Cornerstone:** But, as you go deeper, familiarity resurfaces, and you start to accommodate what's real with what you only imagined.

**Cornerstone:** But you still swing between the two extremes—reality and imagination. Sometimes, it seems like they can't be reconciled.

Though the alcohol has slowed her mind, she finally catches onto a peculiar detail.

**Cornerstone:** Hold on. You said "Stasia." How did you know to call me that?

The old lady doesn't answer.

She just reaches out and pats Cornerstone's head.

**Amiable Old Lady:** It's getting late, dear. Get some sleep. Leave the rest of your designs for tomorrow.

**Cornerstone:** I can't go. I'm still expecting someone, a woman named Olga.

**Amiable Old Lady:** Olga? Why would you want to talk to her?

**Cornerstone:** I need to ...

Not even caffeine can save her.

The vodka has won. Her synapses may as well be covered in molasses.

**Cornerstone:** I need to talk her into rebuilding the dome. I need her help to bring people together.

The old lady can't help but laugh.

**Amiable Old Lady:** Silly girl. You've spent the whole evening designing houses for almost everyone here.

**Amiable Old Lady:** You don't need Olga to win their hearts. You already have.

Her eyes are losing focus.

The old lady's silhouette blends with the neon lights, turning her into a sort of jigsaw puzzle of misaligned pieces from different eras.

An inexplicable yet powerful hunch forms in Cornerstone's mind.

**Cornerstone:** Ah! It's you! You're Olga.

Right as the world goes dark, she hears a response.

**Amiable Old Lady:** I'm just a stand-in bartender. Sometimes, people call me "boss."

**Olga:** But, if you like, yes, you can call me Olga.
