## Story

**Critters:** —!!

The Critters shriek and scurry away.

**Cornerstone:** Didn't see that coming.

**Pale Boy:** Critters like to hang out around the Echo Stones.

**Pale Boy:** That was impressive. I didn't realize you were real arcanists. I thought you were the types that doodle on paper and never solve anything.

**Cornerstone:** Things change. These days, an architect has to know more than how to draft a good plan if they want to be taken seriously.

**Cornerstone:** Wouldn't you agree, Ms. Grundriss?

She shoots a grin at her unamused colleague.

**Grundriss:** Same goes for site supervisors. We need more than just some math and engineering to push back against our architect's grand flights of fancy.

**Cornerstone:** Well said. You know, that is exactly why I like working with you.

An afterthought, spoken as she traces the stones with her fingers.

**Cornerstone:** If the dome was built with Echo Stones, then ...

**Cornerstone:** Hm. Alright. We appreciate your help, gentlemen. That's all I need for now.

She rises to her feet and turns to face the others.

**Cornerstone:** The rest of the building and its materials seem ordinary enough. So far, the only notable things are the dome and these Echo Stones.

**Cornerstone:** If there's a secret behind this building's immunity, my bet is it's in them.

**Jasio:** Bad news for you, uh? It's just a bunch of rubble now.

**Grundriss:** Unfortunate, yes, but not unexpected.

Grundriss quickly stows her files. She's more than eager to finish up.

**Grundriss:** I'll start drafting our report. Then, we can book our flight and—

**Cornerstone:** What if we rebuilt it?

**Grundriss:** Sorry, what?

Cornerstone walks to where the dome would have once stood overhead, ignoring Grundriss's wide-eyed stare.

She squints into the sunlight pouring down from above, fingers coming together to make a triangle.

**Cornerstone:** This building holds a very special message.

**Cornerstone:** In almost every open, sunlit area, the main hall especially, you can see an emphasis put on basic shapes.

**Cornerstone:** Circles, rectangles, triangles ...

**Cornerstone:** These shapes are repeated and combined to form new structures: straight-lined vaults, rectangular rooms symmetrical along a central axis, floor slabs divided into equilateral triangles ...

**Cornerstone:** Whoever designed this place clearly took a liking to them. Now, based on how those concrete beams are arranged and the shape of the opening in the roof ...

She takes a breath before delivering her conclusion.

**Cornerstone:** I think it's safe to assume that the dome was circular on top, with a triangular section supporting it beneath.

**Grundriss:** I don't disagree. But let me remind you, Master Architect.

**Grundriss:** We're here to investigate the building's immunity, not repair a broken roof.

**Cornerstone:** But what if we can replicate the immunity by fixing the dome? Wouldn't that help us understand how it came to be?

**Cornerstone:** Imagine it: we recreate the original plans, rebuild the dome, and in the process solve the mystery at the heart of this place. Exciting, don't you think?

**Grundriss:** "We"?

Grundriss's eye twitches at the suggestion.

**Grundriss:** You mean the three of us here? Even with our arcane skills put together, there's no chance we could rebuild that thing by ourselves.

**Grundriss:** You do know the Foundation's entire construction department was wiped out in the rain, don't you? And we can't even hire local labor. Confidentiality protocol strictly forbids it.

**Cornerstone:** Ah, true.

The unanticipated rebuttal brings the runaway train to a screeching halt.

But only for a moment.

**Cornerstone:** Then, what about the people here? They've already seen the anomaly. Hiring them wouldn't break any rules, would it?

**Cornerstone:** The dome isn't large. With the geometry and structure worked out, leading the construction wouldn't be too tricky. I mean, it isn't too hard to imagine—the people here used to be able to put up an apartment in under ten days.

Words flow out, eyes light up, and the train keeps chugging.

**Cornerstone:** Ms. Bkornblume did mention that the survivors were eager for work.

**Cornerstone:** Take this gentleman here. You wouldn't pass up a job, would you?

**Jasio:** ...?

**Grundriss:** You can't be serious. We don't have any contacts in this city. How would we even go about sourcing the materials? How many of these people do you think have ever even stepped foot on a construction site?

**Jasio:** Eh, I worked in a packing plant before. Can't be too different, right?

**Jasio:** I've hauled heaving slabs of pork around. At least, these bricks won't stink.

**Grundriss:** This is precisely what I mean.

Despite her anger, Grundriss controls her tone—a skill honed through years of strict self-discipline and projects alongside Cornerstone.

**Grundriss:** Alright, discussion over. No more pointless dreaming over impractical and implausible goals.

**Grundriss:** According to the Foundation's mission procedures, we're already overdue for our first field report.

**Grundriss:** If you really feel the need to, go ahead and include this grand reconstruction plan you've dreamed up. Who knows? They might fall for it.

**Kenna:** This must be a new record. A whole three days and fifteen hours before the first major argument. You two are finally starting to get along.

Even over the communicator, they can hear the hint of a smile.

Kenna is all too familiar with the pair's strained working relationship.

**Cornerstone:** Actually, ma'am, we only met up earlier today. We didn't even last three hours.

**Kenna:** Either way, I appreciate your efforts. Grundriss has already delivered a comprehensive report.

The smile in her voice subsides.

**Kenna:** But with the dome gone, whether the building remains immune has become an open question.

**Kenna:** If we were to repair it ...

With the long and tense discussion having come to an end, Cornerstone stows the communicator and returns to her colleague.

**Grundriss:** Took you long enough. I take it you've managed to persuade your commissioner to your side once again?

**Cornerstone:** She wants us to mobilize the residents first and see how many laborers we can gather. That way, she'll have some leverage in the meeting room.

**Cornerstone:** As for the rest of our conversation, I'd really love to talk it through with you, if you're interested.

The banter is over, it seems. Cornerstone is all business.

**Cornerstone:** I know you have your doubts about the reconstruction, perhaps even about the entire investigation.

**Cornerstone:** But before we get to that, I need to speak to the leader of the survivors here, alright?

**Pale Boy:** Heh. What are you up to now?

A voice from behind takes them by surprise.

**Cornerstone:** You mean Grundriss and me? Well, we're—

**Pale Boy:** We've had visitors from the West before, you know.

He approaches Cornerstone.

**Pale Boy:** You come in all tall with your noses up in the air and your fancy cameras and briefcases and sit around in meetings, saying you'll bring "improvements" and a "better future" for this place.

**Pale Boy:** And after a few days or months, once your money's all gone, you'll leave, like we never even existed.

**Pale Boy:** You'll just throw us away, like we're some ... some ...

He frowns and trails off, perhaps lost in an unpleasant memory.

**Cornerstone:** I understand. I really do.

**Cornerstone:** Like I said, we'll talk to your leader first. If all you want is to be left alone, we'll be gone by tomorrow.

**Cornerstone:** But I also want you to understand what you might be giving up. If we can figure out how the dome contributes to this building's immunity ...

**Cornerstone:** Everyone here will stand a better chance if the rain comes again.

**Cornerstone:** The chances aren't high, if I'm being honest. But if we do nothing and leave, it's possible no one will survive next time. It's as simple as that. Now, what do you say?

Logically speaking, it's practically an indisputable fact.

**Pale Boy:** ...

He looks down at his feet.

**Pale Boy:** Sorry. I shouldn't blame you for what others did. My mom would never forgive me for being so rude.

**Pale Boy:** Still, maybe you have your reasons to want to rebuild it right now. But you could just as easily change your mind later.

**Cornerstone:** I won't. I won't give up. Not on this.

Cornerstone kneels to look the boy in the eye.

**Cornerstone:** Because giving up halfway means I started with doubt, that I didn't have faith in the project.

**Cornerstone:** It would be a betrayal of my firmest convictions.

**Cornerstone:** To me, once a project is begun, it must be finished or else never started at all. There's no in-between.

**Pale Boy:** ...

**Pale Boy:** I can respect that.

He turns and leaves, his expression notably softer.

Once he reaches the exit, he stops and looks back at Cornerstone.

**Pale Boy:** If you want to tell everyone about your plans, I'll take you to where we eat. Olga should be there. If we have anything like a leader, it'd be her.

**Cornerstone:** Oh, that sounds perfect. Please, lead the way, uh ...

**Lucjan:** Lucjan. You can call me Lucjan, miss.

**Cornerstone:** Lucjan?

A fuzzy thought makes its way into her head, but before it can fully take form, it's gone.

So Cornerstone simply nods and smiles at him.

**Cornerstone:** Ah, could you give us a moment before we head off? We'd better fetch Ms. Bkornblume first.

**Cornerstone:** Oh, I'm already getting excited. It's been ages since I had proper hometown food. And if Olga's there—

**Cornerstone:** It'll certainly be a night to remember.

**Honza:** —!!

**Honza:** Bad news! News most badly-bad! I listened-heard it all! The enemy's "dexclamation" of war!

**Honza:** They were out-loud plotting about—the naughty plots they have! Plans for concrete, structures, and something finished!

**Honza:** Plans to gather the people here; here together they'll plan—

Behind a damaged wall, Honza relays what he witnessed, exactly as he witnessed it, of course.

**Everecho:** \*gasp\* No. Then it has finally come to this.

**Everecho:** I have had many nightmares about this day. But I never imagined the devil to be this-this ...

**Honza:** Never-ever imagined it to be a four-eyed devil, neither.

**Everecho:** ...

The light in Everecho's body fluctuates, stormy and turbulent.

**Cornerstone:** Hm, I don't know. The way she spends all day and night in that dim-looking station she's set up—it's all a bit unsettling.

**Cornerstone:** You've known her for years, haven't you? You must know about what she's monitoring or who her supervisor is. Do you think she can hear us right now?

**Grundriss:** Hah. I think we've stayed friends all these years because I know exactly when to ask and when to keep my mouth shut.

Honza hears chattering voices, which can only mean trouble is coming.

**Honza:** Four-eyes incoming! Eyes-four come-inning! Hide away, find a way!

The two unusual residents make themselves scarce.

An icy silence hangs in the air.
