## Story

A knock at the door pulls Constantine from her latest pile of work.

**Constantine:** Come in. Ah, Ms. Kenna, it's been a while.

**Constantine:** Is this about the progress of the New Office Construction Project? I thought I instructed that the engineering department was to report to Commissioner Irinei now.

Kenna places a stack of reports on the desk, looking grave.

**Kenna:** Not quite, ma'am. I'm actually here about the LSCC's preliminary survey report on the origin site of the Temporal Anomaly.

**Kenna:** Despite the site itself being affected by the anomaly, Laplace's researchers detected residual energy at its "eye"—the point identified by Admiral Igor.

**Kenna:** Following that, they set up Kirlian cameras at various points around the world and captured some unusual images.

**Kenna:** Analysis suggests that currently unidentifiable substances were present in the observed environments, substances they think are likely linked to the Temporal Anomaly itself.

**Kenna:** Whether they are the cause or a consequence of it remains to be confirmed, but one conclusion is already clear ...

She steadies herself with a breath before continuing.

**Kenna:** Everything suggests the Temporal Anomaly may happen again.

**Kenna:** The exact timing cannot yet be determined. The LSCC will continue monitoring and provide real-time updates.

**Constantine:** I see.

**Kenna:** Ms. Simone is presently reviewing the documents ahead of the upcoming meeting.

**Kenna:** As a project leader on the defense against the Temporal Anomaly, I was given privileged access to this development ahead of any formal announcement. But I thought you should know too.

Constantine taps her fingers on her desk as she processes the information.

**Constantine:** How thoughtful of you, Ms. Kenna. Of course, you already know that I would be apprised of important matters like this long before they reach anyone else's ears.

**Constantine:** There's something else you wish to discuss with me, I presume?

**Kenna:** Sharp as ever, ma'am.

She smiles and opens the door once more to wave into the hallway.

From it, a cautious guest enters, bringing with her the scent of cornflowers.

**Kenna:** Allow me to introduce Ms. Bkornblume, our field investigator sent to examine the Point Irreversible in Nowa Miedź.

**Bkornblume:** Good afternoon, Madam Constantine.

Though Bkornblume takes the time to greet her with a slight bow, her tone hints at urgency.

**Bkornblume:** It's been 15 hours since I delivered the dome model from Nowa Miedź.

**Bkornblume:** May I ask if you've had a chance to review it? I also have a supplementary report regarding—

Constantine leans back in her chair, resting her chin on the back of her joined hands.

**Constantine:** I have. You've successfully reawakened the Echo Stones, a considerable achievement. But I'm afraid that alone won't be enough to overturn the Committee's decision at this stage.

**Bkornblume:** But ma'am, the team put in tremendous effort and sacrifice to get this far, and now it's finally working.

Having never visited this intimidating office before, Bkornblume can't help but hold back a little.

**Bkornblume:** Could you perhaps consider revisiting this project with the Committee and—

**Constantine:** I'm sorry, but we've already determined that the project is neither safe nor stable. It's simply nonessential when compared to the immediate, unpredictable threats posed by Manus Vindictae.

**Bkornblume:** ...

A pat meets her shoulder, and a voice relieves the pressure.

**Kenna:** In my opinion, it is precisely because it is "nonessential" that the project carries significant weight.

**Constantine:** Oh? Do elaborate.

**Kenna:** Funding, manpower, equipment ... We're currently funneling all of our available resources into these "essential" countermeasures.

She weighs her words for a moment, then continues.

**Kenna:** But, with all due respect, ma'am, our long-term success can't rely solely on what is deemed "essential."

**Constantine:** You're accusing the Foundation of being too conservative.

**Kenna:** I wouldn't call it an accusation, just a concern.

**Kenna:** The Foundation decided to make survival our top priority, but survival isn't a goal; it's a prerequisite.

**Constantine:** ...

Kenna takes a tape from Bkornblume's hands.

**Kenna:** I haven't come here intending to challenge the Committee's decision, Madam Constantine. I only want to bypass the usual formalities and speak with you directly.

**Kenna:** Thank you for your time.

She places the tape and a thick stack of files on the corner of Constantine's desk, then leaves with Bkornblume in tow.

**Constantine:** ...

**Constantine:** "The project carries significant weight precisely because it's nonessential"? Hmph, pure sophistry.

Constantine's eyes rest on the tape for a moment, then she stands and picks it up.

She feeds it into the projector.

The machine hums to life, casting a blurry image on the wall.

It's Cornerstone.

She's standing in front of the camera, the wind blowing her hair about.

Truth be told, I must have planned a hundred versions of this speech before I started recording.

I had mountains of documents, photos, and sketches to showcase.

But then I thought to myself: why not showcase the people here instead?

She takes a few steps back to give a better view of the construction site.

The busy workers don't even notice the camera rolling.

There's a saying that goes something like: "No prophet is accepted in his own homeland." I started to think about what that really meant after watching the residents working for us here.

They surpassed all my expectations. From the very first day, they knew they'd been given a near-impossible task.

Yet they responded with action, and they saw it through.

Together, we overcame one hurdle after another: refining the sketches, building the model, tearing down walls ...

Which is why I am enormously proud to share this extraordinary sight with you.

Please, take a moment to listen to the sounds here—the clanging of hammers, the whistling of the wind through the atrium, the dust flickering in the light ...

It's not tidy, not quiet; it's loud and rough, and yet there is a synergy—a harmony—in this place.

It's like the stones of Machu Picchu. Their imperfections are their strength, and layer by layer we've formed the sturdiest of foundations for our work here.

She breathes in and smiles.

In my humble opinion, this project is not some tentative whim. No, it is a sober reckoning.

Are we still willing to act in the face of uncertainty, or will we passively wait to be pushed into compromise?

More Echo Stones have reawakened, which means this path is not a dead end. No, the true dead end is when we decide to stop moving forward.

Hardening our shells is not necessarily the more rational choice. It merely defers the risk, pushing it to a horizon we have chosen to ignore.

If the Foundation does nothing but build more and more walls around our little islands of security, then it won't matter if the rain returns or the world disappears around us. Because they won't need to break our walls down if we smother ourselves inside them.

So, in a sense, this project is not just about the reconstruction of one building. It's about whether we still choose to be an organization willing to stake a claim on the future.

The projector's wheel keeps spinning, and the image on the wall flickers.

But Cornerstone's message is clear as can be. It has cut through the construction site's noise and dust and reverberated through Constantine's office.

**Constantine:** ...

She turns her attention to the stack of files. Calculations, photos, messy sketches ... and on top of them all, a worn notebook.

She flips through its pages. Each one is a detailed record of the day's events, stretching from the project's launch to the present.

They start with complaints.

18. April 1996.

Cloudy.

Day three in Nowa Miedź.

The river, the roads, the fog ... Maybe it's only a reflection of my mood, but this whole place seems grim.

18. April 1996.

Cloudy.

**Grundriss:** Day three in Nowa Miedź.

**Grundriss:** The river, the roads, the fog ... Maybe it's only a reflection of my mood, but this whole place seems grim.

**Foundation Officer:** Could I have a word with you, Bkornblume?

**Bkornblume:** Certainly.

She bids farewell to Kenna and follows the officer to a secluded corner.

**Foundation Officer:** Why is it that I've heard that you went straight to Laplace upon your return, then ran around headquarters requesting all kinds of meetings with Committee members, including Madam Constantine?

**Foundation Officer:** Didn't I tell you to remain neutral throughout this mission? You were supposed to observe, not get involved.

**Bkornblume:** Ma'am, what is the purpose of my maintaining neutrality?

**Foundation Officer:** To prevent division within the Foundation.

**Foundation Officer:** Don't tell me you haven't noticed it. The cracks are already there, still faint for now.

**Foundation Officer:** But if we allow them to deepen, it could spell disaster. We can't afford disunity, not now, especially not at a time like this.

Bkornblume only lowers her head.

Yet there's a clear persistence in her silence. They've come too far to back down now, at this most critical of junctures.

**Bkornblume:** Forgive me, ma'am, but I would say my outreach was within those mission parameters. Yes, only a few young Committee members currently support the project, but still ...

**Bkornblume:** This could be a call to unity, not division.

**Foundation Officer:** A fleeting call.

**Foundation Officer:** A fragile and potentially false hope.

**Foundation Officer:** It's simply naive to expect it to last.

The smile in Bkornblume's eyes disappears as they narrow.

**Bkornblume:** But even if it doesn't last long, that doesn't mean it isn't worth fighting for.

**Bkornblume:** And if real divisions do arise in the future, we'll be able to remember the moments where we stood side by side for the same goal.

**Bkornblume:** For me, that's enough.

**Foundation Staff Member:** Excuse me, Ms. Bkornblume.

A third voice interrupts them.

**Foundation Staff Member:** Thank goodness you haven't gone too far.

**Foundation Staff Member:** Please return to Madam Constantine's office. She wishes to discuss the Nowa Miedź project with you.

**Bkornblume:** ...!

Her eyes go wide.

Perhaps their efforts haven't been for naught after all.

**Bkornblume:** I'll be there right away.
