## Story

8. May 1996.

Sunny.

16 days into the project.

Things have gotten bumpier. It turned out that the west wing had far less usable material than we initially hoped. Each wall only yielded a few intact stones.

In the end, we had to take down walls from the residential wing to get the numbers we needed.

The Echo Stones, once slumbering by the residents' bedsides, now lie in carts to be transported.

Grundriss stands off to the side, taking inventory of the stones.

**Grundriss:** 48, 49, 50 ...

**Grundriss:** That's the last of them. Finally, we have all the materials gathered. All that's left is for Cornerstone to fix the lighting issue.

**Grundriss:** And get a model together, then construction will be officially underway!

With that, she snaps the file shut.

**Grundriss:** I'm sorry that we had to move you out of the residential wing, ma'am. We were hoping to keep the construction's impact on everyone to a minimum.

**Olga:** Oh, not to worry, dear. Personally, I think we'll all sleep a little easier without those Echo Stones in the walls.

**Olga:** And, as we agreed, you'll replace them with ordinary bricks, yes?

**Grundriss:** Right, of course.

**Grundriss:** Still, I appreciate your support. I thought you'd never agree to this, or at least that you'd want more compensation.

Olga smiles and waves her hand.

**Olga:** Ah, we would never.

**Olga:** Even if we don't say it much, everyone here wants to see that dome rebuilt.

**Olga:** Some of them were reluctant at first, but after spending time working on the project, they've come around to it. That's just how people are.

**Grundriss:** ...

Grundriss's eyes go wide, like she'd never considered the possibility.

**Grundriss:** For us, it's just another project. But for you, this is your home being turned upside down.

**Grundriss:** I should have appreciated that sooner.

**Olga:** But you've chatted with almost all of us now, haven't you? That's far more than any other engineer ever bothered to do before.

Grundriss is stunned once more. But then, she smiles.

**Grundriss:** Thank you for understanding. I promise I'll build you a better, quieter wall at the end of all this.

**Grundriss:** Now, you'll have to excuse me. I have a few things to go over with Cornerstone.

She turns and begins making her way left.

**Olga:** You're heading the wrong way, dear.

**Olga:** She's in the residential quarters, the temporary one.

**Olga:** There's a small problem with the lighting there, so we asked her to give it a quick lookover.

**Cornerstone:** I thought this place might be in need of some more natural light.

**Cornerstone:** But this-this is much worse than I imagined, hardly livable at all.

**Jasio:** We're only staying here while construction's underway, but we didn't dare tear an opening in the wall without checking with you first.

**Jasio:** Oh, that glowing girl you caught is in there, too.

**Cornerstone:** I know. Lucjan volunteered to watch her. But I think he just wanted something to do besides hovering by the coffee pot all day.

As she speaks, a dim glow in the distance catches her eye.

**Cornerstone:** Is that a television?

Curiosity gets the best of her, and she approaches.

Kitchen

**Everecho:** Hm?

Everecho and Lucjan are seated on the ground, eyes fixed on a television screen.

Together, Everecho's luminescent body and the fluorescent screen look like a pair of glowing jellyfish floating in the dark sea.

**Cornerstone:** Having fun "watching the prisoner," are we, Lucjan?

**Lucjan:** Mhm. Just watching her watch TV.

His mind is clearly elsewhere.

**Lucjan:** Actually, she chose a good show.

Cornerstone looks at the screen.

It's some kind of bizarre puppet show. She's arrived just in time to see a man open his head and place a fish inside.

**Everecho:** Heheh. That was funny.

**Everecho:** Oh, I wish Honza were here. He would get all sorts of great ideas from this.

The thought of her absent friend brings a frown to Everecho's face.

**Cornerstone:** I think you're already quite talented enough in scaring people. No need to learn any new tricks.

**Cornerstone:** Besides, I'm not sure he'd react all that well to a prank that begins with giving him a craniotomy.

**Everecho:** Crani-on-tony? What is that?

An abstract word, far too complicated for her, but she senses a hint of danger in its syllables.

Her eyes wander to Cornerstone's empty hand.

**Cornerstone:** Your chatty little friend isn't with me right now. But he's safe. You have my word.

**Cornerstone:** I'll free him once the design's complete, like we agreed.

She leaves them be and paces around the room.

Not that there's much room for pacing; from the doorway at one end to the window at the other, there's barely enough space to even turn.

The stove and sink sitting against the wall leave only a narrow path past them.

**Cornerstone:** Hmm. Seems like this used to be a kitchen.

**Cornerstone:** We had a similar place when I was little. I remember my family would crowd together to watch TV or do some reading. We had all kinds of books.

**Jasio:** I'd have thought an architect would hate to be stuck in a cramped place like this.

**Cornerstone:** Hate? Not at all. I quite like it, actually.

**Cornerstone:** It's in spaces like this that you see people's resilience most clearly.

**Cornerstone:** To do your best with so little, that takes real willpower and courage.

**Cornerstone:** Now, about that natural light, I have an idea.

She removes part of the ceiling at the kitchen's center, revealing a grayish-blue sky.

**Jasio:** Oh, that's much brighter.

**Cornerstone:** I lived in an apartment block when I was little. I remember the ceiling hung so low over our heads I thought it might just squeeze us into nothing.

**Cornerstone:** Every day, I'd make a little time to go downstairs and sit on the swing, just to feel the daylight on my skin.

**Cornerstone:** I remember thinking, "What if I could just lift the roof up and let the light pour in ..."

So everyone at home could feel the sunlight?

**Young Cornerstone:** So everyone at home could feel the sunlight?

Her childhood was gray, a dream built from ashes.

The sky always looked as though it carried a layer of grime, and below, rows and rows of gray and white buildings lined the streets.

Yet within all that gray, there was always a sliver of light.

**Róża:** This is so pretty, like that Hagia Sophia place we saw in the magazine.

**Andrzej:** I don't like it. It's too bright! I feel safer in a dark room.

**Young Cornerstone:** I know. I'll get rid of these windows.

**Emil:** But-But I wanna see the stars at night!

**Young Cornerstone:** Alright. Then, I'll add a roof you can see through.

Laughter echoes among the empty buildings. All is soft and slow.

With her friends looking over her shoulder, she sketches building after blocky building, each with an identical facade and veranda, each a similarly imposing obstacle.

As the graphite marks the page, the gray world—an unbuilt fantasy belonging only to her and her friends—breathes.

Somewhere amid the repetitive geometry, she finds a strange sense of peace.

**Cornerstone:** Something as simple as a bit of light can make a room come alive.

**Cornerstone:** I almost forgot I played with this very same idea as a child.

**Cornerstone:** I was younger and bolder then. I raised the ceiling and placed ribbon windows all around.

**Cornerstone:** Like the windowed dome in the Hagia Sophia.

**Cornerstone:** ...

She suddenly falls silent.

**Jasio:** What is it?

**Cornerstone:** I can't believe I didn't see it before.

Still standing in the center of the renovated kitchen, Cornerstone looks up at the newly constructed skylight above her.

**Cornerstone:** If we lift the dome and extend the drum, we can let the light come in from where it meets the ceiling.

**Cornerstone:** And the room will be evenly lit.

An idea springs forth.

With a wave of her finger, materials manifest out of thin air.

One part acts like a crowbar, lifting the cut ceiling section by section, while the rest moves inside, serving as a raised frame.

**Cornerstone:** Mmm. The components align perfectly.

**Cornerstone:** Let's try installing the glass panels.

She conjures glass panels and fixes them into the continuous window frame between the top of the wall and the ceiling.

...

\*click\*

With the last panel in place, the room feels warmer and brighter than ever.

From the kitchenware to the occupants' faces, everything is bathed in a warm glow.

**Cornerstone:** There it is!

**Lucjan:** Whoa! The light's pouring down just like a waterfall!

**Cornerstone:** The light was never coming in from above. It was coming from windows along the sides!

**Cornerstone:** Now, if we were to add it to the dome, it would look something like ...

Cornerstone mutters to herself as her pencil scratches its way across the paper.

**Cornerstone:** Ms. Everecho, would you take a look? Is this the kind of light you used to see?

She gives the sketch to Everecho.

**Everecho:** ...

Sketch in hand, she walks to the space beneath the window, where light and shadow dance at her feet.

Her fingertip traces an aloe vera leaf, and the sunlight rises on the green, almost restoring it to life.

Then she looks up into the warm glow above.

**Everecho:** Ah, the flowing light ...

**Everecho:** It's back.

A Page in the Diary

Saturday - Sunny

A rare good day today! The sky's still gray, but I actually saw the sun. I woke up early, since Róża and I had agreed to meet at the flowerbed. I'd packed sliced black bread and pickles into my bag the night before. I couldn't wait, and after breakfast I ran straight there. Róża was already waiting for me, but Emil and Andrzej were late. We waited ten minutes for them, and Róża talked the whole time.

We took the tram to see the new steel foundry. Emil and Andrzej wanted to see the chimney, and I wanted to sketch. It really was a huge chimney, like some beast breathing coal-fire smoke from its great mouth. Róża said that after all these years, Nowa Miedź finally having a factory this size is a good thing. I drew the chimney, the canal, and a few wildflowers. My river sketchbook keeps getting thicker. Andrzej said he thinks I'll become a river expert. But it's not the canal itself I want to draw, though it matters too, of course. Why do we build our houses by the river? Why do we build our factories by the river? Why does the canal bring more people here? Why does the river turn black? Could we live somewhere better? Róża's explained it all to me before, but I want to find the answers myself. Maybe once I've figured it all out, I can help Mom, Dad, and my friends live better lives.
