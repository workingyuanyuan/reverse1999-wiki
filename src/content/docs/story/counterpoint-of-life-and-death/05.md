## Story

After finishing the installation of the elevated monitoring equipment, Coalwall returns to their temporary outpost.

**Coalwall:** Where's Rachel?

**Marguerite:** Taking temperature readings, she'll be gone for a while.

The reply elicits the precise reaction she had anticipated as Coalwall marches toward her with purpose.

**Coalwall:** I've deciphered the notebook.

**Marguerite:** I'm surprised it took you so long. Though you did make the mistake of asking for that fishbowl's help.

**Marguerite:** What did you find?

**Coalwall:** Initially, I focused on breaking down the equations in the notebook, searching for patterns concealed behind the figures.

**Coalwall:** And yes, I consulted with Mr. Ulrich. He suspected the entire notebook might be itself a kind of elaborate cipher.

**Coalwall:** But then I realized something on my own. Most of these equations were entirely ordinary, just things Annie must have copied down during lectures.

**Coalwall:** There was nothing remarkable about them at all, no ciphers or hidden secrets.

Coalwall takes a deep breath.

**Marguerite:** Go on. It seems like you've thought this through. I'd like to hear it.

**Coalwall:** Then I realized something.

**Coalwall:** All of these notes recorded Annie's entire academic life. From the equations alone, I could tell what classes she attended each day and what topics she was studying. It's clearer than any timetable.

**Coalwall:** It's also clear that at a certain point, her mental health began to deteriorate sharply.

**Coalwall:** Errors start cropping up in her calculations; the margins fill up with corrections. She wasn't able to concentrate on her studies.

**Coalwall:** Eventually, she made a significant computational error. I suspect that was one of the main reasons her research project fell through.

The suffering she once saw on paper now passes through her lips.

**Coalwall:** Something happened to her shortly before her accident. A change in her personality, she wasn't herself anymore.

**Coalwall:** The turning point was a day in late October. That was the day she wrote down your class schedule in her notebook.

**Coalwall:** She was trying to find a time to talk to you.

**Coalwall:** Marguerite, tell me honestly: what did you two talk about that day?!

Marguerite's expression is unchanged despite the frank accusation in Coalwall's tone.

**Marguerite:** After one of my classes in October, 1987?

**Marguerite:** So much time has passed. I can't recall anything specific.

**Coalwall:** I won't let you brush this off anymore!

**Marguerite:** No need to get upset. I'll answer whatever questions you have truthfully.

**Marguerite:** I did hear that, before Annie passed away, she was going through some difficulties.

Marguerite observes her colleague's growing frustration, the anger that tugs at every corner of her face. After allowing the silence to stretch, she speaks again with an unhurried pace.

**Marguerite:** I think I remember. Late October ...

**Marguerite:** I did see Annie around that time.

**Marguerite:** It was the day Ludwig offered me a position at Laplace. Annie overheard our conversation outside the office, and she reacted rather unusually.

**Marguerite:** I suppose it might have been around that time that she really started to withdraw.

**Marguerite:** She had no confidant, no mentor she could trust. As far as I could tell, she was quite lonely.

**Marguerite:** And eventually, that led to her "accident."

**Coalwall:** So, you're saying that, because you left, Annie ultimately chose to end her own life?

**Coalwall:** No. I can't believe that. I simply can't!

**Coalwall:** I may not have known her well, but everything I did know about her tells me she would never have done such a thing.

Despite the protests, she can read the doubt and heartbreak in her expression. She's on the backfoot.

**Marguerite:** Is it really so difficult to imagine? Maybe she wanted a release from all her troubles?

**Coalwall:** What?

**Marguerite:** Nothing. I was just speculating.

**Marguerite:** I simply didn't expect that you would arrive at that conclusion.

**Coalwall:** Oh, no. Don't you dare try to reverse this back to me. What are you implying?

But she can't read anything past her colleague's serene dead-eyed smile. Unsteady in her conviction, she suddenly feels the urge to withdraw.

**Coalwall:** Enough. I don't want to hear it.

**Rachel:** Everything's set up outside! The recorder should already be receiving data.

Rachel's excited voice cuts through the air, her hopeful energy arriving shortly before she does.

**Rachel:** Huh? Were you two arguing? Why does it feel colder in here than outside?

**Marguerite:** Not at all. Researcher Coalwall was feeling a little under-the-weather. I was concerned it might be related to the "Storm" countdown.

**Coalwall:** ...

**Coalwall:** I'm fine. I'm not showing any symptoms of Storm Syndrome.

**Rachel:** We have first-aid supplies on hand. If you feel unwell at all, tell me immediately.

**Rachel:** Wait! What's going on here?!

Marguerite turns toward the recorder at Rachel's side. It is receiving data transmitted from the deep-water probe.

**Marguerite:** That's a massive fluctuation for such a short interval.

**Marguerite:** What's happening outside?

Rachel pulls out a sheet of paper, rapidly converts the signal, and sketches a curve.

**Rachel:** Either the probe is malfunctioning, or we've just detected something extraordinary.

They consolidate the data, and Marguerite writes down a set of highly simplified equations.

Variables entered. Approximation computed. The result is unsettling.

**Coalwall:** This energy value ...

**Coalwall:** It resembles a global climatic phase transition.

**Marguerite:** It's difficult not to associate this with the "Storm."

**Rachel:** But the probe didn't complete its transmission. How is that possible?!

**Marguerite:** Perhaps the sudden shifts in temperature and depth caused the signal to stall.

**Rachel:** A worse possibility is that it never resurfaced at all.

**Rachel:** If that's the case, recovering the probe itself will be extremely difficult. Marguerite, you designed it. What's your assessment?

**Marguerite:** It shouldn't be so fragile that it can't withstand this level of fluctuation.

**Rachel:** S-So the probe itself could be trapped down there?

**Coalwall:** The last signal was at coordinate C023. We should try to recover it.

**Rachel:** I can't see the surface marker at C023. Coalwall, is the submersible hydrophone picking up anything?

**Coalwall:** No release signature or air bubbles detected.

**Coalwall:** The cable is fully taut. I pulled on it, and it didn't budge.

**Coalwall:** Do I have the right read here? Rachel, what's your assessment?

**Rachel:** The probe that detected the anomalous signal is stuck at a depth of thirty-six meters.

**Marguerite:** That's too deep for recovery.

**Marguerite:** Nonetheless, we've already gathered some extraordinary data. It should keep us occupied for quite some time.

Waves roll in under gathering shadows. No one sees Rachel's expression clearly.

She glances at her watch, then stares out toward the rolling sea.

**Rachel:** Researcher Coalwall, Marguerite.

**Rachel:** I'm going to dive down there and retrieve the probe.
