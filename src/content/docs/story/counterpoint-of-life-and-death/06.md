## Story

Temporary Station, Coral Islands

**Coalwall:** Have you lost your mind?!

**Coalwall:** You don't have the training for a deep dive like that! Besides, our suits aren't even rated for that depth!

**Coalwall:** There's no reason to risk your life over a probe!

**Rachel:** It's only a bit over twenty meters down!

**Marguerite:** Thirty-six meters ...

**Rachel:** This probe is of critical importance.

**Rachel:** It's the only instrument that recorded the anomaly. If we can't retrieve the physical unit, we may never get a second chance.

**Coalwall:** This was just our first field expedition with half-baked equipment. Everything is still in its infancy.

**Coalwall:** The recorder clearly captured all the transmitted data leading up to the anomaly. Even without recovering the probe, it will be enough.

**Coalwall:** Why are you trying to get yourself killed?

**Rachel:** Are you really that naive, Coalwall?

**Rachel:** We both know these remote readings are far too unreliable. If we can't retrieve the unit and calibrate it against the baseline probe, then all we have are isolated readings. They would laugh us out of headquarters.

**Coalwall:** But ...

She tries to interrupt, but Rachel, already pulling on her diving suit, continues speaking.

**Rachel:** Without verification, our analysis becomes a mere "hypothesis," or worse, imagination.

**Rachel:** Even Hardy and Ramanujan had to prove their conjectures.

**Rachel:** I must demonstrate ours beyond any dispute. Every rational doubt must be erased. I need tangible proof.

**Marguerite:** "Science is about complete, logical stories, not ambiguous, elusive concepts."

**Rachel:** Hah! That's exactly it!

**Coalwall:** This isn't the time for speeches!

**Coalwall:** The probe might simply be malfunctioning, and this expedition was rushed. We can wait until the next time.

**Rachel:** If we do, we might never encounter data like this again. It could be faulty or it could be real. That's precisely why I need to verify it.

Rachel's gaze softens. She looks at Marguerite.

**Rachel:** You understand me, don't you? Marguerite, I've waited long enough.

Her silence is like a mirror, and in it, Rachel seems to see her own conviction reflected.

**Rachel:** I have always lived my life for science.

**Rachel:** The history of science is filled with brilliant minds. The modern world is built upon that history. After centuries of ignorance, we finally separated myth from materialism.

**Rachel:** And then the "Storm" came, and everything reset to zero.

**Coalwall:** Rachel ...

Rachel lifts her head, looking out over the waves, making a final choice in her heart.

**Rachel:** All those geniuses, mathematicians, and physicists who dedicated their entire lives to discovering and defining the laws of the universe—what was all of it for?

**Rachel:** Plato, Hypatia, Galileo, Darwin ... Brilliant minds that struggled against ignorance for the sake of truth.

**Rachel:** My life, every human life, I can't let that all be washed away by the "Storm."

**Rachel:** I need to prove it still exists. I need to prove all of it.

**Coalwall:** I understand what you're saying, but you need to be alive to—

**Rachel:** Please stop, Researcher Coalwall. Respect my resolve as a scientist.

The words are sharp. There's no room for argument anymore.

**Marguerite:** You're certain you want to take this risk?

**Rachel:** Hah, yes. It's my choice. It feels like the first real choice I've made since the first "Storm."

**Rachel:** Call it fate, hah, or maybe call it "the deterministic ends of an uncaring universe." I don't care. It's my last chance to justify my life's work.

**Marguerite:** Then I won't stop you.

**Coalwall:** Wait. Wait!

A surge of anguish detonates in Coalwall's mind.

She recalls Annie's face swallowed by shadow, and the familiarity terrifies her.

**Coalwall:** Rachel, don't you think this is strange? The timing's too coincidental.

**Coalwall:** Data can be fabricated. Instruments are tamperable. All of this feels so unreal, like someone else was guiding you toward a specific end.

**Rachel:** What do you mean? Coalwall, are you questioning my scientific integrity?

**Rachel:** Or are you accusing Marguerite? Don't be ridiculous!

**Rachel:** I don't know what happened between the two of you back in Cambridge, but I don't have time for this here.

**Rachel:** It's easy to accuse without evidence!

**Coalwall:** No, I ...

Her tongue swells and trips. All she has is intuition, not evidence.

**Coalwall:** I mean that we all know how desperate you are to show science alone is capable of explaining the "Storm," and now this opportunity—it feels too good to be true, too perfect.

**Rachel:** Enough.

**Rachel:** I appreciate your concern, but my decision is made.

**Rachel:** There's nothing more to say. I'm going to retrieve the probe.

**Marguerite:** ...

Equatorial Coral Islands

**Rachel:** Marguerite.

Marguerite quickens her pace to catch up. Dusk is falling as they walk side by side along the shore.

There is no trace of the "Storm" in the sea breeze yet, only the stillness of nature.

**Marguerite:** Hey.

**Rachel:** That paper, Samplings of Global and Regional Chaotic Energy Route Changes, you wrote it, didn't you?

Marguerite stops walking. They hold each other's gaze for a brief moment.

**Rachel:** It had to be you. Only someone with a rare genius could have made those calculations. I wonder if anyone else in the world could have figured it out. No one would have expected you of all people.

**Rachel:** But I saw it. Your statistical habits are identical—small patterns in the way you structure data. It's a pity I never asked you to co-author with me. I might've noticed sooner.

**Rachel:** Lorentz Butterfly is a beautiful pseudonym. I admire the symbolism.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** ...

The pale researcher's face changes to something rarely seen.

She offers a genuine smile.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** In truth, I believe neither science nor technology can unravel the secrets of the "Storm."

**Lorentz Butterfly:** I wouldn't have been much of a co-author. Our ideals stand in direct opposition.

**Rachel:** I see. And now I realize why that component didn't fit right earlier. I assume you modified our probe to fit your own ends.

**Rachel:** So, Lorentz Butterfly, what did you do to it?

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Nothing substantial. I've only added a module designed to monitor Chaotic Energy.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** But there is something else. You know that under our new safety regulations, we have to leave at least ninety minutes before the "Storm." That was our compromise.

**Rachel:** Hah. So that's why it didn't surface.

**Rachel:** Recovering it too early would compromise your observation, just as leaving it down there would compromise mine.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Bring it back at the right time, and you'll have my thanks.

**Rachel:** You mean at the very last moment before the "Storm" sweeps us all away.

**Rachel:** Now I'm curious. What could possibly keep me down there for so long? What else did you leave down there?

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Don't worry. I've ensured you'll have sufficient oxygen.

**Rachel:** You'd better add me as co-author on your next paper.

The words don't land right. Too bitter for a joke, too genuine for a barb.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** I'm afraid your contribution wouldn't warrant co-authorship, Rachel.

**Rachel:** I'd like to think wagering my life might earn a footnote at least.

**Rachel:** But it doesn't matter now, Marguerite. Countless times, you stood firmly, unconditionally, by my side. Now I'll do the same for you.

**Rachel:** Farewell, dear Marguerite.

Her path lies before her. She refuses to wait another second.
