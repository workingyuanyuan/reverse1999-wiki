## Story

A severed rope lies wet at the center of the table.

It once tethered Rachel's diving suit.

Coalwall's every suspicion proven true, and now she sits on a desolate island with the accused.

**Coalwall:** I don't understand why she did it. It was suicide.

Marguerite looks over to her without expression.

**Marguerite:** She was prepared to sacrifice herself for her research.

**Marguerite:** In this era where the "Storm" has stripped science of its meaning, it was her last stand for reason over chaos.

**Marguerite:** Why would you think of stopping her?

**Coalwall:** If she dies because of this, it'll be the end of everything.

**Coalwall:** No future. No possibility. Nothing.

**Coalwall:** Was Annie's "accident" like this as well?

**Marguerite:** Does it matter? Would you want to hear the answer, even if it might destroy you?

**Coalwall:** Of course.

**Coalwall:** This isn't the first time you've implied there's more to her accident. Speak plainly.

**Marguerite:** Have I made any implications? Or have you just been trying to read suspicion into my answers?

**Marguerite:** You want Annie's death to be something other than an accident. You want a culprit to hold responsible.

**Marguerite:** When it comes to Annie, you've never asked me for the truth.

**Coalwall:** ...

Coalwall clenches her fists.

**Marguerite:** Oh! Forgive me. I've finally remembered what else happened that day.

**Marguerite:** That day in late October, 1987. The day Annie came to see me. The "turning point," as you call it.

She pauses, smiling, waiting for Coalwall to ask.

**Coalwall:** What happened?

She lets the question hang in the air, then, as if to toy with her, answers with a question of her own.

**Marguerite:** Do you remember how I correctly predicted that you were coming to Laplace?

**Coalwall:** You said you saw the dispatch list on Simone's desk. My designation was on it.

**Coalwall:** And I'd used that alias to publish a few pieces in the Cambridge student papers.

**Marguerite:** Your memory serves you well.

**Marguerite:** Maybe you remember all the trouble Annie had after those scandals involving her family's business became public?

**Marguerite:** She said her parents' business dealings were just the beginning. They were walking on thin ice with the law and with the tabloids.

**Marguerite:** She wouldn't dare comment on anything political at school or even the slightest bit controversial. So, she stayed quiet—anything—to stay under the radar.

**Marguerite:** Now that I think about it, that month must have been a significant time for you as well.

**Marguerite:** I recall that was the first time the name "Coalwall" appeared in the campus papers.

1987.

**Annie:** Just stop asking, Marguerite. I'm begging you.

She pleads, but the girl before her, her only friend, remains unmoved, pressing her to expose the wound instead.

**Marguerite:** Show me.

**Marguerite:** What did you bring me?

With barely any effort, Marguerite snatches the scrap from her hands.

In the struggle, the paper is torn into pieces and scatters across the floor.

A corner of the latest student newspaper.

**Marguerite:** "I remember the hunger and the cold. I remember the coal dust on my father's hands."

**Marguerite:** "I remember my mother's sobbing cries when news of my father's death came. I remember the rage that crushed me into the earth with the weight of a mountain."

**Marguerite:** Oh. A piece written by a student from a mining family. Both her parents passed away two years ago.

**Marguerite:** That name, how fitting, like a wall of coal rising from the excavation pit, faintly luminous in the dark.

**Marguerite:** Annie, would you care to explain what's going on?

The girl before her shivers fearfully, her hands trembling at her side.

**Annie:** People think I'm just as responsible for their deaths as my father. They think of me as a monster, or at best as a child of monsters.

**Annie:** I don't blame Coalwall. It's been like this since people found out about them. I've been painted with the same brush. They look at me and see a spoiled brat given everything by the worst kind of dirty, dangerous, and exploitative industry.

**Marguerite:** All this article did was give them an excuse to unleash their hatred. It doesn't change anything.

**Annie:** I-I don't know what to do. Marguerite, I'm not sure I blame them. But I didn't choose my family any more than they chose theirs. No one chooses their circumstances.

**Annie:** I saw Coalwall today; I don't think she even noticed me as I walked by.

**Annie:** Seeing her, knowing what she's been through, I don't feel like I deserve anything that I have here.

**Annie:** I can't look at her without feeling like I was the one who let her father die and killed her mother with all that grief and pain.

**Annie:** She had to fight for a full scholarship, just for a chance to stand on equal footing with me.

**Annie:** So maybe I deserve all of that scorn. Maybe that's just what I get for benefiting from her pain.

Her trembling hands slow with exhaustion.

**Annie:** I'm sorry, Marguerite. I shouldn't have troubled you with any of this. You must think I'm being ridiculous.

**Annie:** I ... I should go.

**Annie:** I should never have bothered you in the first place.

She turns and slinks away, looking as pale as a phantom.

Night has fallen as their evacuation timer continues to tick down.

In their tiny outpost, Coalwall reels back, inadvertently knocking over a chair.

**Marguerite:** I never spoke to her after that day.

**Marguerite:** I suppose I did take notice of her change in behavior, but I didn't see how it concerned me. She never reached out to me at any rate.

**Marguerite:** Annie, Annie. She was sweet, really. Probably too sensitive but kind.

**Marguerite:** Then a few weeks later, there was that theft in the lab building—sensitive medical compounds, among them, the kind necessary to induce cardiac arrest.

**Marguerite:** Who can say? It may only have been a coincidence.

**Coalwall:** I ...

Coalwall struggles, suddenly feeling choked with some unrealized emotion.

**Coalwall:** I never meant to make her feel that way. I was bitter, of course, but I never blamed her for what happened.

**Coalwall:** I just didn't want people to forget what happened to my parents.

**Coalwall:** Was it me? Did I cause Annie's death?

**Coalwall:** No conspiracy. No hidden manipulation. Just ... me ...

The other woman shrugs.

**Marguerite:** You were so unsettled by the idea that her death was an accident.

**Marguerite:** Strange. All this time, you've thought I must be guilty in some way.

**Marguerite:** But it's never one thread that pulls the noose. Everything is connected; every word, every action shapes the next, like the flapping of a butterfly's wings.

**Marguerite:** But Annie said it well: no one chooses their circumstances. You couldn't have known that penning a touching memorial to your family would light the fuse that led to her demise.

**Marguerite:** But then, perhaps on some subconscious level, you sensed there was more. That unease you felt led you to look for a culprit.

**Marguerite:** To Annie's only "friend," a reclusive transfer student with an unclear past and habit of writing strange equations on blackboards.

**Coalwall:** It was my indifference that allowed all of this to happen.

**Coalwall:** Because I let them torture her for something she couldn't change.

**Coalwall:** But that still doesn't explain everything. That equation you wrote, I swear it was modeling Annie's behavior. That's why I suspected you.

A chill spreads through her body.

**Coalwall:** Wait, that equation, you wrote it for me to see, didn't you?

**Coalwall:** Right down to my first day at Laplace. You predicted all of this, didn't you?

**Coalwall:** So, what the hell is this? You let me think I was acting on my conscience, that I was searching for the truth, and all the while I was just acting out a formula you had already solved.

**Coalwall:** A study of human behavior and decision-making, but I wasn't even the data. I was the proof, a walking replication.

**Coalwall:** But why? Why would you do this?

**Coalwall:** And what about Rachel? What role did she serve in this sick formula of yours?

**Marguerite:** I encourage you to solve that for yourself. The answer is quite simple, but I would hate to interrupt your process.

Confusion. Disbelief. Incomprehension.

But she excels at solving problems and eliminating impossibilities.

**Coalwall:** Setting aside the risk Rachel faced and all the elaborate staging, your ultimate goal was to delay the probe's retrieval.

**Coalwall:** You let her go out there just to make sure you got reliable data.

Her conclusion defies all reason, yet it seems to be true.

**Coalwall:** So, all of this, all of it was to prove your theories about the "Storm"?

She lets Coalwall study her face without an answer.

And in return, she reads from her an unprecedented mixture of danger and exhilaration.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** You seem upset by this outcome. Allow me to offer a remedy.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** This is our teleport disk, the only means we have of returning safely to Laplace.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Why would I give you it? Can you figure out my motive? I trust you can.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** The decision is yours.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Leave me here to face the "Storm." In this one moment, you have the chance to bypass society, morality, and the law. Pass judgment as you see fit.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Or allow me to return safely to Laplace with you and see whether your hypothesis is correct.

**Coalwall:** You're completely insane.

**Coalwall:** A monster impossible to comprehend.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Well? What will you do?
