## Story

Cambridge University

Cambridge University, 1987.

A cold, damp wind brushes the treetops. At the lectern, Professor Ludwig pauses, momentarily distracted by the rustling.

**Ludwig:** In short, even a system ruled by fixed equations can produce outcomes that appear entirely chaotic.

**Ludwig:** It feels like reading The Phenomenology of Spirit, precision so dense it leaves you lightheaded.

**Ludwig:** To be clear, I am not assigning you any additional philosophy reading. That will be all for today.

The class disperses. As he collects his papers, he observes a small cluster of students lingering.

**Ludwig:** Marguerite.

**Marguerite:** Professor Thoth.

**Ludwig:** Thoth? What an absurdity. I don't even look like an ibis.

**Ludwig:** Humorous nicknames aside. I'm curious: It's been a while since you transferred to my class. How are you adjusting?

**Marguerite:** Everything's going well, thanks to you. As for my coursework, I believe my research reports speak for themselves.

The student before him explains with an air of nonchalance.

**Ludwig:** Yes. On the Atmospheric Neutrino Anomaly and Its Implications for Oscillation Models. An excellent paper, indeed, indeed.

**Ludwig:** Objectively speaking, it is somewhat ahead of its time. Still, physics ought not regress simply because the era lags behind.

**Ludwig:** It appears you've adapted not only academically but to this era as well. My concern was unnecessary.

**Marguerite:** "This era" ...

She turns the unexpected phrase over in her mind.

**Marguerite:** Oh, you haven't forgotten what happened in 1999.

**Marguerite:** "To teach philosophy is the most absurd profession in the world?"

**Ludwig:** That is precisely why I stand here today, in the capacity of a visiting physics lecturer.

**Ludwig:** Cambridge has taken an interest in the research underway at Laplace's Plesetsk Branch and extended an invitation for me to teach when time permits.

**Ludwig:** The events of 1999 cut short my appointment, and with it, my chance to sponsor you.

**Ludwig:** I am pleased to see you also survived the "Storm."

**Marguerite:** You're not curious to know how I survived?

**Ludwig:** Would such knowledge alter your academic progress?

**Marguerite:** Hah, of course not.

**Ludwig:** Then it's none of my concern.

**Ludwig:** There's a lecture on aerospace experiments at the orbital station next week. You're welcome to sit in.

**Marguerite:** Thank you for the invitation, Professor Thoth.

**Ludwig:** Ah, your unique humor strikes again, Marguerite.

**Ludwig:** Three o'clock. Thursday.

Once assured she has noted the details, the avian professor inclines his head courteously and departs.

**Marguerite:** Goodbye, Professor Ludwig.

**Marguerite:** For such a grand enigma, I do hope your every appearance brings a new puzzle with it.

**Marguerite:** Things would be rather dull otherwise.

The transfer student looks over the equations and notes left on the board with a vacant expression.

**Marguerite:** Did you need something?

**Marguerite:** You've been eavesdropping back there for a while now.

A figure rises from her seat, visibly flustered.

**???:** I'm sorry. I didn't mean to. I was only—

**Annie:** I'm Annie.

**Annie:** You're Marguerite, aren't you? The one who wrote On the Atmospheric Neutrino Anomaly and Its Implications for Oscillation Models.

Marguerite looks up and studies the girl addressing her.

In an instant, she gathers what she needs: background, age, circumstances, temperament.

Gentle, timid, and in all respects a model student. Excessively empathetic, poor personal boundaries, a comfortable but ignoble upbringing, socially isolated due to her family's scandalous greed ... These traits combine to form the girl standing in front of her.

And she is not having an easy time with life here.

**Annie:** They all say you're a genius. You transferred in without any prior studies and still managed to write a paper like that.

**Annie:** I-I read your report. It was incredible. I really admire you.

**Marguerite:** There's no shortage of genius in this place.

The transfer student glances at the notebook on the desk. Her intention is not difficult to discern.

A fine leather cover. Meticulous notes. At the bottom: Thursday. Three o'clock.

She says nothing, simply waiting for the girl to finish her performance.

**Annie:** About the lecture Professor Ludwig mentioned, if it's all right, could I go with you?

**Annie:** Some of the third-year students said there may be paired work involved.

**Annie:** Marguerite, I'd like to ask you to go with me.

A tentative, unpracticed smile appears on her mask-like face.

**Marguerite:** Another unexpected development. I accept.

**Marguerite:** Annie ...

**Marguerite:** Annie, Annie, Annie ...

When she returns to herself, the honey-sweet name has already rolled off her tongue.

**Marguerite:** She was an interesting girl. A sweet girl.

**Coalwall:** What?

**Marguerite:** If you truly followed the matter, then you know the police concluded Annie's death was an accident. She had a congenital heart condition. Tragic, but nothing more.

**Marguerite:** Don't misunderstand me. I won't pretend to be oblivious to what you're implying. But I am curious what you're basing this thinly veiled accusation on.

**Marguerite:** You don't strike me as a researcher who speculates without evidence.

**Coalwall:** ...

Coalwall studies her new colleague's stoic and composed face.

**Marguerite:** What's troubling you? This is the Laplace Scientific Computing Center.

**Marguerite:** We're in a safe environment. If something is weighing on you, speak it openly so we can resolve any misunderstanding you might have on the matter.

**Coalwall:** Once, after class, I noticed an equation you left on the board.

**Coalwall:** It had nothing to do with the lecture, and it was no ordinary formula.

**Coalwall:** You wrote it boldly across the center of the board, where everyone could see it.

**Coalwall:** As though you were waiting for someone to notice it.

**Marguerite:** Oh? And what equation was that?

She briskly flips her ash-blonde hair back over her ear as if to encourage Coalwall to continue.

**Coalwall:** I think it was a model for predicting human behavior.

**Coalwall:** Not just any human. Someone you knew well, a particular individual.

**Coalwall:** I think it was for Annie.

**Coalwall:** You were organizing her movements, her decisions, her emotions, her very being, into an iterative mathematical formula.

**Coalwall:** And worse, your equations were complete. They were simple, simple enough for someone like me to understand.

**Coalwall:** What were you intending to do with it?

Marguerite feigns an expression between interest and bewilderment.

**Marguerite:** You're suggesting that I modeled an entire person into a single mathematical formula?

**Marguerite:** An intriguing and rather original hypothesis, Researcher Coalwall.

**Coalwall:** Don't patronize me, Marguerite.

**Marguerite:** Very well. My apologies.

**Marguerite:** If you truly believe there was some hidden motive or culprit behind Annie's accident—

Marguerite stops herself. She reaches among the folders and papers, withdrawing a notebook before handing it over to Coalwall.

The leather is elegant but aged, the paper tinted yellow with time.

Something stirs in Coalwall as she takes the notebook and opens it.

**Coalwall:** This is ...

Neat handwriting. Structured lecture notes.

Orbital station. Three o'clock. Thursday.

**Coalwall:** This is Annie's notebook.

**Coalwall:** I don't understand. Why do you have this?

**Marguerite:** This morning, I saw a dispatch list on Simone's desk.

**Marguerite:** The pen name "Coalwall" appeared frequently in Cambridge's student papers.

**Coalwall:** You predicted all of this from the beginning.

**Marguerite:** And this is the clue you were looking for.

Coalwall hesitates, but the temptation of the truth is too strong to rest on pride. She takes the notebook and leaves.

With Coalwall gone and the room empty, Marguerite slides her empty teacup forward.

A silver kettle descends from the chandelier, tilts slightly, and pours tea neatly into the cup.

**X:** Another pin-pointedly accurate prediction?

**X:** I actually put a surprising amount of thought into choosing between the chandelier-mounted kettle release system and the breadcrumb-launching file apparatus.

The kettle isn't the only thing that descends from above. Soon enough, her hidden colleague lowers himself as well.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** I do hope the next "accident" proves more interesting.

**X:** Whatever you need, just let me know.

**X:** On that topic, is there any dimension of your current research that might benefit from my assistance?

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Xavier, you're clearly not here out of concern for my research.

**X:** Xavier? That name belongs to a stray Eagle rescued from some long-obliterated ruins.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** And? It's likely vanished into the "Storm" by now. Consider the name yours now, Xavier.

**X:** Fine, have it your way. Speaking of Eagle, I meant to check how our eager scout has been doing as your assistant.

**X:** I mean, I was the one that recommended her in the first place. It'd be a real disappointment if she turned out to cause you any trouble.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** Objectively speaking, she's a good kid and a competent assistant.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** If I didn't know that you only handed her over to me because you were too lazy to teach her properly, your concern might come across more believable.

**X:** You are well aware that my pedagogical contributions trend toward the spectacularly impractical.

The two Institutum infiltrators exchange a knowing glance, silently abandoning the subject.

**X:** I noticed you've been unusually invested in this circulation group, so I came to spectate the proceedings for myself.

**X:** You're just looking for a little amusement, aren't you?

**Lorentz Butterfly:** And your chandelier kettle and breadcrumb-launching file apparatus don't qualify as amusement?

**X:** Our definitions are evidently divergent.

**X:** Go ahead and keep playing with your boring circulation team, but when you're free, come help me test my jet-powered inverted glider.

**Lorentz Butterfly:** I intend to be occupied for the foreseeable future.
