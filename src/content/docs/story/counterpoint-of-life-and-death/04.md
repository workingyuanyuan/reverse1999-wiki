## Story

Temporary Station, Coral Islands

**Rachel:** Final equipment check.

**Rachel:** Huh? Why won't this component fit right?

A pair of warm, dry hands settles over Rachel's trembling shoulders.

**Marguerite:** I'll take care of it.

**Marguerite:** It was sooner than we expected, but everything is proceeding according to plan.

**Rachel:** I'm just nervous.

**Rachel:** We don't know what changes the "Storm" might bring in the future. Every occurrence is a rare and unique opportunity.

She takes a deep breath, forcing her hands to steady.

**Coalwall:** Our equipment here is woefully inadequate.

**Coalwall:** Look at these anemometer cups. Some of them are broken.

**Marguerite:** The field application's approval came on short notice. We've improved as much as we could within our time and budget.

**Marguerite:** And we've done it all in a month on a remote coral island in the middle of nowhere—the only place they'd let us try.

**Rachel:** The Foundation's local branch had to source this equipment from civilian suppliers. These things are so out of date we might as well be using coconuts, but we're intelligent, resourceful women. We've got this!

**Coalwall:** All right. Now before we set out, I stuffed a handful of compressed biscuits into my pockets and brought a thermos of hot water for tea.

**Coalwall:** The water's still hot.

**Coalwall:** So, even if we fail to gather any useful observations, at least we won't starve.

**Rachel:** Excellent. Researcher Coalwall has already taken care of our basic survival needs.

**Rachel:** At least we have a functional measuring apparatus. As long as this round of data can prove our hypothesis, we'll undoubtedly be able to apply for a thorough follow-up experiment.

**Coalwall:** And while you two deploy the probe out at sea, I'll set up our trade winds and atmospheric pressure monitoring stations.

**Rachel:** By yourself? That's a long trip up. You know, we could—

**Coalwall:** I work more efficiently alone.

She says with such complete certainty that there seems to be no question as she turns and walks out alone.

Sea, Near the Coral Islands

**Rachel:** Grab the buoy.

**Rachel:** How's it going? Can you manage things? Do you need my help?

**Marguerite:** You don't need to worry. I've got things under control.

**Marguerite:** You know, Rachel, I've noticed you're unusually tense.

**Rachel:** Can you blame me? I've been waiting for this moment for a very long time.

**Rachel:** We've successfully deployed the deep-water apparatus. But honestly, I'm more worried about Coalwall working alone.

**Marguerite:** I'd say she's proven reliable, even just in the short time we've worked together.

**Rachel:** You may be right.

**Rachel:** Still, have you noticed that she's quite harsh whenever she speaks to you?

**Marguerite:** Not especially, no. She has her own kind of way of speaking.

**Rachel:** She definitely comes across quite blunt at times. I think that's just the way she is.

**Rachel:** Even so, I can't shake the feeling that she's especially frosty around you. Am I just imagining things?

**Marguerite:** Hmm ...

Marguerite returns to her work without giving an immediate answer.

**Marguerite:** A mutual friend of ours from Cambridge died in an accident. For whatever reason, Coalwall has always thought I was responsible in some way.

**Rachel:** How could she possibly think you had anything to do with something like that?!

**Rachel:** But that does explain something. I saw Coalwall speaking with the cryptography lead. Y'know that fishbowl-headed Awakened?

**Rachel:** She seemed like she was asking for his help with some equations, maybe decrypting them. Then there's that black leather notebook she's always brooding over at night.

**Marguerite:** I think she means well; she's just trying to verify things for herself.

**Rachel:** Marguerite, I would hope that we can trust one another after all this time.

**Rachel:** No matter what, I'll stand by you.

**Rachel:** But I don't want you getting caught up in anything dangerous.

The water ripples. Their boat lies crosswise on the sea, dividing light from shadow.

Without changing her expression, Marguerite drops the buoy into the water, catching a fragment of memory in the dispersing rings.

**Ludwig:** Come in.

Office, Cambridge University

She steps into the office but does not fully close the door behind her.

**Ludwig:** Please, have a seat.

**Marguerite:** That won't be necessary. Professor Ludwig, may I ask why you wished to see me?

**Marguerite:** I understand your visiting lectureship has concluded. You came all the way from Plesetsk to Cambridge. I assume you have something "important" to inform me of.

The impatience and emphasis in her words fail to reach the other side of the desk.

The raven professor does not answer right away, weighing his choice of words.

**Ludwig:** ...

**Ludwig:** Marguerite, how have you been adjusting lately?

**Marguerite:** This isn't the first time you've asked that question, Professor.

**Ludwig:** It's not such a terrible opening line, is it now? After all, people and circumstances can change when least expected.

**Ludwig:** For instance, have you encountered any problems with your peers?

**Ludwig:** Perhaps I could offer you some assistance.

Exactly the sort of question guaranteed to irritate her.

**Marguerite:** Professor, what exactly are you trying to imply?

**Marguerite:** If you're only extending a courtesy, I have no need for it.

**Ludwig:** Then let us dispense with them.

**Ludwig:** Several days ago, at Foundation headquarters, I came across a newly issued warrant from Zeno.

**Ludwig:** An accused deserter whose description seemed oddly familiar to me.

**Marguerite:** I'm sure it's only a misunderstanding.

**Ludwig:** Perhaps, or it may be that you have your own way of dealing with the matter. I don't intend to probe any deeper.

**Ludwig:** I only hope none of this will affect your future studies. You are a natural-born researcher, Marguerite.

The conversation lapses into a poignant silence, the meaning of the words understated but clear between the two soft-spoken figures.

**Marguerite:** Because of the "Storm"?

**Ludwig:** Because of the "Storm."

**Ludwig:** I know of a place where you would be exempt from Zeno's scrutiny.

**Ludwig:** Would you be interested in joining the Laplace Scientific Computing Center?

As Marguerite leaves the office, she encounters an eavesdropper around the corner. She feigns surprise, although it is anything but.

The girl seizes her sleeve anxiously.

**Marguerite:** What is it, Annie? What are you doing here?

**Annie:** What did Professor Ludwig mean just now? Are you in some kind of danger?

**Annie:** You're not leaving Cambridge, are you?

The half-closed door served its intended purpose, just as predicted.

**Marguerite:** Did you hear all of that?

**Annie:** I ... I was just coming to find you. They told me you'd been called to the professor's office.

**Annie:** So, I decided I would wait out here for you.

**Annie:** I really didn't mean to eavesdrop, but the door wasn't closed.

**Marguerite:** It's fine, Annie. I don't mind. It wasn't anything confidential.

**Marguerite:** Professor Ludwig offered me a position at the Laplace Scientific Computing Center. If I were to accept, then yes, it would mean leaving.

**Marguerite:** Is there something you wanted to tell me?

For a moment, panic flashes across the girl's face.

**Annie:** Oh, is that all?

**Annie:** A placement at the LSCC really is an excellent opportunity. I've always been curious about how science could align with arcanum.

**Annie:** In any case, congratulations!

**Marguerite:** I haven't made up my mind yet.

**Marguerite:** If you are curious about arcanum technology, you should think about submitting an application too.

**Annie:** Oh, I-I don't think so.

Her face pales as she rushes to explain.

**Annie:** Not that I have anything against it, but I've never enjoyed the disorder that comes from arcanum.

**Annie:** Science is about complete, logical stories, not ambiguous, elusive concepts.

**Annie:** I decided a long time ago that I would put my trust in only what I could prove through scientific reason. That's the only way I feel safe.

**Marguerite:** An entirely reasonable human perspective. I'm sure they would appreciate someone with a traditional scientific viewpoint.

**Annie:** But I'm always so unsure, always second-guessing myself. I can't propose bold, creative hypotheses like real geniuses do.

**Annie:** I don't belong somewhere like Laplace.

**Marguerite:** I don't think that's a judgment you should make for yourself, Annie.

**Marguerite:** Oh, well, I shouldn't badger you. Wasn't there something you wanted to tell me?

**Annie:** No, it's nothing.

She awkwardly folds her hands behind her, concealing something.

It looks like a torn scrap of paper.

**Annie:** Just a trivial thing. Nothing important.

**Annie:** I didn't mean to bother you.

**Marguerite:** Please, Annie, what is it?

**Marguerite:** Did you want to show me something?

**Annie:** Just stop asking, Marguerite. I'm begging you.

The girl's plea leaves her unmoved.

**Marguerite:** Show me.

**Marguerite:** And what about you, Rachel?

**Marguerite:** You know, at this point, I don't think anyone at Laplace agrees with your hypothesis that the "Storm" can be explained through natural phenomena. It's quite remarkable that you persevere despite it all.

**Rachel:** Ha! Maybe so. But even if no one else agrees with me, does that mean it isn't true?

**Rachel:** After all, truth belongs in the hands of the brave few! So, why not me?

**Rachel:** I know many people at Laplace think I just refuse to admit natural science has lost its relevance in the face of the "Storm."

**Rachel:** I am stubborn, that much I admit.

**Rachel:** I chose this path, and I'll see it through to the end.

**Marguerite:** Even if it leaves you battered and bleeding?

**Rachel:** Even if it costs me everything.

The words hang for a heartbeat. Marguerite studies the depths of Rachel's pupils.

**Marguerite:** I see.

**Marguerite:** That's admirable. Truly.
