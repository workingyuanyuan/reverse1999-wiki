## Story

Rejowa

The two knights begin their journey across the war-torn countryside and reach Rejowa by nightfall.

They stop at the outskirts of the town, already dilapidated before the war, a maze of husked-out ruins and empty buildings.

**Adelheid:** Their convoy should pass through here this time tomorrow.

**Adelheid:** The roads narrow here before passing through the town center; it will force them to slow and move in single file. This is where we launch our attack.

Adelheid surveys their surroundings and points to the only path leading through the town.

**Marsha:** The houses here are all abandoned. Most left in a hurry; there should still be some things left behind.

**Marsha:** We can borrow this house for the night.

Marsha walks to the back of the house and examines it carefully.

Under the eaves, strings of dried nettle and yarrow sway in the wind. Marsha touches them, sweeping the dust away.

**Adelheid:** Has your Foundation given you leave to come along with me?

**Marsha:** I've reported back to my commander and to Mr. Razor. At any rate, my task was to convince you to leave Sen; I would say this is an interpretation of those orders.

She doesn't respond. The brisk wind passing through the old town rises to fill the void.

**Adelheid:** ... Marsha, I'm truly glad you survived.

**Adelheid:** But I still don't understand your decision. Why didn't you ever attempt to contact us? Why would you want to leave our Order?

I shall never forsake the Order, nor shall I hold fealty to another—Marsha had made these solemn oaths the day she became a knight.

She had been waiting for these questions, accusations even, but when they arrived, she found that Adelheid's tone was far gentler than she expected.

**Marsha:** ... I could not bear to take another soldier's life.

**Marsha:** The path of knighthood should not be one of endless slaughter on the battlefield.

**Marsha:** If I returned to the Order, if I had stayed a knight. I would have been forced to fight, kill, and die.

Absolute loyalty to the Order and unquestioning obedience to its authority. That was the demand made on all those who swore its oath.

And one of the few she ever doubted.

**Adelheid:** I've dealt with this "Foundation" of yours before. I admit their intentions seem noble, and they have done some good. Yet, I also know they've been recruiting soldiers and amassing military strength.

**Adelheid:** Your Foundation will soon command an army, moving between countries and acting according to their own authority. An organization with no oversight and no accountability.

**Adelheid:** In many ways they are as dangerous as Manus Vindictae.

**Marsha:** ... I agree entirely.

**Marsha:** But I have chosen to put my faith in them, in what my own eyes have seen of their work.

**Marsha:** And with the Foundation, I'm free to do the work that I believe in. To save lives, not end them.

**Marsha:** I can do so much more good with them than I ever could before.

Her mentor shakes her head ruefully.

**Adelheid:** What you've done is without precedent in our Order.

**Marsha:** I understand.

Marsha doesn't rush to persuade her. She only focuses on what would matter most to her.

**Marsha:** I never truly abandoned my vows as a knight.

**Marsha:** And worry for the sake of the Order ...

**Marsha:** What are you going to do, Master?

The woman pauses, composing her thoughts and vision for the future.

**Adelheid:** After I reclaim our treasured relics and return them, I will begin a search for all our scattered knights.

**Adelheid:** I don't know what comes after that. I will allow our future to unwind as it will.

**Adelheid:** Though I'm certain it does not include setting up our own feudal state out of an old castle.

Marsha notices the confusion in Adelheid's words.

**Marsha:** Grand Master, may I share my thoughts with you?

**Adelheid:** Yes?

**Marsha:** I want to rebuild the Hochland Order as a humanitarian medical relief organization.

**Marsha:** That way, our knights can be freed from further bloodshed and return to the Order's greater purpose.

She gives weight to the young knight's words, whether out of respect or genuine consideration.

**Adelheid:** ... That is not who we are, Sister.

Marsha had expected her mentor's reluctance, but she purses her lips and doesn't back down.

**Marsha:** Adelheid, please consider the possibilities. I have given this a great deal of thought.

**Marsha:** Since joining the Foundation and assisting people as a medic and a nurse, I've had a chance to reflect on the things you've taught me.

**Marsha:** You told me yourself that our Order was founded as a place of healing for wounded soldiers and victims of war. Couldn't that be true again?

Adelheid goes blank. That familiar look of forcefully suppressed emotion crosses her face. Then she simply turns and walks into a nearby house.

**Adelheid:** It's late. We need to rest.

Marsha remains outside the door and lifts her gaze.

Low clouds drift overhead like the palm of a giant, ready to close in, offering no escape.

**Marsha:** \*sigh\*

Marsha limps into the castle. Her figure looks small beneath the towering columns.

A shadow falls over her, swallowing her entirely.

**Marsha:** Adelheid?

The knight wears a stern expression. She seems to have been waiting for some time.

**Adelheid:** I watched your training today. You were ordered to fall back, yet you chose to stay behind and hold off the pursuers.

**Marsha:** Yes, ma'am.

**Adelheid:** What was your reason?

**Marsha:** There were wounded. Someone had to buy them time, or the whole team might have been caught.

**Adelheid:** And you believed your sacrifice would guarantee their escape—mission accomplished. Is that correct?

**Marsha:** ... Yes, ma'am.

**Adelheid:** And did you buy them enough time?

**Marsha:** ... No, ma'am. After I "died," the pursuers caught up with the rest of the team.

Her "heroic sacrifice" ended prematurely; tapped by a blunted spear, she was declared "dead" and put out of the fight.

**Adelheid:** You stayed behind alone without any cover, without a plan; all you ended up doing was throwing your life away.

The young knight is struck by her master's criticism, but she steels herself and defends her choice.

**Marsha:** The problem was our choice of evacuation route.

**Marsha:** If we had attempted the other mountain pass, where the path was narrow, I would have been able to buy them enough time.

**Marsha:** A knight—a true knight ... is never afraid of death in the service of duty.

She looks up at Adelheid, staring straight into her eyes, her lips pressed into a hard line.

**Adelheid:** ... And if there is no narrow pass next time? What if you cannot guarantee your sacrifice will mean anything at all?

**Adelheid:** Do you think I don't know you, child? It wouldn't have made a difference.

**Adelheid:** You've always acted recklessly.

**Adelheid:** You volunteered to man the battering ram without even knowing how it works. You're the first to the front in every shield wall and the first to leap into every fight.

**Adelheid:** I've been too effective, I fear. I turned a scared little girl into a would-be martyr.

Her tone is self-critical, but she softens as she leans toward Marsha.

**Adelheid:** I know you're eager to prove yourself ... You want to show you're worthy of our vows with some great act of sacrifice.

**Adelheid:** You want people to forget that you're just some lost orphan discarded in the snow.

**Adelheid:** That you are, instead, a valiant knight, a fighter worth celebrating through the ages. Isn't that right, Marsha?

Marsha freezes, her face flushing hot. Her deepest desire has been laid bare with complete precision.

**Marsha:** I ... yes, it's true.

**Adelheid:** You've replaced your fear with bravado. But you haven't learned to use your courage effectively. Making you foolhardy.

Adelheid turns and motions for Marsha to follow.

**Marsha:** Where are we going?

Their footsteps echo through the empty hallway, time seeming to slip away in the rhythmic echoes.

**Adelheid:** A new lesson—something just as important as bravery.

**Marsha:** Yes?

**Adelheid:** To cherish your life.

**Adelheid:** Recklessness doesn't just waste your courage; it puts your comrades in danger as well.

**Adelheid:** Marsha, do you know why the Hochland Order was founded?

**Marsha:** You've told me before. It was created to tend to wounded soldiers.

**Adelheid:** I think it's time I teach you its history in detail.

Adelheid stops and opens the library door behind them.

The night is dark; not a single light nor star illuminates the space beyond her view. Marsha listens to the rustling that pulled her from her sleep.

Someone has slipped inside in the middle of the night and is rummaging around for something.

All but invisible in the darkness, she can spy Adelheid stirring awake. Without exchanging a word, they both continue to feign sleep, waiting for the intruders to come closer.

**???:** ... Damn it. Nothing here ...

**???:** Shh, don't wake them up!

The low whispers come from two men.

They creep toward the cabinet above the knights' heads.

Maybe that's what they're after.

**???:** If it's not here, we have to get back—

**???:** Ugh!!

Before he can finish the sentence, the frightened man suddenly realizes he can't move—his body is pinned to the floor.

He turns his head with difficulty, only to see a gun pressed against his partner's head.

**???:** W-Wait ...! We were only looking for medicine! We didn't mean any harm!

The red-haired woman tightens her grip on his wrist.

**Marsha:** Medicine? Who are you?
