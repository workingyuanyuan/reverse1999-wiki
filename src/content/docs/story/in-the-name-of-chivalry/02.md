## Story

***

**GAMEPLAY**

***

Sen

Sen has already seen the full brunt of arcane warfare, their aftereffects turning the streets into a twisted mockery of their former existence.

But the Foundation succeeded in getting most of the population out before the fighting got too close, leaving the area like a strange lifeless moonscape.

**Marsha:** According to Mr. Razor, the knight should be somewhere around here ...

Marsha stands at the crossroads, taking stock of her surroundings.

Closed doors, dark storefronts, occasionally life stirs in the form of passing refugees trudging their most treasured possessions toward the evacuation points.

**Marsha:** ... Has she already left?

**Woman:** Staś! Wait!

The shout comes from the end of the street, Marsha turns alert and ready for danger.

But no sooner does she turn than a tiny form slams into her leg.

**Boy:** Watch it, lady! Don't you know there's a war coming?

The boy tumbles out onto the street, along with his felt hat and everything he's holding.

**Marsha:** Entschuldigung (excuse me). Is everything alright, young man?

**Marsha:** Are you hurt at all?

Marsha crouches down to inspect the boy.

**Boy:** Leave me alone. Mama is going to catch me—

The boy gathers his things in a rush.

**Marsha:** Hold on!

She grabs the boy just as he is about to run, nearly causing him to stumble again.

His mother runs toward them, out of breath.

**Marsha:** Staś ... Can I call you that? What happened? Why are you running around out here?

**Marsha:** This place isn't safe. You should go home to your mother.

The boy twists with all his strength to break free from Marsha, his thin face flushing.

Seeing that he can't escape, his shoulders slump in frustration.

**Boy:** I-I just want to deliver a gift!

**Marsha:** A gift?

**Woman:** Phew ... You little brat!

The boy's mother at last catches up, puffing with both exasperation and exhaustion, and gives his head a smack.

**Woman:** Thank you so much, madam. I swear I look away for just one second and he starts charging into trouble.

**Boy:** I wasn't charging into trouble! I had to deliver my gift! You always tell me to keep my word, well, that's what I'm doing!

**Woman:** There's no time for that. What if we miss the evacuation train?

The boy pouts, but he has no argument.

**Marsha:** Would you let me know what's going on here? Please, don't worry. I'm on a mission from the St. Pavlov Foundation.

Marsha points to the badge on her chest.

**Marsha:** If it's something as simple as delivering a gift, perhaps I can help?

**Boy:** Really?!

The boy wrings himself free and offers Marsha the item he had been carrying.

**Marsha:** Is this ... a shield?

The construction is somewhat rudimentary, made of welded steel with an old belt for a strap, and it's on the smaller side, only as long as her forearm, but its purpose is unmistakable.

Red paint twists into a shape across its rough surface; it's difficult to make sense of, but it seems to portray the silhouette of a building on a hill.

**Woman:** My little Staś had a bad fever. With the way things were, we were worried we wouldn't be able to get him to a doctor. But then we came across this woman.

**Woman:** Oh, she was wearing armor just like yours.

The woman points toward Marsha's pauldrons, a bit surprised.

**Woman:** She healed Staś, and to repay her kindness, he wanted to give her a shield to match her armor.

**Woman:** My husband ... he helped him make it from an old bucket.

**Woman:** With the evacuation order and all the packing, we haven't had time to give it to her. I put it away for later.

**Woman:** But then Staś happened to find it, and ... since then, I haven't had a moment's peace.

**Marsha:** ... The sigil on the shield, is it meant to be a castle on a hill?

**Woman:** That's right. Are you ...

**Marsha:** Yes. A fellow member of her Order. I'm looking for her.

**Marsha:** Have you any idea where she is, and ... did you happen to get her name?

Within an old but tidy house, a kettle begins to roll and cry with steam.

A woman in knightly armor sits on a wooden chair, cleaning her helmet with care.

It bears the scratches and scuffs of untold numbers of battles, yet it has clearly been meticulously maintained.

A knock at the door breaks the rhythm of her work.

**???:** Who is it?

**Marsha:** Sister Adelheid? Are you in there?

The sound is soft and faint, filtered by the door, but Adelheid feels her heart skip.

**Marsha:** It's me, Marsha.

**Adelheid:** ...!

It's a voice she could never mistake.

Her palms feel numb as she moves toward the door; she almost involuntarily jerks it open.

The face she sees behind the door is both everything she feared and hoped for.

A face she once thought gone and buried. A face that elicits grief and sorrow in every reflection. But now all she feels is joy.

**Marsha:** Adelheid. It's really you ... I'm so glad.

The woman in front of her is tired—maybe from a long journey—but breathing, radiating warmth.

Not a skeleton, not some revenant or nightmare.

Living, breathing, at her doorstep.

**Adelheid:** It's a miracle! Marsha, you're here! You're alive.

She nearly leaps toward her, her hands stretch outward, but she freezes in place from shock or awe or fear—she can't say.

Yet Marsha reaches in and pulls her into a warm embrace.

**Marsha:** Yes, Adelheid, I survived "Dead Man's Hill."

**Marsha:** I'm so glad you did as well.

It is a true blessing, being able to reunite in these times of war.

Adelheid lets out a long-drawn breath and nestles into the hug, before breaking from it with a reassuring pat.

**Adelheid:** Come inside. The winter has come early this year; I'd rather not let the chill in.

**Marsha:** I have something for you, a gift of thanks from Staś.

The rough shield is delivered to the knight as promised. Adelheid takes it and turns it in her hands.

**Adelheid:** I will have to give him a proper thank you, should we ever chance to meet again. His family has done me a great service; they helped me find this place.

**Adelheid:** But Marsha, I must know ... How did you manage to survive?

The question is asked alongside an offered cup of tea. The curling steam masks the betrayal at the heart of her answer.

**Marsha:** I was injured during the fight. When I went down, I thought perhaps I would die. But my armor had saved me from the worst of the damage. I used my training and waited.

**Marsha:** I just lay still, sealing away every thought and pain in my body until a medical team from the Foundation discovered me.

**Marsha:** I recovered under their care.

**Adelheid:** But your armor ...

**Marsha:** This was all that was salvageable after the medics cut me free. But I could never fully part with it.

**Marsha:** Not that it matters much. Armor is more of a hindrance than an aid on the battlefield now.

**Adelheid:** ... We have always clung to our traditions, perhaps even after they were no longer of use.

She studies Marsha as she speaks, taking note of her uniform beneath the pauldrons. No longer a knight, but not a soldier either, a nurse's frock.

And on this new uniform, an unexpected badge, one she immediately recognizes with a stir of aversion.

**Marsha:** Adelheid, why are you out here alone? Where are the other members of our Order?

Adelheid takes a long sip of her tea, as if trying to swallow down questions she hasn't yet found the courage to ask.

**Adelheid:** ... A lot has happened in the Order.

**Adelheid:** I suppose, first of all, that I am now its acting Grand Master.

**Marsha:** ...!

In the event of the Grand Master's death, the role is passed to the knight with the highest seniority until a new leader is confirmed.

Neither needs to remind the other of this plain fact.

**Marsha:** What happened to Master Hermann?

**Adelheid:** It is as you fear; he's dead.

Marsha can't help but draw a sharp breath.

**Marsha:** ... How? When did it happen?

**Adelheid:** Less than a month ago.

**Marsha:** I heard nothing about it ...

**Adelheid:** Surely you know that Manus Vindictae rose out from the shadows, that they've unleashed this war on all of humanity rather than face the consequences of their manipulation.

**Adelheid:** But their earlier infiltration was deeper even than anyone had known. Every communication network has been compromised or crippled. Including ours.

**Adelheid:** You have been away from the Order for too long. It's only natural that you wouldn't know.

Marsha tightens her grip on the cup; there's a needle-like accusation in her words.

Master Hermann had led for more than a decade.

Though as a young novice and later knight, she had little direct contact with him.

But she knew, as all young knights of the Order did, that Hermann was a model of chivalry: brave, loyal, strict, and maybe a little too rigid, but a man worth looking up to.

**Marsha:** ... How did he die?

The idea that a man of such virtue and wisdom was gone, that he had fallen in some godforsaken battlefield, twists in her heart.

**Adelheid:** He was killed in our very castle ... Whoever did it framed things to look like a suicide, but I saw the scene with my own eyes. There was a struggle in that room.

**Adelheid:** Yet, the Order was under pressure from the government; the others decided to brush the investigation under the rug rather than face the possibility that we had been compromised like the army.

**Adelheid:** I went through his letters and diaries. He already noticed the shadows of Manus Vindictae operating in our ranks.

**Marsha:** ... Master Hermann was loyal to his oaths and to us. He must have been attempting to expose them.

**Adelheid:** But even so, once the military's involvement with the Manus was revealed, things started to fall apart.

**Adelheid:** There was only one thing that all of us realized—

**Adelheid:** We had been betrayed.

**Marsha:** ...

**Adelheid:** With the armistice coming, we withdrew from fighting to determine what to do next.

**Adelheid:** But even as we left the front, the divisions began to show. Some wanted to return to Hohes Schloss and build it into an independent state. To carve out our own personal kingdom as it was in the ancient past.

**Marsha:** Had they learned nothing from the war? How were mounted knights supposed to rule in a time of machine guns and tanks?

**Adelheid:** I think they understood the futility; maybe they even realized how foolish they were being. But when all else falls apart, people often turn to nostalgia and tradition. They faced into the bleak future and chose to believe in an idyllic past.

**Adelheid:** But there is a greater tragedy. Manus Vindictae had beaten the main force home. They ransacked the castle, killed its defenders, and left it in ruins.

Adelheid puts down her cup. Its bottom clinks as it hits the table.

**Adelheid:** Centuries of wealth accumulated by our Order and all the rewards of its glory were gone.

**Adelheid:** Our treasures are doubtless being melted down to fuel their schemes.

Marsha closes her eyes.

She knows every room, every corridor, every pillar of that castle.

It was her home.

She imagines all those familiar things being torn down, stripped, sold for parts, and melted into scrap. A firestorm of anger and humiliation wells up inside her, but years of training kick in, and she calms herself with her arcane skill.

**Marsha:** ... I hope the castle itself will be restored.

The emotions fall away, and reason takes over; something clicks in her mind.

**Marsha:** Is that why you haven't left this place?

Adelheid's lips curve.

**Adelheid:** Ach so, then you have come to me on behalf of your Foundation.
