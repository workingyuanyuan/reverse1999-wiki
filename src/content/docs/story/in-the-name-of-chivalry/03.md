## Story

Marsha falls silent.

She looks at Adelheid, and Adelheid looks back—measuring her.

**Marsha:** ... I won't deny that I was sent here by them.

**Adelheid:** Then at the very least you have not sacrificed your honesty along with the rest of your sacred vows.

**Marsha:** I would never lie to or deceive another of our Order. Least of all you.

**Marsha:** But ... there's no contradiction between my mission and my loyalty to you.

**Marsha:** Adelheid, you raised me; the Order gave me a home and a purpose.

**Marsha:** I will always care for you—and the safety of our people.

She chooses each word carefully but sincerely.

Her mentor holds a long silence, leaving only the sound of the windows rattling in the wind. At last, she breaks the quiet with intention.

**Adelheid:** In two days, a convoy from Manus Vindictae will pass through Rejowa, carrying all that they've stolen from us.

**Adelheid:** As acting Grand Master, I cannot allow them to escape with their plunder—not after failing to prevent their raid in the first place.

**Adelheid:** I will not allow this opportunity to pass untaken.

**Marsha:** But Manus Vindictae has weapons enhanced with arcane skills. You're only one knight. You can't do this alone. The danger is too great.

**Marsha:** Maybe you can work something out with the Foundation so that—

**Adelheid:** "Danger."

Adelheid repeats the word under her breath like a rebuke.

**Adelheid:** They killed Master Hermann and violated the sanctity of our home. If our vows mean anything at all, I must answer these transgressions. To leave without avenging it ...

**Adelheid:** This is the gravest danger a knight has ever faced!

**Adelheid:** The Order may be much diminished, but we are not so base as to ask bureaucrats to fight our battles.

Adelheid's words land with weight.

Her expression is cold and point-of-fact. The warmth and joy she had shown to Marsha is gone.

Now, she speaks to her as she would, and certainly has, to any other St. Pavlov Foundation employee.

**Marsha:** ... I'm sorry. I shouldn't have brought it up.

The pale sunlight outside the window cuts deep shadows across Adelheid's face.

But then Marsha notices something in that cold detachment and pulls her friend's closed fist into her hands.

**Adelheid:** Marsha ...

Anger, sadness, and disappointment begin to pass through their interlaced fingers into the young knight.

The older woman exhales, relieved. Her emotions, too long dammed up, are now passed into another's hand.

**Marsha:** You've borne this weight too long, Adelheid.

**Marsha:** We are sworn to carry each other's burdens. You may doubt me, but I have not forgotten my vows. Those words made us sisters, and I will never forsake that bond.

She can feel Adelheid's emotions spin over the words; a rush of anger, pain, and sorrow floods between them.

**Adelheid:** You say you remember our teachings. Do you not remember the danger in taking in another's emotions?

**Adelheid:** It is a last resort, one used only in times of great peril. Did I fail to impress that lesson on you too?

Marsha smiles—feeling like that clueless young novice she once was. With a rush of embarrassment, then guilt, but also affection.

**Marsha:** You taught me to put the needs of my comrades above all other things; that includes sharing their pain and their duty.

**Marsha:** So, allow me to fight with you. If we stand together, we may stand a chance at succeeding.

**Marsha:** I won't leave you to fight this battle alone, sister.

**Adelheid:** ... I don't presume to think I could tell you no. You've never listened to a word I've said.

Marsha looks up at her mentor, feeling a hot flush of pride pass through her.

**Adelheid:** We leave at dawn.

Outside Hohes Schloss

The crashing of the waves on the rocky shore does its best to drown out the little whimpers below.

**Adelheid:** I knew I'd find you here, Marsha.

The young girl looks up, her face streaked with tears.

**Adelheid:** It was the other novices again, wasn't it?

Marsha wipes her tears with her sleeve, but the spray of seawater has left her robes too damp to dry her face.

**Marsha:** ... What should I do, master?

Adelheid lowers herself to the rocky precipice along with her.

**Adelheid:** What do you think you should do, Sister Marsha?

**Marsha:** I-I tried to leave them alone and just do what I'm told, but—

**Adelheid:** No.

A warm, broad hand brushes the girl's tears. She looks up at her caretaker, confused.

**Adelheid:** They upset you because you allowed them. You showed them fear, and they seized on it.

**Adelheid:** Wolves do not strike a standing deer. The hunt begins only after they put them to flight.

**Marsha:** Then ...

**Adelheid:** Be as a stag in the forest. When they push you, show no fear, turn your antlers on them. Fight back.

**Marsha:** But they're taller than me ... and stronger ...

**Adelheid:** Strength will come as you train. Height too, so long as you remember to eat your vegetables.

**Adelheid:** You must show them that you won't run.

**Adelheid:** They may howl and bark; you may even lose a few battles—but you must show them above all else ...

**Adelheid:** That you have the courage to fight back.

**Adelheid:** You are to be a knight, Sister Marsha. A true knight does not falter, nor does she pout.

Adelheid offers a reassuring pat on the young girl's shoulder.

**Marsha:** I know. I want to become a true knight, brave and strong ...

She says the words with both innocence and ambition.

The waves crash against the rocks again like the drumming of a coming battle.

Marsha opens her eyes. In the dark room, the only thing she can see is the faint metallic shimmer of light glinting off of her pauldrons.

**Adelheid:** Good, I don't need to wake you.

By the window, Adelheid is already packing. Staś's shield is fastened at her waist.

Marsha lets out a long breath.

**Marsha:** Please wait, Adelheid. I'll be ready in a minute.

Soon, Marsha shoulders her first-aid crate and heads out with Adelheid.
