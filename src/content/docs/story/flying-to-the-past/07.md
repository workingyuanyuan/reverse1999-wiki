## Story

Beach, Bay Shore Town, 1969

Birds swoop out over the water, flying east over the crashing waves.

**Joanna:** You know, the papers love writing crazy headlines about Florida.

**Joanna:** Like "Ghost Ship Returns from the Atlantic," "Lost War Plane Guided Home by Little Green Men," "Winged Man Lands on Tampa Beach" ...

**Lucy:** I have heard them before. Our colleagues sometimes share their favorites over lunch.

**Lucy:** They would laugh so hard they spilled their coffee.

**Lucy:** It was always a mess afterward.

**Joanna:** They say it's the "Devil's Triangle" three points between our coast, Puerto Rico, and Bermuda.

**Lucy:** I never considered them credible.

**Lucy:** The waters in this so-called triangle are no more treacherous or magical than any other. People should be careful about separating truth from superstition.

**Lucy:** I do not doubt that you are more than capable of that.

**Joanna:** Of course I can, but, I mean, that last story, I guess I want it to be true. What if it is?

**Lucy:** I see. So, you believe that a winged human appeared from the Atlantic and landed here?

**Joanna:** Well, it's just that after you gave me that draft from Uncle Victor.

**Joanna:** At first, I thought we'd never get any further than he did, but when I saw that, I thought maybe we can do it. Maybe it was fate?

Fate. Fate has no direction in her view. Ascribing purpose or intention to a chain of events was just a flaw in human reasoning.

**Lucy:** I never considered that.

**Lucy:** I had insufficient time to work on it.

**Lucy:** So, I gave it to you. That is all.

**Lucy:** Where your uncle failed, I had hoped you might succeed.

**Lucy:** There was no notion of destiny. I simply made a practical decision.

**Lucy:** And this winged man story is just sensationalist journalism. It was a coincidence.

**Joanna:** Say what you will. You may not believe in fate, but plenty of others, humans and arcanists alike, do.

**Joanna:** Anyway, fate or not, you should see how far we've come.
