## Story

**Cornerstone:** Once the Foundation confirmed the year we had arrived in, there were two places I knew I had to visit.

**Cornerstone:** The first was Marseille, of course. Le Corbusier's apartment building there had been granted architectural heritage status in this era, and I couldn't miss a spectacle like that.

**Cornerstone:** The second, I suppose, was Barcelona. The German Pavilion designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe had been rebuilt, and people said it was as if they had returned to 1929.

**Cornerstone:** It was a wonderful era, at least for someone like me. I was in the heyday of my architectural passion; this was the era when Brutalism was finally earning the right to stand as a style that represented the past fifty years.

**Cornerstone:** It was also not long after the first Macintosh was released.

**Cornerstone:** It was nowhere near becoming a "classic" yet, let alone something people would design buildings around.

**Client:** And what would these be, miss?

**Cornerstone:** It's a residential design inspired by the form of a computer. One of my students once mentioned such an idea. I thought it would suit your request for something futuristic and technological—

**Client:** This makes no sense. We flew all the way here after seeing those beautiful concrete buildings of yours!

**Client:** Like those libraries! Those theaters! The things the Soviets once tried to keep for themselves but can't afford to build anymore!

**Client:** Those are true works of art! Those are the future!

**Cornerstone:** Oh, oh, I'm sorry.

**Cornerstone:** We'd only gone back by a little over a decade. To be honest, that wasn't enough to turn the world upside down.

**Cornerstone:** Our skyscrapers hadn't become castles, security guards didn't become knights, and arcanists weren't being burned on piles of straw.

**Cornerstone:** But this era still felt strange to me.

**Cornerstone:** Of course, it was nothing so surprising. I was an architectural designer from 1999, after all.

**Cornerstone:** My designs simply didn't belong to this era.

**Foundation Staff Member I:** Ms. Rossella, what is going on?

The staff member follows Rossella's gaze toward a scene that would make safety-compliance officers in any era, anywhere in the world, faint on the spot.

**Cornerstone:** Give me a little more cable!

Cornerstone rides a hovering block as if it were a magic carpet, floating some thirty meters above the office building.

On the ground, several Foundation staff members keep feeding out the cables in their hands as if flying a kite.

**Foundation Staff Member II:** Ms. Cornerstone! We're at maximum length! This is all the cable the office has!

**Cornerstone:** That'll do!

**Foundation Staff Member I:** This is absurd.

**Foundation Staff Member I:** What on earth is she doing? Are you really going to let this nonsense continue?

**Rossella:** Let's just watch for now.

At the other end of the "kite string," Cornerstone holds a microphone, a loudspeaker, and a camera that has been removed from its mount.

Image data travels back down the "kite string" to the ground, and the building clearly sees the distance.

**"Building":** I can see.

**"Building":** It's beautiful.

**"Building":** So, this is what it means to "look out at the world." So many buildings, countless people living their lives out there.

**"Building":** Thank you, Professor.

**Cornerstone:** You're welcome. Just keep me steady with your arcane skill. A fall from this height would probably break my spine—whoa!

**Cornerstone:** You'd think an architect wouldn't be afraid of heights.

**"Building":** I'm lowering you now.

The concrete slab descends more softly than a falling leaf. The block that carried her breaks apart into its original shapes and floats beside her as usual.

**Cornerstone:** We've connected all the transmission cables in series, so you can use your arcane skill to control this camera whenever you like. Whether you want to look at yourself or the world around, it should be much easier now.

**"Building":** Thank you.

The building falls silent for a while.

**"Building":** I think I need to apologize for driving you all out.

**Cornerstone:** Have you finally realized we're not bad people trying to hurt you?

**"Building":** It wasn't really that.

**"Building":** I just felt so frustrated.

**"Building":** When I first became aware, I could only see into these tiny little worlds—the archive room, the cafeteria, offices, dormitories with terrible soundproofing, and a lobby no one ever visited.

**"Building":** I watched and watched. It was all I could do.

**"Building":** I didn't know how to look away; I couldn't just close my "eyes" the way you might. I have no legs, so I couldn't leave.

**"Building":** I'm a building. I understand that this is what I was made to do.

**"Building":** But I didn't want to see people doing all the things I couldn't do. I couldn't stand it. I got angry.

**"Building":** So ...

**Cornerstone:** It'll be fine.

**Cornerstone:** You aren't so different from the other Awakened I know.

**Cornerstone:** It's not at all uncommon for them to make some real trouble when they first become aware of themselves.

Rossella steps forward and pats Cornerstone on the shoulder.

**Rossella:** I'm truly happy that you've found some resolution. It makes me hopeful for all of the conflicts in the world, including those between buildings and residents.

**Rossella:** I believe Ms. Kenna will be pleased to hear about this development as well. I'll include your contribution in the report, Ms. Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone:** Oh, you're reporting to Ms. Kenna? She helped us a great deal in Nowa Miedź.

**Cornerstone:** But ...

Cornerstone turns toward the microphone floating in midair.

**Cornerstone:** Our lesson isn't over yet, is it? You said you wanted to build something like yourself.

**"Building":** Only because I felt so lonely.

**"Building":** I wanted a friend, someone like me.

**"Building":** But I understand things more now. Any building I made wouldn't necessarily become an Awakened like me. It would be like all the other quiet buildings, wouldn't it?

**Cornerstone:** We don't really understand what causes an Awakened to form. Le Corbusier once said buildings were "a machine for serving humans." Before now, that's all I thought they could be.

**"Building":** Then it doesn't matter anymore.

**"Building":** Let's just start over. If I can't make a friend, I'll have to meet them. Maybe you can teach me that too.

**"Building":** I can be helpful! I'll find documents and tools for you with my arcane skill.

**Rossella:** That's quite the skill!

**Cornerstone:** Looks like my mission went off surprisingly smoothly?

**Rossella:** Thank you for your help, Ms. Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone:** To be honest, I only ended up here because I missed a deadline. I was making up to a friend.

**Rossella:** I heard about that. You're designing a monument commemorating all those we lost in the "Storm."

**Cornerstone:** When they first asked me, I had no idea how I would commemorate, how I would mourn all that we'd lost. But I think I've finally stumbled onto an idea, something to make sense of my feelings.

**Cornerstone:** This monument can't only be for lost family, pioneers, volunteers, and the tragedy of it all ...

**Rossella:** "Eureka." I think that's what you geniuses say.

**Cornerstone:** That's right.

**Cornerstone:** I want my design to make people think about more than just the "past."

**Cornerstone:** I have a Shade friend. The echoes she weaves endlessly mourn things of the past, things that are about to disappear.

**Cornerstone:** I want to follow her example and remind people of something else that was taken away from us—the future.

**Rossella:** Oh?

**Cornerstone:** We lost the year 2000.

**Cornerstone:** Who knows what we lost alongside it? The next generation of computers, the chance to glimpse the far side of the Moon, new designs and waves of fashion ...

**Cornerstone:** We lost all that the new millennium promised us. That world has been cut off from us behind the rain, along with everything else we lost in its onslaught.

**Rossella:** You're making it sound like a war.

**Cornerstone:** Really? Well, if a general ever sets out to reclaim our future, I'll be first in line with a shovel to dig the trenches.

**Cornerstone:** But before that, even now, while the world we took for granted is already gone ...

**Cornerstone:** I want to stay on the right path of time for as long as I can.

**Cornerstone:** Who knows, maybe I'll one day be presenting the visual trends of the new millennium to a medieval pope.

**Cornerstone:** Wherever we may drift in the future ...

Cornerstone gazes into the distance at the building, and the Foundation staff gathered around it, finally breathing a sigh of relief.

**Cornerstone:** Architecture is eternal.
