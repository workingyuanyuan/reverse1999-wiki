## Story

Guest Room, Foundation

**Grundriss:** I have one piece of good news and three or more pieces of bad news.

**Cornerstone:** Let's start with the good news.

**Grundriss:** First, congratulations on surviving and on not making anyone wait around at death's door the way they wait for your late drafts.

**Cornerstone:** You make it sound as if I'd hold up an entire transport just to say a heartfelt goodbye.

**Grundriss:** In that case, everything else is bad news.

Grundriss lets out a long sigh and shakes the design draft in her hand.

**Grundriss:** I believe this design is worthy of a place in architectural history. I mean, assuming the people who write those books ever learn what the "Storm" is.

**Grundriss:** You know I rarely say this, and I don't want to repeat it. But the proposal you submitted was worth the wait.

**Grundriss:** Unfortunately, the "Storm" monument project itself has been canceled.

**Cornerstone:** I can't say I'm shocked.

**Grundriss:** With how costly the "Storm" has been, time after time, the Foundation has decided that it needs to conserve its resources for necessary infrastructure.

**Grundriss:** Some ill-intentioned extremist pure-blood arcanists are claiming that the intervals between "Storms" will keep shrinking.

**Cornerstone:** That won't be good news for the construction industry.

**Grundriss:** Mm-hm. Also, the local investigators in Barcelona checked in.

**Grundriss:** Your building friend ... is gone. There's nothing there now but an open wasteland.

**Grundriss:** And now that Laplace has made a major breakthrough in the application of teleportation rituals, upper management sees no need to continue Ms. Rossella's project.

**Grundriss:** In other words, it seems we aren't going to be building any more prefab offices.

**Grundriss:** As the Foundation gets better at handling the "Storm," I imagine they'll sweep this whole idea under the rug.

**Grundriss:** Thrown away into the forgotten scrap heap, like every other failure.

**Cornerstone:** That doesn't surprise me either.

Cornerstone takes a sip of coffee. It is the first properly satisfying cup of coffee she has had after months of drifting outside.

**Cornerstone:** Has my travel permit been approved?

**Grundriss:** Sorry, I'm fresh out of good news.

**Grundriss:** Is that all I am to you? A Foundation delivery service?

**Cornerstone:** Of course not. You're also my only precious subject for studying German humor.

**Cornerstone:** Sadly, you don't seem to have taken to my "Storm" joke.

**Grundriss:** You know, I often feel as if there's a screw loose in your head. But then, we are arcanists, so there's bound to be something rattling up in my head too.

Grundriss shakes her head and tosses Cornerstone the document she has been gripping for some time.

**Grundriss:** You know there's nothing left for you in Barcelona.

**Grundriss:** Not your building friend, nor the Barcelona chair that haunts your dreams.

**Cornerstone:** Even so, I'm still going.

**Cornerstone:** Don't pretend that comes as a shock.

**Grundriss:** I knew it.

A second sigh follows. As Cornerstone leaves, Grundriss is left alone in the room once more.

**Grundriss:** Everything will get back on track, won't it?

**Cornerstone:** I heard how people died in this "Storm."

**Cornerstone:** Their arms turned into linked rings, and their heads fell to the floor, becoming spheres as round and smooth as the Earth itself.

**Cornerstone:** What would you have become?

**Cornerstone:** A triangle on top of a rectangle with a little grid-like pattern drawn on it? Perhaps a small chimney too.

**Cornerstone:** Like those pictures every child has drawn at some point.

**Cornerstone:** Little kids trying to draw home.

Cornerstone walks through the overgrown woodland. The drastic change in the environment means she can only rely on the coordinates provided by the Foundation to confirm where her friend once stood.

**Cornerstone:** This really is a terrible location. I can't imagine how much those construction crews suffered getting in here.

**Cornerstone:** Well, it's humid, but the birds are singing, and even the spiders and geckos seem to enjoy it here. I guess it's as fine a place as any.

Cornerstone usually meets her clients in a custom-made suit. In social settings, an expensive appearance is often a form of hard currency.

But none of that has any real meaning to an architect.

So, she simply lies down on the grass, letting the soil stain every expensive fabric on her body.

And then she imagines.

She imagines a hard foundation breaking through her skin, gripping the soil tightly like roots.

She imagines her body swelling, her hands becoming extensions of sight as she looks out at enormous things like herself.

The airport in the distance is no longer just a series of buildings but another living thing lying beside her, their boundaries drawn on draft paper but not felt in the heart.

She imagines herself becoming a building.
