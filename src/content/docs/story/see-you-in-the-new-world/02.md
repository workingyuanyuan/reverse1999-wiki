## Story

Distant, professional, meticulous.

The Foundation staff member before Cornerstone matches her initial impression to perfection; even the creases in her shirt seem to be intentionally parallel.

The only thing out of place seems to be the location for their meeting.

Cornerstone blinks, trying again to make sure she hasn't found herself in another dream.

Tents of every size have been pitched among the trees, while the normally restrained and efficient civil servants of the Foundation are gathered around eating canned beans.

She wishes Grundriss were here, if only to have another witness to this bizarre spectacle.

**Cornerstone:** Pardon me. Is this supposed to be some kind of team-building camping trip?

The woman before Cornerstone seems to remember that she is responsible for explaining this absurd scene, and her already glum features twist into an even more bitter expression.

**Rossella:** Ms. Grundriss, this is rather complicated.

**Cornerstone:** I'm Cornerstone.

**Rossella:** Ms. Cornerstone ...

She might just be imagining things, but the woman before her looks to be on the verge of tears.

**Cornerstone:** Ah, my apologies. I swapped assignments with Ms. Grundriss on short notice. I'll take care of the handover paperwork later.

**Cornerstone:** In any case, I'm here to help. Whatever trouble you're facing, just let me know.

**Rossella:** Thank goodness.

**Rossella:** Well, you see, about that ... As far as we know now, there are only a few places we've discovered that can shelter us through the "Storm" besides headquarters, but we still need to carry out our operations worldwide.

**Rossella:** It's been a point of contention whether the Foundation should adapt local buildings or construct dedicated offices of its own.

**Rossella:** And that's why we're here. This plot of land is meant to test the feasibility of creating our own structures.

**Cornerstone:** Well, it's humid, but the birds are singing, and even the spiders and geckos seem to enjoy it here. I guess it's as fine a place as any.

**Rossella:** Most importantly, it's close to the airport, which means we can withdraw to headquarters as quickly as possible before the next "Storm."

**Rossella:** Location is all that matters.

She stresses the words precisely.

**Cornerstone:** About what you said regarding building beyond headquarters ...

**Rossella:** As far as we know, it's the only place in the world that is confirmed as a "Point Irreversible."

Cornerstone swallows the words on the tip of her tongue.

Even within the Foundation, the actual number and locations of "Points Irreversible" are confidential and not widely disclosed.

**Rossella:** Since the "Storm" could arrive at any time, we're planning on using a prefab panel structure that can be finished in under two weeks.

**Cornerstone:** Oh, I know this kind of building very well. I spent my childhood in a neighborhood full of them. They have a more familiar name back home—apartment block.

**Rossella:** That's right.

**Cornerstone:** Alright. In that case, I think I more or less know what I'm here to do.

**Cornerstone:** Shall I assume that there's an issue with the building, then? Has to be something hazardous to explain this impromptu camping trip.

**Cornerstone:** Why didn't you put your people up in a hotel for now? Seems cruel to have them stuck out here with all these spiders.

**Rossella:** There isn't a hotel that can accommodate our needs within a safe range of the airport.

**Cornerstone:** Ahem ...

**Cornerstone:** Checking for hidden hazards and inspecting stress patterns ... That is indeed my colleague's area of expertise.

**Cornerstone:** Still, no need to worry. I'm just as qualified for this kind of work.

**Rossella:** Um, Ms. Grundriss—

**Cornerstone:** I'm Cornerstone ...

**Rossella:** Right, sorry. Actually, there's nothing wrong with the office itself.

**Cornerstone:** Huh?

**Rossella:** Do you know much about Shades? Poltergeists, apparitions, whatever else you prefer to call them.

**Cornerstone:** I happen to have a few Shade friends, actually.

**Rossella:** Oh, that's good to hear.

**Rossella:** We asked headquarters to send an investigator familiar with Shades and qualified as an architect to help us out with this strange situation.

**Rossella:** They recommended Ms. Grundriss.

**Cornerstone:** But I don't understand why you'd need an architect who knows about Shades.

**Cornerstone:** Surely the Foundation has plenty of arcanists who can deal with spirits. Shouldn't this be their department?

**Rossella:** That's true. In fact, when the poltergeist activity first started, we consulted the Supernatural Incidents Emergency Manual and contacted the nearest available exorcists.

**Rossella:** But each of them was picked up by the collar and tossed out of the building.

**Cornerstone:** Did any of them bother to explain why?

**Rossella:** There was one thing.

**Rossella:** They claimed there was a powerful Shade within. It demanded that they "bring someone who knows how to build a house."

**Cornerstone:** Ah, I may have misread the assignment at first, but not to worry. This is well within my area of expertise.

**Cornerstone:** I have plenty of experience dealing with mischievous spirits like this, the ill-intentioned sort that likes eating erasers and draft paper. Most of them are no trouble at all, like spoiled kids who want things a particular way.

**Rossella:** That is reassuring, Ms. Grundriss.

**Cornerstone:** I'm Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone:** By the way, have you prepared a tent for me?

**Rossella:** Uhh ...

**Cornerstone:** Or coffee? Anything that can substitute for sleep will do.

**Cornerstone:** I could really use a rest. I've gone a few days without sleep, and my heart is knocking against my rib cage, muttering something like, "Warning: cardiac arrest imminent."

**Rossella:** Oh my goodness! But you look completely fine. How incredible.

**Cornerstone:** Thank you. Looking fresh and radiant after a week of sleep deprivation is a compulsory course for architectural designers.

**Cornerstone:** A designer who looks weak and worn out can't hold her ground.

**Rossella:** I understand. Take the path on your right to Tent Seven. It's already set up, but the person using it has gone back to headquarters for the time being.

**Rossella:** Have some rest. The investigation can wait until tomorrow.

**Cornerstone:** How thoughtful of you.

**Cornerstone:** Ah, forgive me. I really do need some sleep.

**Rossella:** We'll reconvene in eleven hours.

**Rossella:** Rest well, Ms. Grundriss.

**Cornerstone:** I'm ... \*sigh\* Forget it.

**Cornerstone:** Good night.

**GAMEPLAY**

The season was different then. When Stasia was invited to the academy, summer dusk still held enough heat to roast a sharp, tarry smell out of the asphalt.

It was a very unremarkable period of time.

Her childhood, her student days, even the years after she truly became an architect—

Unbuilt fantasies, frantic assignments, the proudest days of her life ... all of them are more worthy of remembrance than this time.

**Cornerstone:** But still, it's this classroom that keeps appearing in my dreams, again and again, like an echo.

**Cornerstone:** A place with no tiresome bureaucrats picking apart my designs with their amateur aesthetics.

**Cornerstone:** No greedy developers throwing good planning aside in pursuit of prime real estate.

**Cornerstone:** I don't believe I'm the kind of arcanist trapped by old wounds, forced to ruminate on painful scenes night after night.

**Cornerstone:** Well, the architect qualification exam might count. I always dream that I'm sitting in that examination room without my paperwork ready.

**Cornerstone:** We architects are, by nature, drawn toward the future.

**???:** Yes, the future.

**Cornerstone:** Duvalle?

**Duvalle:** You never told us much about your own designs, madam. I've never actually seen any of them.

**Duvalle:** That's why I'm here again. Would you be willing to tell me about them?

This is a dream, and the dreamer has no obligation to answer the questions of a shadow within it. Yet, she does.

**Cornerstone:** The first project I designed independently, after leaving my apprenticeship, was a warehouse.

**Cornerstone:** Honestly, compared to a library, a theater, a government office, it didn't need beauty or concept. You only had to place a regulation-compliant storage space on the provided plot of land.

**Cornerstone:** But even then, I made a mess of it.

**Cornerstone:** I think the first project that really earned me any recognition was a few years later when I was asked to design a park.

**Duvalle:** Not a building?

**Cornerstone:** The work of an architect reaches further than most people imagine.

**Cornerstone:** In any case, I designed it with an observation deck that looked out over the scenery. Looking back, it was completely flamboyant and frivolous, but it attracted attention from the right people.

**Cornerstone:** After that, I slowly worked up to the point where I could leave my firm and work for myself; I even got to appear in some magazines.

**Duvalle:** Can I still find them somewhere?

**Cornerstone:** I'm afraid not, my dear. Because it's 1987 now.

**Cornerstone:** All my projects disappeared alongside you, behind a curtain of rain.

**Duvalle:** That's right.

**Duvalle:** And the "future" along with it.

**Cornerstone:** The "future" ...

**Duvalle:** Those who disappeared into the rain ... those who helped you cross it ...

**Duvalle:** The ones you want to memorialize ...

**Duvalle:** It isn't just the past that's being erased.

**Duvalle:** In the "Storm," things that were new, things that should have come into being, things meant for the future ...

**Duvalle:** They were lost as well.

**Duvalle:** Like a twig burning from both ends.
