## Story

**Rossella:** Very well.

**Rossella:** Ms. Cornerstone and I have entered the main building of the Foundation branch office.

**Rossella:** We are currently in the reception area. There is no sign of poltergeist activity so far.

**Rossella:** All other personnel, please take your nitrated garlic extract, load it into your regulation exorcism devices, and stand by at the south entrance.

**Rossella:** We're ready, Ms. Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone:** Wonderful. I feel safer already.

**Cornerstone:** Shades don't usually appear on their own, do they?

**Cornerstone:** In the films I've seen, this sort of Shade usually grabs victims by the neck with invisible hands or quietly slips something sharp into their arteries.

**Cornerstone:** It is a little too quiet here. Has it ever tried to communicate with you?

**Rossella:** No. Never with us directly. Just the exorcists.

**Rossella:** But I'm sure, the moment we head inside, everything will start up again. Just like before.

**Cornerstone:** It threw a book at you, didn't it? One of those heavy Foundation handbooks, right?

**Rossella:** That was just the start. At one point, it was batting us around like ping-pong balls.

**Cornerstone:** In that case, a little trick I just learned in Nowa Miedź may come in handy.

**Cornerstone:** Would you object if I used an arcane skill here?

**Rossella:** I don't mind, no, but maybe you could explain first.

**Cornerstone:** Alright. My friend told me that Shades are often beings tied to specific things and that it's not uncommon for Shades to be attached to buildings or objects important to them.

**Cornerstone:** If I want to know what it wants, I simply have to ask this building.

**Cornerstone:** Buildings like this have a shape, a natural contour of their geometry that reveals their spirit. That can take the form of anything really: plants, animals, or even a crescent moon.

**Cornerstone:** All I need to do is find the idea that fits within the lines of the masonry.

**Rossella:** This is it.

Monitor Room

**Rossella:** This is the office's monitoring center. From here, we can use the cameras to observe every corner of the building. You can also use that communicator over there to broadcast inside the building.

**Cornerstone:** That's exactly what I need.

**Cornerstone:** No need for an arcanist who can communicate with Shades. No need for a Type-III arcanum frequency. This is a tool made just for you.

**Cornerstone:** Now, would you mind saying hello to everyone?

**???:** Hello.

A childlike greeting comes out over the loudspeaker.

**Rossella:** What is going on?

**Cornerstone:** I've established communication with the building.

**Cornerstone:** There aren't any Shades here.

**Cornerstone:** What we're dealing with is the building itself.

**Rossella:** You mean ...

The Foundation staff member flips through the documents in her hands.

**Rossella:** I see.

**Rossella:** You're suggesting that our entire office has become an Awakened.

**"Building":** "Awakened"—is that my name?

**Cornerstone:** No, no. You can choose a name for yourself.

**Cornerstone:** Huh, I'm speaking to a building. This feels like something out of a fairy tale, like Baba Yaga's hut.

**Cornerstone:** Ahem. First thing's first. Would it be possible for you to stop throwing around the people who have been living inside you?

**Cornerstone:** You are a building, one designed for integrated habitation. The people here need a place to live and work.

**"Building":** I don't wanna.

**"Building":** Having all these people inside me makes me feel yucky.

**"Building":** And all the garlic, charms, talismans, salt ... They all feel awful! Stop throwing those things around me!

**Cornerstone:** This feels remarkably familiar.

**Cornerstone:** Allow me to formally apologize on their behalf.

**"Building":** Okay, and?

**Cornerstone:** Hmm, let me think. We could repaint your exterior, reinforce your structure, extend your lifespan, or maybe you'd like to be redecorated? Maybe you'd like something grander? More beautiful?

**Rossella:** Ms. Cornerstone, we do have a budget, you know ...

**"Building":** Hmph. People always think in such narrow ways, measuring others' wants and needs based on their own!

**Cornerstone:** That's a very astute observation. You seem to know a lot about the world for someone that was, for lack of a better word, "born" quite recently.

**Cornerstone:** For most Awakened, it takes only about a year to become legally responsible and fit for work. Hmm, maybe that's why the Foundation has so many Awakened staff members.

**"Building":** I told them before. I need someone who knows how to build a house.

**Cornerstone:** That's right. And you've got her.

**Cornerstone:** When I heard you needed someone who "understood architecture," I thought perhaps you needed an architect to maintain or improve your body.

**Cornerstone:** But it sounds like that isn't what you're after.

**"Building":** Teach me. Teach me how to build.

**Cornerstone:** But you don't have any hands. Oh, your arcane skill. I guess if you can toss dozens of healthy adults around like toys, construction might not be entirely out of reach.

**Cornerstone:** That said, I can see some safety concerns.

**"Building":** If you can teach me, perhaps I will agree to let your people live inside me. I know this is how you make deals.

**Cornerstone:** May I ask why?

**"Building":** No.

**Rossella:** I guess we have no other choice.

**Rossella:** I doubt we could find a better location, not to mention the applications and planning alone would set us back months. I swear simple demolitions are treated as seriously as a homicide these days.

**Rossella:** But how long do you think it would take to train it to become an architect?

**Cornerstone:** If we were going by typical licensing requirements, it would be five years' worth of university courses and a year-long internship until they would be able to qualify.

**Rossella:** That-That's a lot of time to live in a tent.

Left unspoken is another much more painful reality.

**Foundation Staff Member I:** We should just give up on the whole farce. We were fools to think we could set up a fully functional office in just two weeks.

**Foundation Staff Member I:** And now, as if just to mock us for our hubris, our building becomes an Awakened and throws us out!

**Foundation Staff Member I:** Let's just make one thing clear about this whole nonsensical situation:

**Foundation Staff Member I:** This building, the land, and even the concrete under our feet supposedly belong to the Foundation. This thing, whatever it is, wouldn't be here without us!

**Foundation Staff Member I:** It has no reason to refuse us entry!

**Foundation Staff Member I:** But if what it's saying is true, and it can "feel" us when we're walking through it, I don't even think I want to go back inside!

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Rossella:** What do you think we should do, Ms. Cornerstone?

**Cornerstone:** From a rational standpoint, it might be better to cut your losses.

**Cornerstone:** The benefits of prefab construction are an obvious fit to the Foundation's needs in terms of build times and cost-efficiency.

**Cornerstone:** But relying on it may end up being a mistake. If the "Storm" keeps dragging us backward, we may find ourselves in an era where we won't be able to acquire or manufacture prefab structures easily.

**Cornerstone:** Speaking only from my limited insight as an architect ...

**Cornerstone:** I think adapting existing local structures would better fit the Foundation's needs across different eras, not to mention it would also help us to blend in with and integrate into communities without raising suspicion.

**Cornerstone:** Just to hear myself talk like that, I feel almost like a spy! But it's based on practical experience.

**Cornerstone:** I think, weighing the options ...

**Cornerstone:** ...

**"Sooner or later, we will reach an age where even concrete does not exist."**

**Cornerstone:** I'm going to help this building become an architect.

**Cornerstone:** Or try my best anyways, at least to give it the fundamentals of architectural theory. Hopefully, I can persuade it to accept you as occupants while it learns from me. Even so, I fear we might not have enough time before the next "Storm" arrives.

**Rossella:** Are you sure?

**Cornerstone:** I know. It doesn't make a lot of sense, does it?

**Cornerstone:** Call it an arcanist's passion. I don't often even really think of myself that way. But we are not always ruled by what makes sense. Right now, this is the path that's calling me. I have to trust my intuition.

**Rossella:** I understand. You have our support, Ms. Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone:** I couldn't ask for that. This is a personal conviction.

**Rossella:** Maybe, but I'm willing to share it with you.

**Rossella:** As director of the Barcelona branch office, I offer you our full and unequivocal support on this task.

**Cornerstone:** Oh. Forgive me. I didn't realize you were ...

**Rossella:** Branch Director? I guess I didn't feel like it amounted to much. I'm the Director of Bushes and Tents ...

**Rossella:** I know the Foundation's goals here are lofty. I admit when I first heard them, I thought we were completely out of our minds—an arcanist's pie-in-the-sky dreaming, no chance of any practical implementation.

**Rossella:** But even if it is an unachievable dream, I think it's worth pursuing. The Foundation needs to stand up, be present, and make an effort to help. Even if this era or even the whole world is doomed to melt away in the rain, I want to know that we tried.

**Rossella:** Maybe adapting existing buildings is the wiser and more practical choice.

**Rossella:** But I haven't given up hope on our project here.

**Cornerstone:** I appreciate it.
