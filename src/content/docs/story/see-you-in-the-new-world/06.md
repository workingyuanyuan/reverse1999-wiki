## Story

**Kenna:** Ever since that last horrific Temporal Anomaly, we've been working hard to push administrative reforms.

**Kenna:** Of course, we decided to call it the "Storm" now.

**Kenna:** This is our first draft of the "Storm" Shelter Notice. We plan to work with Laplace to develop a harsh, peculiar sound effect that will "draw the maximum possible attention from humans and arcanists."

**Kenna:** This notice may help save more Foundation personnel when the next "Storm" arrives.

**Kenna:** You should look it over too.

**Grundriss:** Mm. Restrictions on the weight of personal belongings, procedures for bringing unregistered persons, emergency measures for the Storm Syndrome ...

**Kenna:** I hear Laplace is also researching transport methods based on arcane skills, so even those more prone to wanderlust, like our architect friend, can reach headquarters safely.

**Kenna:** Planes are too slow, too unreliable to make any guarantees. We lost a group of investigators last time when their plane had trouble getting flight clearance. They were turned into a tangled mess of wires, fused into the plane ...

**Kenna:** Fortunately, before the plane hit the ground, they'd already been washed away.

**Grundriss:** Mr. Irinei assured me everything would get back on track.

Grundriss hesitates slightly as she selects the words to begin with.

**Grundriss:** Ms. Kenna, I heard Plesetsk Cosmodrome has begun systematically transferring personnel and projects back to the local branch. Does that mean ...

**Kenna:** It's just a precaution. Laplace's arcane energy readings have picked up an anomaly. We're not certain of anything yet. We're keeping our investigators in the field for now.

**Kenna:** Besides, there've been several reports like it over the past few months. All of them were eventually confirmed not to be signs of an impending "Storm."

**Kenna:** I imagine Madam Constantine is eager for a method that can predict the timing of the "Storm" more accurately.

**Grundriss:** That isn't what I meant.

**Kenna:** Oh, I see. You're worried about Cornerstone.

**Grundriss:** If you insist on putting it that way. Fine. I do want to know whether you warned Cornerstone in advance that a "Storm" might arrive soon.

**Grundriss:** She's still sightseeing in Barcelona. Given how absent-minded she is, I'm worried she won't even find the airport before the "Storm" falls.

**Kenna:** The Barcelona office is currently one of the most conveniently located sites for transit. As long as our personnel board immediately after the "Storm" warning is issued, they'll be able to touch down in headquarters in under two hours.

**Grundriss:** I understand. I was just overthinking things.

**Irinei:** I'm afraid Grundriss's concern isn't unfounded.

**Grundriss:** Mr. Irinei?!

Kenna gives a nod of acknowledgement to her colleague, though the strain between them is obvious.

**Irinei:** I've heard you've been spending quite a lot of time with Ms. Kenna recently, and indeed, here I find the two of you together.

**Grundriss:** I just came to ask about the "Storm."

**Irinei:** Your timing is impeccable then. Laplace has sent out the warning. Another "Storm" will arrive in two to three hours.

**Irinei:** We're already alerting our branches.

**Kenna:** I appreciate you coming personally to notify me.

**Irinei:** Don't mention it. Your people performed beautifully in Nowa Miedź, after all.

**Irinei:** And yes, I mean that sincerely.

As soon as Irinei finishes speaking, a piercing alarm rings through the entire Foundation headquarters.

**Irinei:** Looks like we're short on time.

Barcelona Office

Streams of gray-and-white checked figures flow through the building's east and south doors, officials waiting inside carefully checking their materials.

One struggling figure runs against the crowd and enters the building.

"Tap, tap, tap, tap." Like the crisp clatter of hooves, the sound is drowned in the somewhat panicked footsteps of the crowd.

Everyone thinks that if they get out even one second sooner, they'll be that much more likely to escape the coming "Storm."

Only the sound of hooves grows closer and closer, heading deeper into the building.

**Rossella:** Ms. Grundriss!

Rossella grabs Cornerstone as she runs against the flow of people.

**Rossella:** Oh, Ms. Cornerstone! Did you forget something? The "Storm" will be here any moment!

**Cornerstone:** I know. But I ...

**Rossella:** ...

**Rossella:** I understand. The final transport won't arrive for another five minutes.

**Rossella:** Once all personnel are assembled, we'll take off immediately. After that, you'll have to find another way back to headquarters.

**Cornerstone:** Thank you!

**Cornerstone:** \*pant\*

**"Building":** Cornerstone? You're back?

**"Building":** What happened? Why are people running out? I didn't make them leave.

**Cornerstone:** I ...

She has no idea how to explain what's happening.

**Cornerstone:** I'm here.

**Cornerstone:** I went to the old exposition site. There's a building there by an architect I've always admired.

**Cornerstone:** It was demolished more than 50 years ago, then rebuilt last year. I drove there yesterday just to see it.

The architect avoids the question and begins speaking about something completely unrelated.

**Cornerstone:** The last time I saw it, I was just a child.

**"Building":** I don't understand. You don't look 60 years old. How can that be?

**Cornerstone:** No, haha, not quite.

The timing is entirely wrong for her to laugh.

**Cornerstone:** I'm going to tell that to Grundriss. There's a whole new category of jokes left untapped.

**Cornerstone:** ...

**"Building":** I already know. Something is going to destroy this place, including me.

**Cornerstone:** Yes. The "Storm."

**"Building":** Then you'll hide inside another building?

**Cornerstone:** It's the only one we know that can protect us.

**"Building":** It must be amazing!

**"Building":** I would have liked to have been a building like that—a shelter.

**"Building":** That way, people wouldn't need to rush out of me in such a hurry.

**Cornerstone:** We-We can't take you with us.

**Cornerstone:** I'm so ...

**"Building":** No. It's just like you said—

**"Building":** Perhaps ten thousand years ago, when some Homo sapiens built a little shelter because they wanted to keep out the rain, the idea of "architecture" was born.

**"Building":** Stasia, I still don't have a name. But I think I'm fine with just being called what I am.

**"Building":** I was so happy that I could shield you from the wind and rain, even if it was only for a short while.

**"Building":** Goodbye.

Time passes second by second, and Cornerstone hears the urgent honking of horns in the distance.

**Cornerstone:** Goodbye, my friend.

**Cornerstone:** I'll come back here. I promise.

**Cornerstone:** It may not matter, but we'll rebuild you.

The architect leaves without looking back.

Behind her, the fountain pen she used floats in the air and begins to sway rhythmically.

A last lesson the building had learned—an imitation of the human ritual of farewell.

**"Building":** Why do humans make buildings?

**Cornerstone:** Oh!

Distracted by the question, the tower of playing cards Cornerstone had meticulously built on the table comes tumbling down.

**Cornerstone:** Well, do you want the standard textbook answer or my personal thoughts?

**"Building":** Either.

**Cornerstone:** Let me think.

Cornerstone gathers up the playing cards and instead begins searching for balance among three soda crackers.

**Cornerstone:** For humans, the first real shelters would have caves. Natural protective alcoves of stone and earth, not unlike a burrow for rabbits.

**Cornerstone:** To defend against wild beasts, against other humans, against wind and rain ...

**Cornerstone:** A space created to meet a need—that was the first definition people gave to architecture.

**Cornerstone:** But the real question came after that.

**Cornerstone:** Perhaps one day, a human who failed to make it back to the cave got caught in the pouring rain. She had no choice but to pile together a shelter from wood and leaves, something that could keep the water off her back.

**Cornerstone:** And in that moment, as the rain fell beyond but she remained warm and dry, she discovered that the small world beneath her new shelter was something new, something cozy.

**Cornerstone:** That was the first true building, more than just a structure. It was a home.

**Cornerstone:** A place she could experience the beauty of nature while in comfort and security.

**"Building":** Wow! Is that a true story?

**Cornerstone:** Probably not. But I think it's the message of the story that matters.

**Cornerstone:** The concept has a strict textbook definition. Thatched huts and those ugly little lean-to shelters are the rigorous and objective origins of architecture.

**Cornerstone:** But perhaps such a romantic story really did occur somewhere in history.

When she walked away from the stuffy cave and was caught in heavy rain on the road ...

When she broke off branches, built a rain shelter, and gazed through tender leaves at the world beyond ...

The concept of "architecture" was born in that moment.
