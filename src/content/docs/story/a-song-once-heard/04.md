## Story

23:40

A Temple in the Mountains

A Temple in the Mountains

Tap to light the candle

***

***

**Musician:** "So this is the temple of the Miemengs?"

Seeing the lights kindle before him, the musician pauses.

**Getian:** "It seems this place is not quite what you had in mind."

The musician carefully steps around the layers of paper hung like drapes, almost tripping on the scattered scrolls strewn about the ground.

**Musician:** "Well, I must say, this is nothing like anything I've seen before."

He notices a bone beneath the papers and is unable to hold back his question.

**Musician:** "This is ... the remains of your kin?"

The Miemeng steps further into the temple, not looking back.

**Getian:** "Correct."

**Musician:** "Is this a custom of the mountains? Handling them this way feels somewhat irreverent."

The Miemeng's voice comes from ahead.

**Getian:** "They are gone—but flesh and bone. Why fuss over it?"

His voice pauses, tinged with curiosity.

**Getian:** "Your concern tells me it is different in the lands below. How does your kind treat the dead?"

**Musician:** "Well, we also have temples. In them, we gather the remains of the deceased, assess their deeds in life, and enshrine them."

**Getian:** "How do you assess one's deeds?"

The musician freezes again, unsure of how to answer.

**Musician:** "How do we assess deeds? By whether they benefited others or whether they served only their own aspirations ..."

**Getian:** "But can anyone but oneself truly know the nature of one's aspirations? How could others possibly judge such a thing?"

On such a night, in such a place, the Miemeng's voice sounds eerily calm, echoing as if both near and far.

**Getian:** "This is precisely what troubles me. By your account, those who descended the mountain must have chosen to pursue their own aspirations."

**Getian:** "But how were they to confirm the purpose of their lives? On what basis?"

**Musician:** "Basis? Now I really don't understand what you mean."

Getian walks back a few steps, gesturing for his companion to look in his direction.

**Getian:** "On the way here, I told you of three of my kin."

There's a scattered pile of bones.

Getian picks one up.

**Getian:** "For perfecting principles."

And then a second one.

**Getian:** "For humans' joy and sorrow."

And a final third bone.

**Getian:** "For the injustice of the world."

The Miemeng gazes at them in thought.

**Musician:** "Remarkable aspirations indeed. Nothing like those of ordinary mortals."

The musician murmurs.

**Getian:** "Perhaps. But now, that is all but a moment from the past, and they have returned here, silent and still."

**Getian:** "If they had foreseen such an ending, would they still have made the same choice?"

**Musician:** "..."

**Getian:** "You said you were willing to die for a score. To me, that notion is no different from that of my kin. I am curious how you came to your choice."

**Musician:** "So that's how you see it."

**Musician:** "To give everything for one's purpose in life is indeed a difficult thing to choose. And yet, to have the choice itself may be a blessing."

**Musician:** "In the mortal world, countless people live without freedom of choice."

As he gives this vague reply, his gaze drifts absently out the window. The moon has begun to sink westward, and the sky has shifted from a shade of deep inky black to dark blue.

Dawn is still some time away.

He withdraws his gaze, clears his throat, and changes the subject.

**Musician:** "How are we to find clues about the score among all these scrolls and bones? I can't imagine we'll come up with much of anything."

**Getian:** "There's no need to worry. Just play something."

**Musician:** "What?"

**Getian:** "Please. Your ancestor bonded with the Miemeng through music. If any clues remain here, they will respond."

**Musician:** "..."

A mountain wind blows into the temple, causing the hanging papers to rustle.

The musician reaches out to hold down a scroll that is almost blown away, offering an apologetic smile to his friend.

**Musician:** "I'm afraid the damage my instrument suffered in the mountains hasn't been repaired yet."

The Miemeng thinks for a moment.

**Getian:** "I believe that can be resolved. I do not know what your instrument is made of, but whatever you need ought to be here in the temple."

**Musician:** "..."

The gentle smile on his face slowly fades.

> **Choice 1：** Wait quietly

**Getian:** "..."

The Miemeng lowers his head, studying the musician's eyes. The wind flickers the candlelight, casting the musician's face in shifting shadows.

After a moment, he lowers his head and sighs.

**Due to your decision, you've grown more confused.**

> **Choice 2：** Offer to repair the guqin

Getian's gaze falls on the musician's guqin.

**Getian:** "Perhaps my arcane skill can be of help."

**Musician:** "..."

His lips move, but in the end, he only lets out another sigh.

**Musician:** "I appreciate it, but there's no need. I know how to repair a guqin."

\*bang\*

He unfastens the guqin and throws it to the ground. It lands with a heavy thud.

**Musician:** "Please believe me, I have no ill intent."
