## Story

19:30Clear

Military Camp

***

***

The lieutenant drifts back into her memories, slowly tracing her way through the past.

Seizing the moment, she begins telling the old man a story about a girl she knows better than anyone, a girl no one else could ever know as well.

**Moldir:** "When I was sixteen, I received two pieces of paper."

Red and orange firelight washes over her face, while outside its glow, people laugh and dance, fluttering through the shadows like butterflies beating their wings toward another world.

**Moldir:** "The first told me that my parents were gone. The second that I had a new father."

The lieutenant murmurs the words to herself by the fire.

**Moldir:** "No one ever asked what I thought, and back then, not one choice in my life was mine to make."

Military Camp, Outside the Ancient City of Ai-Khanoum

She makes herself a little space by the fire and sits, left leg relaxed and bent, hands resting at her sides, vision locked on the burning embers.

The old man stands beside her, listening in silence.

**Moldir:** "I was his first child."

**Moldir:** "No other soldier has remained at his side as long as I have."

**Moldir:** "Some he took in after me, and some fought beside him long before I arrived, but few have made it all the way to now."

**Moldir:** "That day, the Admiral, my adoptive father, took me to the Hall of Merit at headquarters."

She picks up a dry twig and stirs the fire with it.

Hall of Merit, Zeno Arms Academy

**Moldir:** "Solemn. Silent. They call it a memorial hall, a place that houses the deeds of the fallen."

**Moldir:** "Whether soldier or commander, the fallen all have a place there."

**Moldir:** "Cold metal, stone, and light."

A far cry from the warm, living fire before her.

**Moldir:** "I found my mother's and father's names there. My biological father."

**Moldir:** "They sacrificed their lives for a worthy cause."

**Moldir:** "Heroes and martyrs both."

**Moldir:** "And I ..."

She pauses, waiting for the words to come.

**Moldir:** "I am the child of martyrs, yet I find myself on a path I never could have dreamed of."

A wry smile crosses her face, an acknowledgment of fate's cruel irony.

**Moldir:** "Seeing their portraits, I felt a strange calm, hoping that one day I, too, could be there beside them."

**Moldir:** "Unfortunately, that hope became reality."

**Old Man:** "Why do you say 'unfortunately'?"

The old man doesn't look at her; he keeps his eyes steady on the flame, his expression neutral.

**Moldir:** "I've been told that my adoptive father's name and mine are now etched into that hall."

She answers with a bitter smile.

**Moldir:** "I can't say if it was meant as a joke or an insult, but I know that I'll never have the chance to see it with my own eyes."

**Moldir:** "Will our past honors be preserved? Will I bring shame to my parents' legacy?"

**Moldir:** "They called us traitors."

She shakes her head, looking at the old man, but he remains motionless.

**Old Man:** "We are nothing more than driftwood carried on the current."

He repeats his earlier words; the crackling firelight dances over his face.

**Old Man:** "This is your story."

**Moldir:** "It's part of my story."

**Moldir:** "Fate has treated me well. It took one family from me, then gave me another, like those two letters."

**Moldir:** "The Admiral took in other orphans like me, bringing them under his command."

**Moldir:** "At the arms academy, or at his camp."

**Moldir:** "As his daughter, I was given authority over the youngest adoptees."

**Moldir:** "In better times, they should have still been in their parents' arms, not fighting in some cold barren field being corralled and commanded to obey."

**Old Man:** "My granddaughter was the same way when she was a child."

**Old Man:** "A wild little foal, always so stubborn and fierce."

**Moldir:** "I thought she’d be as gentle as she looks."

She laughs softly and bitterly before she returns to the topic.

**Moldir:** "Not long after, my father brought a few boys to join us. They were close, as close as cubs raised in the same den."

**Moldir:** "But years passed, and the young cubs fell until only Ptolemy was left. War took his brothers, and along with them, an eye."

**Moldir:** "He became cold and cruel after that, twisted by one mission after another."

**Moldir:** "Ten set out, and only three returned."

**Moldir:** "If he were numb to it all, he'd have learned to disregard death, to the point where his own life would mean nothing, and he'd become a fugitive."

**Moldir:** "And if he wasn't, every one of their deaths turned that anger further inward, and each turn made him that much more ruthless."

**Moldir:** "And I can't blame him. Because I know what molded him."

**Moldir:** "Even so, I know his sins cannot be washed away."

The words come out calm without condemnation.

**Old Man:** "But you still see him as your family?"

**Moldir:** "Family? Always, the same as these boys."

The lieutenant tilts her head with a half-hearted smile as she gestures toward the other side of the fire.

A few of the soldiers spot their sister and wave from afar, holding half a roasted lamb leg.

**Moldir:** "I just hope that this time they'll get to live, not just survive."

**Moldir:** "But they're hungry for battle; many of them think it will be like hunting rabbits on the steppe."

**Moldir:** "But in war, the hunters and the hunted are one and the same."

**Moldir:** "All they know of death and sacrifice comes from stories or propaganda."

A trace of disgust flickers across her face.

**Moldir:** "I hope that it will all be as easy as they think—winning honor and glory."

She sighs.

Honor. Glory.

The words glimmer like two medals soaked in blood.

**Old Man:** "So, you see these soldiers as your family."

**Moldir:** "Yes. They are all my brothers. I also have a sister."

**Old Man:** "And your sister? What kind of person did she turn out to be?"

**Moldir:** "She's still the same stubborn little girl."

**Moldir:** "Luckier than Ptolemy."

Two emotions cross her face and battle beneath the surface: nostalgia and regret.

**Moldir:** "The last time I saw her, we had raised our guns toward one another."

**Moldir:** "She said goodbye with a bullet. It was her way of returning the one I shot at her in the rain."
