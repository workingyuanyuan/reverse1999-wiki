## Story

20:20Clear

Military Camp

Military Camp, Outside the Ancient City of Ai-Khanoum

***

***

**Moldir:** "It's getting late."

**Moldir:** "I appreciate you listening to me, sir."

**Old Man:** "You are my guest."

The lieutenant nods respectfully.

The music has picked up again, a familiar song that brings a wave of cheers from the nearby revelers.

Smoke rises up into the night sky before fading into the canopy of stars.

**Moldir:** "May I ask that young lady over there if I can borrow the dombra?"

**Old Man:** "Of course."

He raises his hands and claps. The musician, noticing the gesture, stops playing and walks over with the instrument in hand.

**Old Man:** "The young commander wishes to perform a song for us."

The old man calls out to the people in the yard.

**Moldir:** "Haha, please, I didn't mean to stop everything on my account."

**Moldir:** "I suddenly feel as if I'm about to embarrass myself."

She takes the instrument, testing the sound, like reacquainting with an old friend.

Her soldiers gather around, eager to join in.

Curious villagers trail behind, edging closer to the honored guest.

**Young Officer:** "You're gonna play a song, Lieutenant?"

**Moldir:** "That's right. I thought I might show you all that I can relax too."

She sits down with the instrument, facing the bonfire and the people waiting by it, and begins to play; her voice, initially soft, rises into a clear and bright song—a memory of her homeland.

**Moldir:** "Oh golden summer, you've lingered too long. ♪"

**Moldir:** "We lift our cups to the dwindling days. ♪"

As she plays, several voices join in harmony, following and stumbling over the words.

**Moldir:** "To salute the sun as it sinks away. ♪"

**Moldir:** "Oh golden years of youth, how fast you've gone. ♪"

**Moldir:** "For cold winter's breath will find us in the dawn. ♪"

**Moldir:** "Reminding us to savor our days and the warmth of the sun. ♪"

**Moldir:** "So live and love while the night is young. ♪"

She's stood up without ever realizing it, losing herself in her song, as if she were lifted from time and space entirely.

...

...

**Moldir:** "Alright, alright. That's enough for now."

She waves away the cheers of the crowd and promptly returns the dombra.

**Moldir:** "It's been so long since I last played. I'd almost forgotten how ..."

She offers a self-deprecating chuckle, shaking her head as her voice fades away.

**Moldir:** "Go on, enjoy yourselves."

At this, the crowd breaks out in laughter and then begins to follow the musician as they resume the previous melody.

As the crowd disperses, the old man approaches again.

**Old Man:** "Do you think about her often?"

> **Choice 1：** Yes, often.

**Your decision changes what is yet to come.**

**Moldir:** "..."

The lieutenant loses herself in thought, reflecting on the past. Until, after a long pause, she speaks.

**Moldir:** "When she joined us, things were a lot like this. We danced, we celebrated, and I remember playing that song for them as well."

**Moldir:** "She's always been a fighter, from the moment we met."

**Moldir:** "There is a difference between that and a soldier."

**Moldir:** "Just like there's a difference between us."

> **Choice 2：** Now and then, but ...

**Moldir:** "She'll always be my sister, but that wildness in her ..."

**Moldir:** "We grew apart. Maybe there was nothing that could stop us from growing apart."

**Moldir:** "All that matters to me now are the lives of these soldiers under my command."

**Old Man:** "The past is a river we've already crossed; tomorrow matters, yesterday does not."

**Moldir:** "You're right."

**Moldir:** "I suppose she was always going to grow up."

**Moldir:** "My sister isn't the kind to feel helpless. She won't cry herself to sleep if I'm not there with her."

**Moldir:** "She's a Zeno soldier."

The lieutenant stresses the words, her gaze firm.

**Moldir:** "She made that choice for herself."

**Moldir:** "We tried to force her down a path she couldn't follow."

**Moldir:** "Sometimes, I wonder if I had just explained things to her from the beginning, if I had said the right words, would she have chosen differently?"

Her tone is wistful; it catches her off-guard to have let something so naive slip out.

**Moldir:** "I don't know."

She shakes her head. It wasn't that she didn't know. It's that she knew there was no answer.

**Moldir:** "She's always lived according to her own rules, her own conscience."

**Moldir:** "And I would never take that from her."

A bitterness holds on her tongue as tart as the old man's almonds. Her sister's image feels faint and distant; she can't even recall the sound of her voice.

What comes to her mind, instead, is the thundering rattle of gunfire.

A sweltering afternoon. Thick, humid air that clings to her skin, mixing with beads of sweat.

She guides her sister's hand, telling her to aim at the target and not hesitate when pulling the trigger.

Decisive and resolute.

**Moldir:** "Just like her dice games, she could lose every time and never regret it, no matter how badly she lost."

**Moldir:** "Perhaps while I was away, she really grew up."
