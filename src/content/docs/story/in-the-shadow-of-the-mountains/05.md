## Story

19:50Clear

Military Camp

Military Camp, Outside the Ancient City of Ai-Khanoum

***

***

**Moldir:** "Most of them have never shot at anything that could shoot back."

**Moldir:** "Rabbits, birds, deer, maybe a wolf or a bear."

**Moldir:** "A hunter is not a soldier just because he can fire a rifle. No more than climbing on a horse makes you a knight."

The old man nods in firm agreement.

**Old Man:** "You speak truly."

**Moldir:** "To be a soldier is to be both the best and the worst of humanity."

**Moldir:** "I still remember their names."

Her voice trails off.

After taking two deep breaths, she begins again, reciting the names as if they were a solemn vow.

**Moldir:** "Marshall, who was always better with a sword than a gun, Whitewood, who took better care of his books than his rifle, and Vasilisa, with her falcon ..."

Their names linger as ghostly wisps in the night air.

**Moldir:** "Lopera and I were supposed to go out on a mission; it was her first opportunity to witness a battlefield firsthand."

**Moldir:** "But just before we left, Father decided to leave the two of us behind."

> **Choice 1：** I still don't understand why.

**Moldir:** "We were still at the station, waiting for the train, when the messenger showed up with orders for us to turn back to base."

**Moldir:** "By the time I finished reading, the train had pulled into the station. The others started getting on, one after another."

**Moldir:** "The disappointment on her face hardened into something else."

Sadness became protest. Protest turned to rage.

**Moldir:** "All I could do was grab her hand and tell her to follow orders."

**Moldir:** "I didn't understand Father's reasoning, but I followed his orders."

**Moldir:** "Just as I always have."

**Moldir:** "I keep my opinions to myself, and I follow my orders."

> **Choice 2：** I remember how disappointed she was.

**Your decision changes what is yet to come.**

**Moldir:** "Lopera screamed in the middle of the station, demanding to know why she couldn't go to the front."

**Moldir:** "The fall was colder than usual, and it was already snowing. I can still picture those white flakes that dotted her orange hair."

**Moldir:** "As she yelled and protested, all the soldiers turned to look at us. So many familiar faces. I thought perhaps the message meant that the war was ending, that we weren't needed there."

**Moldir:** "But that was the last time I saw many of those faces. Some of them I once called siblings too."

**Moldir:** "They went to war. We went home. Those were Father's orders."

She lowers her head.

**Moldir:** "It wasn't long after that we received the first of their remains. Lopera dug the graves herself."

**Moldir:** "It was snowing that day too."

She feels a chill run down her spine, one that doesn't belong to the cold air around her but to that distant memory.

**Moldir:** "Fate brought me to my father and then, one after another, brought others to our side."

**Moldir:** "They would come and go, live and die, then more would come. Orphans created by and for war."

Exhaustion lingers in her voice.

**Moldir:** "War forces us to grow to adapt to our conditions, to the brutality around us."

**Moldir:** "It's not right to say 'grow'; we survive. Those that adapt survive."

She bites down on her lower lip, her brow furrowing.

**Moldir:** "My sister has been on many missions. But I don't know if she's ever seen that side of war for herself. I never got the chance to ask. I never wanted to—"

She opens her hands.

**Moldir:** "I hope not. I hope, in some way, she never has to adapt to the cruelties that I have: the brutal calculus of command. She's suited to what she does. She isn't meant for the front lines."

The old man pulls an almond from his sleeve and hands it to her.

**Moldir:** "Thank you."

The lieutenant takes it, her face scrunching involuntarily, anticipating the oncoming tartness.

**Old Man:** "And what was it that changed you?"

**Moldir:** "A battle that wasn't worth remembering, when I shot a man I didn't know."

A Historical Battlefield

Years ago, the Admiral brought his daughter to the battlefield.

They flew in during the final days of the war. She was still being trained as an officer.

From the window of their plane, she could see husks of concrete buildings jutting out like bones over the scarred landscape.

It was her first real taste of the battle—an assault on a fortified position.

**Moldir:** "We secured the street. We had tanks then, large armored vehicles with cannons."

She explains for the man. Though perhaps even the notion of a motor vehicle might elude him.

**Moldir:** "But tanks are no use in taking a building. I led the troops in; I think we had reached the third or fourth floor without opposition.

**Moldir:** "Then I heard a voice. A man speaking in a language I didn't know. I turned to see a stranger."

**Moldir:** "It didn't take long before he saw me too."

**Moldir:** "I reached for my gun. He raised his."

The lieutenant taps her thumb on her chest, signaling with a small gesture.

The old man nods in understanding, his wrinkled thumb turning the lapis lazuli beads on his wrist.

**Moldir:** "I reacted on instinct. Father's training. It was over in a single shot."

She sighs.

**Moldir:** "Then, I don't know why, but I crumpled to the ground and lay there. My mind went blank."

**Moldir:** "My comrades came running over; they thought I had been hit."

Her hand trembles on her chest. The moment replays itself in her mind.

**Moldir:** "They told me I was crying and throwing up as they dragged me out to safety."

**Moldir:** "All that training. All that was expected of me. Honor and glory, a martyr's death."

**Moldir:** "But I only killed that man because I was afraid to die myself."

**Moldir:** "I couldn't escape the look on his face as I shot him."

**Moldir:** "For a long time after, even in training, if I heard a gunshot, I would feel sick to my stomach."

**Moldir:** "Until one day I changed. That sickness in my stomach just went away."

**Moldir:** "Or maybe I learned to live with it."

The old man stops twisting the beads with his fingers. The fire twinkles back at her in the bright blue reflection.

**Old Man:** "It is a feeling all soldiers must learn to live with."

#### Notes · Marshall

A fallen Zeno soldier. Killed in action at 19 years of age in the southern foothills of the Caucasus on April 14th, 1966.

#### Notes · Whitewood

A fallen Zeno soldier. Lost at sea at 20 years of age after the Cape of Good Hope shipwreck on October 23rd, 1913.

#### Notes · Vasilisa

A fallen Zeno soldier. Killed in action at 22 years of age in the southern Balkans on November 25th, 1912.
