**Moldir:** "I used to call her Pera."

**Moldir:** "Pera. Lopera."

There wasn't any trick to the name; it was short and simple.

**Moldir:** "First, press your lips together, then part them, and gently drop your tongue toward your chin."

**Moldir:** "It sounds light, floral maybe, like the frail creature I saw when I met her."

**Moldir:** "An unripe orange, fallen to the ground too soon."

**Moldir:** "Do oranges grow around here?"

She asks the old man, who gently shakes his head.

**Moldir:** "Right. I'd never seen an orange back home, either."

She asks herself a question, then answers it.

**Moldir:** "Tarter than grapes, sweeter than plums."

Zeno Arms Academy

**Moldir:** "A runaway child."

**Moldir:** "Father brought her in, and we couldn't help but be curious about the little girl with her orange hair."

**Moldir:** "Ptolemy sized her up with his one good eye."

**Moldir:** "I remember the first thing he said to her: 'You're shorter than I expected.'"

She mimics her brother's tone as she speaks.

**Moldir:** "Lopera walked right up to him, stood up on her tiptoes, and pulled him down by the collar. 'Call me short again, I dare you.'"

**Moldir:** "And just like that we were all laughing; Ptolemy could still laugh back then. But not Father. Even then, he stood there, solid as stone."

**Moldir:** "I could see him watching her with that cold, calculating gaze, studying her, waiting for her next move."

**Old Man:** "I know what he must have been thinking. Most fathers would feel the same."

**Moldir:** "Ptolemy, naturally, couldn't help but meet the challenge. He went down a list of words, 'runt, shrimp, short stuff,' as if he had them prepared. He's always had a cruel sense of humor."

**Moldir:** "Father and I simply sat back and waited for what would happen next."

She sighs. Reliving these memories has been a welcome rest from her Exhaustion and Mental Strain.

**Moldir:** "She practically leaped as she yanked down on his collar; Ptolemy tumbled over her shoulder like a sack of oats."

**Moldir:** "I can still remember that stunned look on his face, staring up at the sky, then we all turned to Father. I already knew what we would see. The same nearly imperceptible smile he gave any of us when he was pleased."

**Moldir:** "Ptolemy was furious; he pulled her down into the mud right along with him. The training grounds were always muddy, even on sunny days. I think that was by design."

**Moldir:** "I tried to pull them apart, yelled at them to stop, but Ptolemy had long since stopped listening to me. And Lopera ... I'm not sure she ever did."

**Moldir:** "There was nothing I could do."

She says it with a smile.

**Moldir:** "Eventually, I managed to grab ahold of my new sister, like grabbing an orange, and pulled her out from the muck. Ptolemy reached for her, but I stood in his way."

**Moldir:** "I told him he needed to cool off."

**Moldir:** "That was their first fight, certainly not the last. I'm sure those two fought more than anyone else."

**Moldir:** "All of our siblings fought. Father encouraged it. He believed it made us tougher, more resilient. Sometimes that was true."

The old man nods, as if in agreement.

**Moldir:** "I taught her how to hold a gun."

**Moldir:** "I also taught her how to ride, how to match the horse's rhythm, and how to aim while on horseback."

Happy memories flood to the surface, playing out like a movie lit by the flickering flames.

**Moldir:** "That's how things were, the good and the bad."

"But things change."

The sun has long since set, and a clear starry sky has replaced it.

The chill mountain air creeps up from the darkness, making the bonfire's heat feel all the more precious.

She shifts her gaze up from the fire to the moon. A familiar sight in an unfamiliar time.

**Moldir:** "We come from somewhere very far away."

She tells her story.

**Moldir:** "But no matter where we go, the moon is the same."

**Moldir:** "When I was young, I would often sit with my grandmother and watch it as it hung above the sky."

**Moldir:** "Later, whenever we weren't on patrol or training late, my sister and I would do the same."

**Moldir:** "I remember once, when she was still young, she ran up to me, tugging at my sleeve, saying, 'Molly, I've got something to show you!'"

**Moldir:** "She pulled me up from my desk and dragged me outside."

**Moldir:** "I followed her to the courtyard, and there was that pale moon. She stood beside me, looking up at it."

The lieutenant lets her head fall back, looking up at the dark and starry sky.

**Old Man:** "You left her."

**Moldir:** "Yes, I did. She was free to follow her own sense of right and wrong, to choose a different path. That's who she is, and that's how it should be."

**Moldir:** "I wonder if she still looks up in the sky and thinks of me."

She closes her eyes.

**Moldir:** "I told her once."

**Moldir:** "We're soldiers. Goodbye is a luxury in our profession."

**Moldir:** "Ptolemy understood that all too well, and so did the other soldiers."

**Moldir:** "But Lopera never seemed to take it seriously; she thought of it like a game."

She hesitates.

**Moldir:** "It wasn't."

She casts a glance at the soldiers singing and dancing with the villagers.

#### Notes · Exhaustion and Mental Strain

For months, she hasn't had a moment's rest, working to keep her people together, backing her father, and at times fretting over the sister who chose a different path.
