## Story

Bar Helicon

The man behind the bar sets down the shaker he's just finished washing, dries his hands with practiced ease, and pushes up his glasses.

**Apollo:** O gods! Wherever did you find our favorite "interlopers"?

**Hermes:** Right outside your door, as it happens. Oooh, new couches.

Hermes throws himself into the wide couch and lets himself sink into the cushions.

**Hermes:** But why replace the old ones with these four-seaters? Don't tell me. You got an oracle for a big crowd tonight, right?

**Apollo:** Hermes, how many times must I explain that oracles and prophecies are not the same? An oracle offers divine counsel, not infallible predictions of the future, and I highly doubt any prophet would waste their time prophesying a headcount.

**Apollo:** My friend, it really is simple. I suppose you only meant to jest, but these kinds of questions can get you into trouble. As for you, Narcissus, you'll be wanting your regular table, I presume?

He switches tones casually between prophet and barman.

**Sonetto:** Do you come here often?

Behind Sonetto, Narcissus turns her head, doing her best to avoid eye contact with the man behind the bar.

**Narcissus:** Ahem. That table there—shall we?

**Apollo:** You're most welcome to take any table you like, ladies. You've more than earned it after the day you've had.

Apollo greets the newcomers, gliding past the awkward little scene a certain someone had stirred up.

**Apollo:** It's been a long time since we've had any guests on this island. It's a true pleasure to personally welcome you.

He rests a hand over his heart and leans forward in a graceful bow.

Then, Apollo glances toward the Nymph hiding behind her new friends and sighs.

**Apollo:** But first, I must bend your ear, my dear Narcissus.

**Apollo:** Are you still fixed on the path you determined from your oracle? Are you certain in your conviction to abandon those you once held close until you achieve victory?

**Narcissus:** You, of all people, should be the last person to sway me from my destined path, don't you think? Besides, you've all been more than happy to work for that good-for-nothing tyrant Hedone.

**Narcissus:** I know you have your reasons, but as far as I see it, you're all working against the will of fate itself.

She raises her hand to the sky as she makes her declaration.

**Apollo:** My dear, I sometimes believe you speak more of fate and destiny than I do.

**Apollo:** But I'm pleased that the fates led you here tonight. So, what will it be?

**Narcissus:** I ... I ...

Hermes starts humming a tune from the couch as Narcissus hesitates beside him.

**Vertin:** Sonetto and I are rather curious about your local specialties. I don't suppose you'd stay awhile to tell us more.

**Sonetto:** Hmm. I hope they have something non-alcoholic.

Sonetto eyes the dazzling wall of liquor behind Apollo, a little worried.

**Apollo:** Naturally, my dears. But first, I'll fetch you each one of our signature drinks. What about you, Narcissus? The usual?

**Narcissus:** Hmm. Fine. Just, just one.

She shuffles over to take her seat at the bar.

**Vertin:** I have to say, I'm still a little in awe at the blend of modernity and tradition here.

**Apollo:** Hahaha, we have adapted to the times. A bar is a fitting post for an Oracle, wouldn't you agree? At least I'm not running messages or acting as a glorified referee.

Hermes clicks his tongue from the sofa.

**Hermes:** Oh, you're wicked, Apollo. Better not say that around Themis. She'll never play pinochle with us again.

**Apollo:** I would never. Now scooch over a little, please. I thought we could play a game with our guests.

**Vertin:** A game?

**Apollo:** Of a sort. Forgive me, ladies. I know you may wish to know much about this place and your purpose here.

**Apollo:** But my words are not given freely. Win a round, and I shall answer one question. How does that sound?

**Hermes:** Bring it on. I was ready from the word "game"!

Exchanging a glance with Sonetto, Hermes bolts up and slides over to them.

Three martini glasses are set beside the game deck at the center of the walnut table.

**Hermes:** Ahahaha, I'll pass, but you should try them. Of all his many talents, this may well be Apollo's finest.

**Narcissus:** Ugh. This again? I knew it.

**Sonetto:** I have a feeling this drink might be something Regulus would enjoy.

**Vertin:** Gingery, light, sparkling, like ginger ale.

**Hermes:** It is ginger ale. All he's done is dress it up to make it look fancy. Hold on, is this really your first time playing?

**Hermes:** 'Cause I'm starting to get suspicious. Ms. Sonetto, where'd you learn to calculate like that?

Hermes buries his face in his hands with a groan and tosses his whole hand of cards aside.

**Sonetto:** It really is my first time playing. Maybe it's just beginner's luck.

Her words come out as though the mere act of winning had violated some deeply held principle.

**Hermes:** Pft. Never apologize for winning. I'm just whining over sour grapes. Now, what's your first question?

**Sonetto:** We saw the fresco of Lady Hedone's journey. How much of what it depicts is true?

**Apollo:** All of it.

The outsiders grow still, their breath caught.

**Hermes:** Yep. As far as anyone can tell, it's a faithful record of her battle with Typhon. That fresco's been there for millennia.

**Hermes:** Made by the very first settlers of this island.

**Vertin:** Then, Hedone originally came here thousands of years ago, and the one we met is her reincarnation?

**Apollo:** Correct. She slumbered dormant and unseen until Typhon began to stir.

**Sonetto:** Reincarnators can recover memories from their past lives. How much does she recall?

Apollo falls silent for a moment. Even the usually lively messenger doesn't pick up the thread.

**Apollo:** All of them, I believe. Her memories returned to her early in her new life.

**Apollo:** And she has been bound up in the knowledge of her coming quest ever since.

**Vertin:** You mean, to fight Typhon again?

**Apollo:** I cannot say for certain what she intends. Only she knows her path. But the power she wields does not come without a price.

**Apollo:** To know one's destiny is written from the moment of their birth is an unimaginable burden, even for one as mighty as her.

**Apollo:** Even the merest glimpse into the fates' design can be calamitous to a mortal mind.

**Apollo:** I suppose that's why we Oracles speak in riddles.

**Sonetto:** Then, I wonder what she'll do next.

**Narcissus:** She's not going to die. That much is certain.

Silent until now, Narcissus breaks in with an earnest tone.

**Narcissus:** Wicked things never die easily, and she's the most wicked of them all.

**Hermes:** Hahahaha! Well, that's one way to put it, Narcissus.

**Hermes:** I can always count on you to bring some levity when Apollo gets all brooding and philosophical.

**Apollo:** She even surprises me at times, I must admit.

**Vertin:** But you're the one who spoke her oracle, aren't you?

**Apollo:** Of course. It is my duty.

**Apollo:** Though, I'm afraid Narcissus has taken this one rather too seriously.

He mumbles under his breath, glancing over at Narcissus by the bar.

Without fail, any mention of oracles sets her off.

**Narcissus:** But the oracle helped me find my blessing.

**Narcissus:** And through it, I found my champions. \*burp\*

The fizzy soda makes her burp, and she slaps her two webbed hands down on the heroes' shoulders.

Sonetto studies the melting ice and leftover liquid in her cup with concern.

**Sonetto:** Aren't these non-alcoholic?

**Narcissus:** The oracle has—hic—spoken it. We will defeat Hedone in the Aquatic Games. I'm certain of it.

The bartender steers the conversation back cautiously.

**Apollo:** It is true that the oracles I divine may provide some sort of answer for those who seek them.

**Apollo:** But an oracle is not a promise. The words of the gods can be inscrutable. How one hears them and how one chooses to follow their suggestions depends entirely on the individual.

Long since used to his friend's well-polished non-answers, Hermes shrugs it off.

**Hermes:** Right, what he said. Not that I'd know. No one's ever "oracled" for me before.

**Hermes:** But whether you defeat Hedone or not, you know you'll always be our friend, Narcissus, don't you?

**Hermes:** I know we all say that winning is all that matters here, but that doesn't mean we'd cast you aside just because you're, well, at a disadvantage.

**Narcissus:** I don't want to hear it.

She springs to her feet and then begins to climb atop her stool, standing in such a way as to make a show of turning her back on Hermes.

**Narcissus:** I DON'T NEED YOUR PITY, BECAUSE I WILL WIN THIS TIME!

**Sonetto:** Oh! Please be careful, Ms. Narcissus. You don't look too steady.

Themis strides into the bar with purpose.

**Themis:** This is a bigger crowd than I thought.

**Hermes:** Three hours, Themis. You're three hours late. I'm surprised you came at all.

**Themis:** My apologies, Hermes. But with the Aquatic Games beginning tomorrow, my duties must take priority.

She offers her answer in a calm, unapologetic tone before clapping her hands to quiet the room.

**Themis:** And on the subject of Games, I fear I have to caution you. The rules forbid contestants to mingle before the competition. So, I'm afraid your guests will need to call it a night.

Themis turns to the outsiders.

**Themis:** We may gather and speak again after the first event.

**Hermes:** Just-Just hold on a minute, Themis. I haven't even won a single round yet.

**Apollo:** Our guests didn't come here seeking card games, Hermes.

**Apollo:** I believe they're entitled to some well-deserved rest.

The messenger eyes the cards scattered across the bar, scratching his head through his baseball cap, still reluctant to let go of the game.

**Hermes:** So be it, my friends, like Diomedes and Glaucus exchanging armor and parting ways.

**Hermes:** I'll see you again here in Bar Helicon after the game. May you return victorious.

**Vertin:** Thank you, Hermes. We'll do our best.

**Themis:** See you on the field tomorrow, ladies.
