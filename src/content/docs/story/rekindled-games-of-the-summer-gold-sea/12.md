## Story

The Volcanic Vent

A thick and steamy fog begins to envelop the island as the volcano roars to life.

Every head turns up, every eye locking onto where the fog started.

**Vertin:** Lady Hedone ... The volcano ...

The race has already drained a great deal of energy.

Getting here was only possible with the help of friends.

**Hedone:** Vertin.

Hedone stares into the volcano's mouth, the molten rock inside matching the fiery glow in her eyes.

**Hedone:** You have many friends at your side, just as I once did.

**Vertin:** \*panting\* I don't understand. Apollo, Themis, Hermes, even Narcissus ... Aren't they your friends?

**Hedone:** A sovereign has no friends among her subjects.

Her answer leaves no room for argument.

**Hedone:** But that was not always so.

**Hedone:** In ancient days, my comrades stood beside me in the battle against Typhon. But one by one, they fell and left me alone.

**Vertin:** ...

**Vertin:** But you remember them. Their memory is in every step of your ritual. It's always been your way to honor them.

**Vertin:** The challenges you faced and the friends you lost are all part of these games.

**Vertin:** Typhon is awakening again.

**Vertin:** Lady Hedone, will you—

**Hedone:** Vertin.

**Hedone:** I envy you.

The words tumble out with a crushing weight.

**Hedone:** It's a rare thing, far too rare, to have friends that will stand beside you to the very end.

**Hedone:** I envy the bond you share with them, unreserved and unbroken.

**Hedone:** And much more than this, I envy that, win or lose, your story will be told through them, with them, together with raised cups and cheerful hearts.

**Vertin:** Your friends, your comrades back then ... Would you tell me what really happened to them?

**Hedone:** \*sigh\* Heh.

**Hedone:** I had convinced them to sail here. Before we embarked, they insisted on bringing all manner of things from our polis: the Golden Goblet, figs, olive branches ...

**Hedone:** They even insisted on taking some broken ostraka, relics of the lives we were giving up.

**Hedone:** I mocked them at the time. "Just remember to brew the wine," I told them. "That alone will suffice."

**Hedone:** But the seas turned rough and the voyage difficult, besetting us with storms and battles. It wasn't long before the first of them fell, and others, dear to us as kin, turned their sails toward other horizons.

**Hedone:** We weathered trial after trial, and weather us it did. The sharp sea winds smoothed away the marks of our homeland and our former bonds, until we were made into something new.

**Hedone:** But at last, the mighty volcano rose on the horizon to meet us.

**Hedone:** That was when we first beheld Typhon, the primal beast coiled within the crater's throat. It stirred the air into a wild tempest, hurling thunderbolts that turned the dark night into a flash of blinding noon.

**Hedone:** It was then that I made my bargain with the heart of the volcano: that I would do battle with the beast and, in return, it would make this land our home.

**Hedone:** But I was promised an even greater prize. The volcano would bestow upon the victor its own infinite power.

**Hedone:** Hedone, born hunter of the tides and champion of champions, would not refuse such a challenge. How could I?

**Hedone:** But I knew, even then, that victory here would be a challenge greater than threading the rings of a dozen axes.

**Hedone:** So, even as the gale raged about us, my friends and I sought the favor of the gods in our final drops of wine.

**Hedone:** We carried out our rituals, honoring the gods even as we were rocked by wind, storm, and swelling sea.

**Hedone:** The feast, the ritual, and the games ... It is no mystery to you, I'm sure. That evening set the pattern.

**Hedone:** But then, at Typhon's first roar, we cast aside our goblets.

**Hedone:** And though we knew that even the finest wine brewed on a foreign land could never truly taste like home, we decided, then and there, to claim ourselves a new one.

**Hedone:** For we embraced the journey—the change that had come upon us. And thus, we set ourselves to the task.

**Hedone:** We drew close to the island's shores.

**Hedone:** But the beast roared again, its pyroclastic lightning giving way to a shower of molten fire. Our ships were set ablaze.

**Hedone:** War was upon us. Victory, our only chance at survival. And as our ships burned, so did my chest, for I too was ignited with fury.

**Hedone:** Wielding the wind, Typhon churned the sea into a great vortex.

**Hedone:** So we slashed our burning sails and held them against the driving wind as wings to carry us beyond the range of the maelstrom.

**Hedone:** But many did not catch the wind. Their sails turned to ash in their hands, and more still were swallowed by the sea.

**Hedone:** Those who survived met the foot of the volcano.

**Hedone:** We ran to the cliffs and began our climb, scaling them as we would a city under siege.

**Hedone:** But Typhon, with the power of the volcano still in its grasp, erupted at our presence. We were met with rivers of fire and choking ash.

**Hedone:** Panic grew, and yet more of our numbers fell away.

**Hedone:** What last remained of us as we crested the volcano was caught betwixt the savage beast below and the ravaging tempest above.

**Hedone:** In the end, I was alone.

**Hedone:** I had witnessed death a thousand times or more, whether it came to me in battle or from the cruel hunger of the sea.

**Hedone:** But never had the silence been so ... catastrophic.

**Hedone:** With a volcanic rage burning in my heart, I slew Typhon.

**Hedone:** I gouged out its eye, and I twisted the arrow that had found its heart. Again and again, I twisted and churned until the beast was still.

**Hedone:** Its breath was gone. No more tempest, lightning, or fire. Night fell.

**Hedone:** The volcano slumbered. I had won.

**Hedone:** But I was alone in my triumph.

**Hedone:** I received what I had been promised. The infinite power of the volcano was mine, and so I left to gather what I could of my people.

**Hedone:** To bring them to their new home.

**Hedone:** There was another gift I received from the volcano's power: the blessing of reincarnation.

**Hedone:** Though I died thousands of years ago, here I stand, remade in new flesh, but burdened with the echoes of ancient memory.

**Hedone:** Treasure this life, Vertin. Keep the faces of your friends vivid in your mind, even as your paths diverge.

**Hedone:** Despite all these memories, try as I might, I can scarcely recall their faces anymore.

**Hedone:** I still find myself searching the fresco, wondering: are these figures truly them? It's just ...

**Hedone:** You must think me ridiculous.

She opens her hand and looks down at her palm.

**Hedone:** All that I have left of them is the very goblet I once mocked.

**Hedone:** I hate them. I hate them for their weakness as I hate them for their bravery. They've gone to Elysium, and I am left behind, victorious, immortal, and utterly alone.

**Hedone:** The power of the volcano has never been a "blessing." It is a curse, a power that forces me awake again and again to relive this wine-dark loneliness.

**Hedone:** Vertin.

The fire burns even brighter in her eyes.

**Hedone:** I cannot lose more comrades and forget more faces. That is why I will not call you friend, why you cannot join me in this battle.

**Hedone:** This part of the legend will not be repeated.

**Hedone:** The feast has ended. The Games are spent.

**Hedone:** The hunt must fall to me alone!

**Hedone:** I shall slay the beast once more!

**Vertin:** Hedone!

**Typhon:** —!

The terrifying beast wakes at last, stirring earth and fire around them.

**Hedone:** You wish to aid me? There is but one thing I ask of you.

**Hedone:** Leave.

**Hedone:** Tell the people not to panic.

**Hedone:** All will be over soon.

***

COMBAT:

***

Struggling desperately, Typhon flees into the volcano's depths.

**Hedone:** ARRGGHHH!

All reason gone, Hedone hurls herself down into the magma-lit depths.

**Vertin:** ...!

**Vertin:** It's still alive.

**Vertin:** The hunt hasn't finished.

A Certain Someone's Solo-Living Challenge Log

Foreword The King of Ithaca returned home with no one at his side. Another hero completed his twelve labors without a companion. Still others turned the tide of battle single-handedly ... A great many tales have been written by lone heroes, and thus I have decided to begin a challenge of my own: to see how far I can travel the road to heroism by myself. Challenges Dine alone - Complete Take a walk alone - Complete Drink alone - Complete. I know my limits. Practice archery alone - Complete. My aim is respectable. Hold a conversation alone - Complete. No one enjoys the subjects I raise more than I do. Compete alone - Complete. I need only keep breaking my own records. ... Conduct a prophecy ritual alone - Incomplete. This is a difficult one, as I am not a prophet. Sail the open sea alone - Incomplete. First, I need a ship. Build a ship alone - Incomplete. It appears I shall need a separate challenge plan for shipbuilding. ... Conclusion Although this challenge began with a sudden flash of inspiration and has been laughed at more than once (let them laugh—I don't care!), it has brought me a great deal of pleasure. More than that, I have made new friends along the way. They never make trouble and have kindly suggested additions to my plan (for which I thank them). Yet now that I have reached the conclusion, these days must naturally be drawing to an end. My old friends, who have been traveling beyond the island, have written to say they will return soon. With them beside me, I suppose this challenge will have to be put on hold for a while.
