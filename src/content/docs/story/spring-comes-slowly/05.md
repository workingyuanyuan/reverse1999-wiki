## Story

Apricot Tree, East Port

**Pip:** Your paper swallow is amazing!

**Paper Heron:** Is this really the East Port? It looks nothing like I remember.

**Paper Heron:** Hmm, it's quite different from the town too.

Frost still clings to the trees near the water, barring signs of life or greenery. Ahead, the river moves slowly, cutting its path through stretches of dry grass and sandbars.

Near the door of a tall, worn house, an old man with white hair and a beard stares at one of the dead trees.

**Pip:** I was just thinking the same thing. What do you ...

Suddenly, the mule lifts its head and brays. Then, without warning, it charges forward.

**Mule:** Whinny!

**Pip:** Hey, you! Don't be so impatient!

**???:** You're back? Didn't I tell you to accompany this nice young lady?

The mule stops beside the old man.

**Pip:** It seems more comfortable here with you.

**Pip:** I only want to do the right thing, sir. There's no need to give me anything in return.

**Elder:** No, no. What would an old man like me do with a mule anyhow?

**Elder:** You're a strong, good fellow, aren't you, boy? You'd be wasted with me, don't you think?

**Mule:** Whinny!

**Elder:** I know, I know. I'll miss you too. But this is for the best.

**Elder:** Once, many people passed through here. I made enough to feed you back then. But now, well, I'm sorry. I can't anymore.

He gently strokes the mule's ear and sighs.

**Elder:** Look at me, going on about a mule. What brings you back so soon?

**Pip:** You can rest assured, sir. Help has arrived. This is Paper Heron.

**Pip:** I may have lacked the skills to finish what I'd started, but I'm certain that she will be able to help!

Paper Heron smiles at the old man and glances thoughtfully at the dead apricot tree nearby.

**Paper Heron:** Hmm. Seems we've come to the right place.

**Elder:** Oh, don't trouble yourselves. The good days are long gone, and they're not coming back.

**Elder:** \*sigh\* Madam, I apologize for getting you involved in this nonsense.

**Pip:** It is not nonsense!

**Elder:** \*sigh\* There's a strong wind in the air. If you're to stay, please, come inside to talk.

Old House, East Port

**Elder:** It's not much, but you're welcome here. Allow me to make you some tea.

Bright light streams through the window, making the time-worn carvings difficult to see.

Inside, the furniture is fine but old, lending the room a solemn air.

**Pip:** Alright, while he's making tea, let's speak frankly.

**Paper Heron:** Frankly? About what exactly?

**Pip:** Heron ...

**Pip:** The old man doesn't understand what's going on. How can he? But we both know this place is suffering from Qi stagnation.

**Pip:** On the way here, did you notice anything unusual?

**Paper Heron:** Now that you mention it, yes. There were a few oddities on the road.

**Pip:** Such as?

**Paper Heron:** Let's see: I got woken up by a fight, had my cakes stolen by a mule, and spent most of the trip wandering around lost. Quite the peculiar turn of events, wouldn't you say?

**Pip:** Not that kind of "unusual." I'm talking about the Qi flow!

**Paper Heron:** Hmm. It is true that this was once a busy ferry crossing.

**Elder:** Oh my, few remember that old ferry now.

The old man hobbles over with a platter of coarse porcelain bowls filled to the brim with strong tea.

He sets them on the table, its lacquer worn and patchy but its surface clean.

**Elder:** This is a local blend. When the ferry thrived, even the people of Wu loved it!

**Elder:** It can't quite compare to the fancy teas folk prefer now. But it'll certainly quench your thirst.

**Paper Heron:** This will do nicely, really. I'm not picky.

Smiling, she takes a generous sip.

**Paper Heron:** Oh my, this tea is good! Better than most I've tried. Pip, you really must take a sip.

**Pip:** Me? I ... I tried it last time.

Pip holds her warm bowl in her hands, but her focus remains anywhere else.

She hesitates, then leans forward, looking at her companion across from her.

**Pip:** I know you know more than you're letting on. Please just tell me the truth.

**Pip:** Do you think Chun Town went downhill because there's something wrong with the Qi here?

Outside the window, a dead leaf falls in silence.

**Paper Heron:** No.

**Pip:** No?

**Paper Heron:** The Qi here is blocked, that much is certain. But you can't pin a whole town's bad luck on just that.

**Paper Heron:** As I said before, there could be many reasons for what's happening here. It could be the shifting of trade roads or simply a turning of the human heart.

**Paper Heron:** In the end, everything has its season.

**Paper Heron:** Not everything comes from magic and sorcery, nor can they be fixed by them.

**Elder:** She's right.

**Elder:** I don't understand Qi like the two of you seem to.

**Elder:** But the new trade routes that opened all go through Zhu County, so the ferry and town fell silent. That's just how things are.

**Elder:** Please excuse her, madam. She's young, and her heart is in the right place. She believes everything must have a reason, a solution.

**Pip:** So it's not the Qi?

**Paper Heron:** I never said it wasn't. Stagnant Qi flows do pose a bit of a problem.

Paper Heron pauses for a moment in thought before changing the topic of conversation to a more optimistic motif.

**Paper Heron:** "Spring comes upon a withered tree." True, I cannot bring spring back. But making an old tree bloom again? That I can do.

**Elder:** What?!

**Elder:** It used to be a local custom to plant trees here, back when the ferry still ran.

**Elder:** For those returning, the sight of our tree standing guard was how we knew we were home, even before the ferry docked.

**Elder:** My family has lived by the ferry for generations. This apricot tree was planted by my ancestors. It's a reminder of home. Many generations have lived under its shade.

**Elder:** My grandfather stood under this tree when he sent my father off to work on the water. My father stood under it to see me push off for the first time. And every time we returned home, the tree was the first thing we saw.

**Elder:** And now the ferry is dead. Sometimes I think that the ferry, this tree, and I are all too old.

**Elder:** If it could bloom again, that would grant this old man his dearest wish.

Paper Heron tilts her head in thought, a wry smile forming on her lips.

With a small flourish, she conjures a red paper-cut Mei blossom. It drifts down to settle on the worn table.
