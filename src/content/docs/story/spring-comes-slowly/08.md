## Story

**Elder:** Look! The flowers! They're really blooming!

**Elder:** This calls for a celebration! I'll head into town and buy some wine for us, the real good stuff! You just wait right here!

**Pip:** What? Wait! Please, there's no need to go to all that trouble.

**Pip:** He waited all this time for it to bloom, only to take off the moment it does. Can you believe it?

**Paper Heron:** So what? We have nothing but time ahead.

**Pip:** Really? But ...

Pip lowers her voice to just above a whisper.

**Pip:** But he's nearly 80 years old.

**Paper Heron:** Hmm? Did you say something?

**Pip:** Nothing, never mind. I think the sun will set soon.

**Paper Heron:** You're right. We should rest while it's still shining.

Paper Heron stretches out on the revivified grass, and Pip takes a seat beside her.

**Pip:** Where will you go after this?

**Paper Heron:** I'm not sure. I've left matters in Huanzhou with a friend, so perhaps it's time I explored somewhere new.

**Paper Heron:** And you?

**Pip:** Maybe I'll go home. I still have a lot to learn.

**Pip:** Actually, I slipped away without telling anyone.

**Paper Heron:** I suspected as much. You're too young to be out here alone. Your family must be worried about you.

Soft blades of grass press against Paper Heron's fingertips, a sign that they're still growing.

**Pip:** I have another question.

**Paper Heron:** Go ahead.

**Pip:** Have you lived a long time?

**Pip:** The things you mentioned on the way here, the East Port being busy once—they're all from the past.

**Pip:** And the story of the immortal in Huanzhou dates back nearly two hundred years.

**Pip:** ...

**Pip:** I'm sorry. It's alright if you don't want to talk about it. Just forget I asked.

The wind brushes against Paper Heron's hair.

**Paper Heron:** That's quite alright. Yes, it's true. Not much gets past you.

**Paper Heron:** But I didn't intend to. It's hard to explain. Just think of me like the old man's tree. I, too, have suffered a long illness.

**Pip:** What does it feel like to live for so long?

**Paper Heron:** Hmm. Well ...

**Paper Heron:** It doesn't feel so special. When you're ill for a long time, you almost forget what it's like to be well.

**Pip:** Why call it an illness? Many people dream of living long lives. Even in stories, that's what the Daoists sought.

**Paper Heron:** You shouldn't believe everything you read in stories!

**Paper Heron:** And from what books, pray tell, did you find this information?

**Pip:** Legend of the Getian, The White Horse Immortal: A Tale of Medicine, and Three Years of Strange Dreams: A Collection.

**Paper Heron:** So Legend of the Getian is still popular, even after two hundred years, eh? I remember a story about a mortal seeking the Way from the Getian clan. He descends the mountain, only to discover that a thousand years have passed.

**Paper Heron:** Those stories talk of immortals desiring the mortal world or mortals yearning for the immortal realm. At their core, they describe what one cannot attain.

**Paper Heron:** ...

**Paper Heron:** My senior sister once said that, as a person ages, time seems to speed up.

**Paper Heron:** I didn't argue then, but deep down, I disagreed.

**Paper Heron:** When you're young, the blooming and fading of flowers marks a season. That time doesn't change, even decades later. Flowers still bloom. Seasons still pass. Everything feels just as it did.

**Paper Heron:** But now, I see she was right.

**Paper Heron:** The first ten years feel slow. But the next ten are easier to endure.

**Paper Heron:** After that, the days just pass, one after another.

Pip gazes at the sky.

**Pip:** I ... I don't quite understand.

**Pip:** I'll be seventeen soon, so for me, this is the seventeenth spring.

**Pip:** Paper Heron, how many springs have you seen?

**Paper Heron:** ...

**Paper Heron:** I don't remember. You certainly ask tricky questions.

**Mule:** Bray—

Not far away, the mule lifts its head, braying and running off as if it spots something.

**Pip:** I'll go get it!

**Paper Heron:** No need to rush, eh? It won't get far.

**Pip:** But ...

**Paper Heron:** It likely knows this area better than us. It'll come back if it wishes it.

**Pip:** No, I made a promise. I have to keep it.

**Paper Heron:** What promise?

**Pip:** When we met today, I said I would help you anytime you needed it.

**Pip:** I wasn't sure how and when I would give it, but, at the very least, I can fetch your mule.

**Paper Heron:** My mule?

She looks up, surprised, propping herself on her elbow to better see Pip.

**Pip:** Yes! The mule should go to the one who fixed the Qi flow, no, to the one who saved the tree.

**Pip:** So it belongs to you now!

**Paper Heron:** Well, I suppose it'll give me a companion on my travels.

**Pip:** He's a slow thing, but it's not as though you're in a hurry.

The sound of the mule's steps grows quieter with each passing second.

**Pip:** I should go check on it. You stay here, alright? I'll be right back.

**Paper Heron:** Sure. You'd better be quick.

Paper Heron lays her head back down.

The rustling of the grass quiets, and the rays of sunlight feel warm on her skin. It's not long before she closes her eyes and drifts off to sleep.

**Paper Heron:** Seventeen ...

**Paper Heron:** The year I went down the mountain with Finch, I was seventeen then too, I think.

**Paper Heron:** ...

A soft rustle from the bush rouses her attention.

**Mule:** Bray.

**Paper Heron:** Hmm? You're back? Where's Pip?

She pats the mule's neck affectionately.

The mule ignores her instead, shaking its ears and walking over to the tree to munch on the new grass.

**Paper Heron:** Good timing. You can help me think ...

**Paper Heron:** How many springs have I seen?

...

How many springs have I seen?

The same spring breeze blows, the same vitalized grass sways, and the mule gives no answer.

Is it two hundred springs?

Twenty?

One?

...

Or has she lost all her springs?

**Pearl Finch:** Master, I thought long and hard on the matter yesterday. Please don't be too harsh with her.

**Pearl Finch:** We will all leave one day.

**Pearl Finch:** And when she does, she will walk a long, lonely road.

**Pearl Finch:** ...

**Pearl Finch:** Don't make her loneliness any heavier to bear.
